Gymnastics skills checklist

Use this in April and any shape/travel day. Tick I (introduced), P (practising), or C (consistent). One student per printed page, or watch 6–8 students per class.

  • Control and a still ending beat speed.
  • Yellow rows need a taught progression and a mat. Skip them if you have not taught the skill.
  • No neck-loading. Rocks stay on the shoulder-blades. Headstand and a diving forward roll are Grade 5–6 only, and only if you are trained.
  • Partner skills need consent. A student may say no.

How this fits April

Week 1: shapes and locomotor. Week 2: balances. Week 3: log, egg, rock — then taught rolls only. Week 4: a 20–30 second routine with a still ending. Use the sample rubric for stillness, control, and ending.

The sequences below follow the old Alberta educational-gymnastics strand (landings, locomotion, balances, rotations). They are gym-ready summaries, not copies of those resource pages. Old A/B/C/D codes map to PEW Movement Skill Development, Safety, and Character.

Landing cues (every jump)

  • Toes, then heels. Feet apart. Knees bend slowly.
  • Arms out. Hold still for a count of three. Then stand tall.
  • Ask: loud or quiet? Quiet is the goal.
  • Two-to-two, two-to-one, one-to-two. Forward, back, side.
  • Benches and boxes need mats on all landing sides. One jumper at a time.

Apparatus stations

One group per station. Mats on every landing. Waiting students do a floor task (shape, log, stork).

  • Bench. Walk forward, side, back. Jump off and stick 3. ¼ or ½ turn for 4–6.
  • Low beam or tape line. Same walks. Step off to a mat — do not jump a high beam.
  • Springboard. Two-foot jump to a mat. Shape in the air only if the landing is quiet.
  • Climbing frame. Three points of contact. Pathways: up, side, diagonal. Two-foot land.
  • Rope. Climb down, never slide. Short hangs first.
  • Box or stacked mats. Jump on, walk, jump off. No diving rolls off the box.

Apparatus signals (warm-up or closer): freeze — around — under — over — through — on/off — hang — across. Control, not speed.

Taught-roll reminder

If you teach a forward or backward roll: weight on the hands, chin to chest, roll on the shoulder — not the head. Stop if a student is stacking on the neck. Log, egg, and rock still count as rotations for report-card evidence.
