Dodgeball games

Twenty stay-in versions for a large elementary class. Best in October (send/receive) and January (invasion). This is the only unit that uses dodgeballs. Hits stay below the waist. A hit is a stretch, a switch, or a jail-catch — never sitting the period out. Soccer, football, basketball, hockey, volleyball, and T-ball use the real ball.

Games

    House rules

    • Soft playground or dodgeballs. No rubber balls at heads.
    • Throw or roll below the waist. A high ball is dead.
    • A catch is a save. The thrower stretches 10 seconds, then stays in.
    • No benches. Hoops, tape, and cones only.
    • Grades 1–2 roll or underhand. Grades 3–6 may add a bounce-throw.

    Alberta PEW (K–6) — what these games practise

    Organizing ideas: Movement Skill Development · Safety · Healthy Relationships · Active Living. Pick one or two look-fors per class.

    • Send, receive, retain — opposite-foot step, eyes on the target, two-hand catch, roll or bounce to keep it low.
    • Tactics — pass before a throw, move after you send, protect a teammate, use space.
    • Safety — dodgeballs, below the waist, freeze on the whistle, no chasing a person down.
    • Fair play and relationships — a hit is not shame; help a medic or jail catch; accept a switch of teams.
    • Active living — keep moving for the 16–25 minute game block.
    October send/receive · January invasion · below the waist · no elimination.
