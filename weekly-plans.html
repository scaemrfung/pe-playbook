Old Grey Cat

Warm-up

A chant-and-chase circle game: the birds circle the cat and chant, then run for the trees (safe lines) while the cat chases; the first bird tagged becomes the new cat

Equipment: None. Optional: 8–12 cones to mark two safe lines (the “trees”); 1 pinny or scarf for each cat.

Setup: Use the centre circle (or mark one with cones). Mark a safe line (“the trees”) across each end of the gym with cones or use existing floor lines. Keep each safe line at least 3 m in from the end wall and the stage, so runners have room to slow down. Teach the chant before the first round.

How to play:
  1. The birds hold hands in a big circle. One player stands in the middle. That player is the old grey cat (give them a pinny or scarf).
  2. The birds walk around the cat in one direction and chant together: “We are weak little birds way up in a tree, you're an old grey cat and you can't catch me!”
  3. On the last word, the birds let go of hands and run to either safe line (the trees). The cat chases.
  4. Birds behind a safe line are safe. The cat tags with two fingers on the back or shoulder.
  5. The first bird tagged becomes the new cat. The old cat joins the circle as a bird.
  6. If nobody is tagged, the teacher picks a new cat.
  7. Everyone walks back to the circle, joins hands, and the next round starts.
This week:
  • Gr 1–2 Old Grey Cat (NEW, PhysEdGames).
  • Gr 3–4 Old Grey Cat (NEW, PhysEdGames) with two cats; change the circle travel each round (skip, gallop, side-slide).

Hockey hurdle dribble

Game

Students push the ball under or through a wavy line of low cones with the blade on the floor.

Equipment: Low cones as ‘hurdles,’ stick, hockey ball.

Setup: Wavy line of low cones. Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How to play:
  1. One runner at a time. Next player starts behind the home cone and goes on a high-five — not a shout from halfway.
  2. Push the ball under or through the cones. Blade down. No slapshot.
  3. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  4. Play Hockey hurdle dribble in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

I See, I See, What Do You See?

Warm-up

Call and response warm-up: "I see, I see." "What do you see?" The teacher names a scene and everyone acts it out in their own space until the next call

Equipment: None (music optional)

Setup: Students spread out in the gym or a cleared classroom with arms-length space all around. Stand where everyone can see you.

How to play:
  1. Teacher: "I see, I see." Class: "What do you see?"
  2. The teacher names a scene. For example: "I see children on a basketball court playing basketball."
  3. Everyone acts out the scene in their own space: dribbling, shooting, defending, jogging on the spot.
  4. After 15–20 seconds, freeze and repeat the call with a new scene.
  5. Keep going until the class is warmed up (6–10 scenes). Finish with a calm scene.

Mooshake

Warm-up

Pass a stuffed cow (or soft ball) around the circle without a drop; every clean lap, everyone steps back; drop it and everyone yells “Moo-shake!”, wiggles, steps back in and starts again

Equipment: A stuffed cow (or a soft ball) for each circle

Setup: Students stand in a circle, a little more than arm's length apart.

How to play:

Rope Hoop Scoop

Warm-up

Partners share two skipping ropes to scoop a hoop off the floor and carry it home without touching it with hands or feet; if it drops, stop and scoop again

Equipment: 24 skipping ropes (2 per pair); 24–36 hoops (mixed colours if you have them); 12 cones (one home cone per pair); optional colour-to-points chart (for example red 1, blue 2, yellow 3)

Setup: Place 12 home cones around the outside of the gym, at least 2 m from the walls and about 3 m apart. Each pair starts at its cone with two skipping ropes. Scatter the hoops flat on the floor in the middle of the gym, spaced so pairs can reach them from different sides.

How to play:
  1. Each partner holds one end of each rope, so the two ropes run side by side between you, like a stretcher.
  2. On "Go", walk to any hoop on the floor.
  3. Slide your ropes under the hoop without using hands or feet on the hoop. Lift it together.
  4. Carry the hoop home to your cone and lower it next to the cone. That hoop is yours.
  5. If the hoop falls, stop where you are, slide the ropes under it again, and keep going. Nobody restarts from home.
  6. Go back for another hoop. The round ends when the last hoop on the floor has been collected.
  7. Count your hoops (or add up the colour points). Then play the "put back" round: carry your hoops back to the middle the same way.
This week:
  • Gr 3–4 Rope Hoop Scoop (NEW, PhysEdGames) — pairs lift hoops with two ropes, no hands.

Spin the Pin Tag & Cone Flip Mixer

Warm-up

Half the class plays Cone Flip Mixer in pairs.

Equipment: 2 bowling pins (or plastic bottles); 2 hoops; 2 short foam noodle tag sticks; 8–10 small cones for cone flip (one per pair, plus spares); a row of upright gym mats or a volleyball net to divide the gym; 8–12 boundary cones; optional tape X's for younger grades

Setup: Divide the gym in half with standing mats or the volleyball net. Cone-flip side: scatter pairs with one small cone each, well spaced. Tag side: cone two separate tag zones (each about the size of a volleyball half-court), at least 2 m from the walls and stage. Put one hoop with a pin lying in it in the centre of each zone, with one noodle beside each hoop. Start with 4 students in each tag zone (8 total) and the other 16 at cone flip (8 pairs).

How to play:
  1. Cone flip side: partners take turns flipping their cone (toss it with one flick so it spins). The first player to land it standing upright twice wins.
  2. The winner walks to the tag side and joins the tag zone that needs a player (the teacher points). The other player finds a new cone-flip partner.
  3. Tag side: four players stand around the hoop. The newest player spins the pin.
  4. When the pin stops, the player it points to is the tagger. They pick up the noodle while everyone else moves away inside the zone.
  5. The tagger has 5 slow seconds to make a soft noodle tag on the back or shoulder. The group counts aloud: "Five, four, three, two, one."
  6. If the tagger makes a tag, the tagged player goes to the cone-flip side. If the tagger misses, the tagger goes to the cone-flip side instead.
  7. The noodle goes back beside the hoop. The three who stay wait at the hoop for the next player from cone flip, who spins the pin.
  8. Both sides run at the same time, nonstop.
This week:
  • Gr 3–4 Spin the Pin Tag & Cone Flip Mixer (NEW, Front Range PE), no sticks — full game, jog-tag, two upright cone flips move you to the tag side.
  • Gr 5–6 Spin the Pin Tag & Cone Flip Mixer (NEW, Front Range PE) — full speed inside the zone, one “freeze” fake per turn; a student explains the rotation to the class.

Backyard Dog Tag

Warm-up

Each dog guards a small yard while visitors try to sneak in, and steal the bone if you use one, without being tagged.

Zone Tag

Warm-up

Cones split the space into four zones with one tagger locked into each; everyone else runs freely across the zones; a tag swaps the tagger and runner; last taggers do a short fitness challenge

Equipment: 8–12 cones or floor markers to mark four zones; pinnies for the four taggers (optional); a whistle or music to start and stop

Setup: Use cones or lines to cut the playing area into four zones (think four quarters of a gym floor or field). Pick four taggers and send one to stand in each zone. Everyone else scatters anywhere in the space.

How to play:
  1. On the signal, the runners move around freely and may run through any zone.
  2. Each tagger must stay inside their own zone. They can lean and reach, but they may not step across a cone line to chase.
  3. When a tagger gently tags a runner, they swap on the spot: the runner becomes the tagger for that zone, and the old tagger is now a runner and can go anywhere.
  4. Keep playing until the time is up. Freeze on the whistle.
  5. At the end, the four players who are taggers lead a short fitness challenge for the group (for example 10 push-ups, squats, or jumping jacks). Then pick four new taggers and go again.

Warm-up

Players pull a bib tail with two fingers, then hand it back so the game keeps going.

Setup: Two halves. Each team names 2–3 end-zone catchers who stay in their end. Pinnies on one team. Show the two attacking directions before GO. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How to play:
  1. Players in their half roll balls to their own catchers.
  2. The other team tries to intercept rolls.
  3. A clean catch in the end zone scores.
  4. Play 4–6 minute periods. Extra players are bumpers, walls, or the next wave — they stay in a job.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

End Wall Soccer

Each team’s goal is the whole end wall, so the game works with a large group.

Equipment: foam soccer ball, pinnies

Setup: Make two teams (pinnies on one side). Play the full gym or a large field. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How to play:
  1. Each team’s GOAL is the entire end wall / end line — no small net required. That is what makes this work with a crowd.
  2. Play soccer rules with a foam soccer ball: feet only. Decide in advance whether a goalie is allowed to use hands.
  3. A goal counts when the ball hits the opponent’s end wall or fully crosses the end line (choose one and stick to it).
  4. Restart at centre after each goal. Add a second foam ball if numbers are very high so more players get touches.
  5. Play 4–6 minute periods and rotate roles.
  6. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

The Food Chain

RPS food-chain levels; losers stay in

Equipment: None

Setup: Everyone starts at the bottom level (e.g. egg). Teach the levels and an action for each: egg → chicken → dinosaur → human (or class choice).

How to play:
  1. Walk around doing your level’s action and find someone at the same level.
  2. Play rock–paper–scissors. The winner moves up a level; the loser moves down (or stays at egg).
  3. Top level players keep playing other top-level players to stay up.
  4. Play 3–5 minutes. No one is out — everyone keeps finding partners.
