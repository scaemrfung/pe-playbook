Sample assessment rubric

Use this for any month. Swap the skill cues row for the look-fors on that month’s page. Four levels match typical Alberta elementary language. You do not need to mark every student every class — pick 6–8 students per period, or one criterion per week.

Report on the PEW learning outcome, not on who won. Fitness times stay private and are not part of this rubric.

1. Ongoing class rubric

Four criteria you can use all year: skill, participation, safety, and fair play.

Grades 1–6 PE — ongoing observation
Criterion Beginning Developing Meeting Extending
Movement skill
Use this month’s cues		 Needs a demo beside them. Cues are missing or unsafe. Shows the cue with a prompt. Form comes and goes. Shows the month’s cue most of the time in practice and in the game. Uses the skill in a changing game. Can give a classmate one useful cue.
Active living / participation Enters late or sits out when not required. Little continuous movement. Joins when invited. Needs a reminder to keep moving after a tag or a drop. Enters on the first call. Tries the task. Re-enters after hospital / stretch / 10-count. Stays in the activity, adjusts effort, and helps a peer stay in too.
Safety Needs stop-and-reteach (space, tag, implement). Follows a rule after a reminder (soft tag, foam, freeze). Moves in space, tags softly, uses equipment as taught. Looks after others’ space. Reminds a peer kindly. Chooses a safer send.
Relationships / fair play Public picking, blame after a drop, or refusal to take a tag. Works with an assigned partner when prompted. Sometimes argues a call. Accepts teacher-formed teams, a tag, and a drop. Uses kind language. Unfreezes others, offers a helper job, or teaches a rule in 60 seconds.

Grades 1–2. Score from what you see in play, not from a spoken explanation. A nod, a freeze, or a high-five counts.
Grades 3–4. Expect the cue plus a simple tactic (pass before a shot, call “mine”).
Grades 5–6. Expect the cue in a small-sided game and occasional peer leadership.

2. Skill snapshot — send and receive (October / November)

One example of swapping in month-specific cues. Print this when you are on throw, catch, or kick.

Throw / catch / kick — look-fors
Cue Beginning Developing Meeting Extending
Ready to receive Back is to the sender. Hands at sides. Looks at the sender after a prompt. Eyes on the sender, body behind the ball or beanbag. Moves feet to the ball before it arrives.
Underhand / Throw Level 1 Same-foot step or no step. Ball is pushed. Opposite foot sometimes. Little follow-through. Opposite-foot step, eyes on target, follow-through. Repeats the cue to a hoop; G5–6 try the other hand.
Kick / trap (foam) Runs through the ball. No plant foot. Plant foot is late. Trap bounces away. Plant beside the ball; trap stays near the body. Chooses inside-foot pass for accuracy; weaker-foot try.
Catch Turns away or traps with the body only. Hands are late. Needs a short toss. Ready hands (thumbs or pinkies together as taught). Soft catch. Calls “mine.” Catches on the move.

3. Quick tick sheet (one class)

Copy names down the side. Mark only the students you are watching today. One letter is enough: B D M E.

Student Skill cue Participation Safety Fair play Note
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

B = Beginning   D = Developing   M = Meeting   E = Extending. Leave a cell blank if you did not see it today.

Comment stems

  • Meeting: “Uses the opposite-foot step when sending to a hoop and re-enters after a tag without being asked.”
  • Developing: “Shows the freeze and soft tag after a reminder. Next step is to keep moving between signals.”
  • Extending: “Calls ‘mine’ before the catch and offers a high-five unfreeze without taking over the game.”
  • Do not write: times, ranks, “best in class,” or “can’t keep up.”
