Sample assessment rubric

Use this for any month. Swap the Movement skill look-fors for that month’s cues. Levels match the school achievement standards (printed sheet) — not older Beginning / Extending labels. You do not need to mark every student every class — pick 6–8 students per period, or one criterion per week.

K–2: Not Yet · Meeting · Mastering (tick NY · M · Ma).   Grades 3–6: Not Yet · Minimally Meeting · Solidifying · Mastering (tick NY · Min · S · Ma).

Below Standards = Not Yet (+ Minimally Meeting on 3–6). Meeting Standards = Meeting/Solidifying + Mastering. Mastering = grade-level transfer, explain, and intention — not extra volume or “beyond grade.” Score PE outcome achievement, not behaviour-as-mark. Fitness times stay private and are not part of this rubric.

1. Ongoing class rubric

Four criteria you can use all year: skill, active living, safety, and fair play. Choose the grade-band table that matches the class.

Grades K–2 PE — ongoing observation (3 levels)
Criterion Not Yet Meeting Mastering
Movement skill
This month’s cues		 Cannot yet complete the taught cue successfully; still on building-block form (demo beside them, cues missing or unsafe). Shows the month’s cue most of the time in practice and in the game. Uses the skill in a changing game with intention; can show or explain one useful cue to a classmate.
Active living Does not yet sustain entry into the activity; little continuous movement toward the grade expectation. Enters on the first call, tries the task, and re-enters after hospital / stretch / 10-count. Stays in the activity, adjusts effort for the task, and helps a peer stay active too.
Safety
PE outcome: rules & signals		 Does not yet follow taught space, tag, or equipment expectations successfully. Moves in space, tags softly, and uses equipment as taught. Looks after others’ space, reminds a peer kindly, and chooses a safer send when options exist.
Fair play / relationships
PE outcome		 Does not yet show fair-play expectations in play (e.g. public picking, blame after a drop, refusal to take a tag). Accepts teacher-formed teams, a tag, and a drop; uses kind language. Unfreezes others, offers a helper job, or teaches a rule in about 60 seconds.

K–2 note: Score from what you see in play. A nod, freeze, or high-five counts as evidence of understanding.

Grades 3–6 PE — ongoing observation (4 levels)
Criterion Not Yet Minimally Meeting Solidifying Mastering
Movement skill
This month’s cues		 Cannot yet complete the taught cue successfully; foundational form still in progress. Imitates the cue; form is inconsistent or needs a model nearby. Shows the cue efficiently in practice and games; understands the underlying idea; few errors on harder parts. Transfers the skill in changing or unfamiliar game contexts; can clearly explain one cue to others.
Active living Does not yet meet expectations for entering and sustaining activity. Joins when invited; basic participation on simpler stretches of the lesson. Enters on the first call, tries the task, re-enters after hospital / stretch / 10-count. Adjusts effort with intention and helps peers sustain activity.
Safety
PE outcome		 Does not yet apply taught safety procedures successfully. Follows a safety rule after a model or reminder; basic compliance on simpler situations. Moves in space, soft tag, equipment as taught, with few errors on complex gym moments. Protects others’ space and makes intentional safer choices; can explain why a rule matters.
Fair play / teamwork
PE outcome		 Does not yet meet fair-play / teamwork expectations in activity. Works with an assigned partner when the structure is clear; basic fair play on simpler tasks. Accepts teams, tags, and drops; kind language; supports participation. Unfreezes others, offers helper roles, or teaches a rule; transfers teamwork into new game formats.

Grades 3–4: Expect the cue plus a simple tactic (pass before a shot, call “mine”).
Grades 5–6: Expect the cue in small-sided play and occasional peer leadership (still grade-level Mastering, not extra work).

2. Skill snapshot — send and receive (October / November)

One example of swapping in month-specific cues. Print this when you are on throw, catch, or kick.

Grades K–2 — throw / catch / kick look-fors
Cue Not Yet Meeting Mastering
Ready to receive Back to sender; hands at sides; cannot yet show ready position successfully. Eyes on sender; body behind the ball or beanbag. Moves feet to the ball before it arrives.
Underhand / Throw Level 1 Same-foot step or no step; ball pushed; cue not yet successful. Opposite-foot step, eyes on target, follow-through most of the time. Repeats the cue to a hoop with intention; can show the step to a peer.
Kick / trap (foam) Runs through the ball; no plant foot; trap not yet successful. Plant beside the ball; trap stays near the body. Chooses inside-foot pass for accuracy when the task allows.
Catch Turns away or body-trap only; catch not yet successful. Ready hands as taught; soft catch most of the time. Calls for the ball; catches on the move in a simple game.
Grades 3–6 — throw / catch / kick look-fors
Cue Not Yet Minimally Meeting Solidifying Mastering
Ready to receive Cannot yet show ready position successfully. Looks at sender after a model; body position inconsistent. Eyes on sender; body behind the ball most of the time. Moves feet to the ball before it arrives in changing play.
Underhand / Throw Level 1 Cue not yet successful (no step / push throw). Opposite foot sometimes; little follow-through. Opposite-foot step, eyes on target, follow-through efficiently. Adjusts for accuracy; G5–6 try other hand as a grade-level variation (not extra volume).
Kick / trap (foam) No plant; trap not yet successful. Plant late; trap often bounces away. Plant beside ball; trap stays near body. Chooses inside-foot pass for accuracy; weaker-foot try when appropriate.
Catch Catch not yet successful. Hands late; needs short toss. Ready hands; soft catch. Calls “mine”; catches on the move.

Comment stems

Use school level names. Keep comments about the outcome, not rankings or times.

  • Not Yet: “At this point, [Name] is still building the [cue/skill] and is not yet completing it successfully in class activities. Next step: …”
  • Minimally Meeting (Grades 3–6): “[Name] shows a basic attempt at [cue] and can imitate the technique when it is modelled. Working on consistency on the more complex parts of the task.”
  • Meeting (K–2) / Solidifying (3–6): “[Name] uses [cue] efficiently in practice and games and understands the idea behind it. Minor errors show up mainly on harder tasks.”
  • Mastering: “[Name] applies [cue] with intention in changing games and can explain or show the skill to others. Assessed on current grade-level expectations (not extra work).”
  • Avoid: times, ranks, “best in class,” “can’t keep up,” or grading on behaviour/support alone.
