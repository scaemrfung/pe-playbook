Indigenous games

Classroom versions of games shared in Indigenous Games for Children (HIGH FIVE / Parks and Recreation Ontario), from First Nations, Métis, and Inuit communities across Canada. Play them as PE — and as a chance to name the Nation and the skill the game was meant to teach.

Ask first. Some communities play these for fun. Others treat the same activity as ceremony. Before you teach a game, speak with an Elder or knowledge keeper in your community. In the Edmonton / Sherwood Park area that often means Treaty 6 First Nations, Métis Region 4, and Inuit families in your school. If they say a game is not for the gym, do not play it.

  • Name the Nation on the card. Do not invent a “generic Native game.”
  • Do not dress students in costumes, headdresses, or face paint to play.
  • Soft tags. No elimination. Fitness records stay private.
  • A talking-circle close is often a better cool-down than a score.
