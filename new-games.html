1. Sinkerball

Cooperative & circus

New this week

Grades: 2–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: 6 scooter boards; 6 plastic bowling pins from bowling pin sets; 6 skipping ropes; Softex or Gator skin foam balls; saucer pylons. No tape or wooden pins needed.

Pairs walk a scooter carrying a balanced plastic pin between safe end lines. Opponents roll foam balls from side zones to knock the pin down. A fallen pin means a new helping role, not elimination.

Suggested month: November · also fits January · Slot: 16–30 · 6 tugboat pairs and 12 rollers for 24 · 3–4 minute rounds · 15–20 minutes

Set-up: Mark two turn lines at least 2 m from walls and the stage, with separate outward and return lanes. Mark side rolling zones and a protected equipment parking area. Each of six pairs has a pin on a scooter: one puller, one spotter. The teacher secures a short rope to a suitable scooter handhold, checks the knot and wheels, and tests the pull before play. Never improvise an unsafe attachment. Practise walking the route without balls first.

How we play

  1. Pullers walk forward, eyes up, keeping the pin balanced on the scooter. Nobody rides or stands on a scooter. Spotters walk beside, clear of wheels and rope.
  2. Rollers stay in their marked side zones and roll foam balls at pins, never at people. No airborne throws or kicks (our safety adaptation).
  3. Score one point when the whole scooter crosses the far turn line with its pin upright. Walk around the turning cone into the return lane. Do not touch a wall.
  4. If a pin falls, stop that scooter. Call “Freeze”; all balls and scooters stop. The pair parks its scooter, rope and pin outside the playing area before play restarts. Never abandon gear in a lane.
  5. One member of that pair may help guard a remaining pin from beside the route; the other scores, then swap. Maximum one guard per cargo, with no contact, diving or surrounding the scooter.
  6. Retrieve loose balls only during teacher-called freezes. After 3–4 minutes or when all pins fall, change tugboats and rollers. Swap puller and spotter halfway through the next round.

If this happens

  • A ball stops in a travel lane: freeze and clear it before restarting.
  • The rope catches or a pin repeatedly falls: stop and shorten the route; the teacher checks equipment.
  • Traffic crowds a turn: reduce to four cargo pairs and rotate others through scoring or rolling.

How a round ends. After 3–4 minutes or when all pins fall. Switch teams, then swap pair jobs. Everyone returns next round.

Cues

  • Walk forward. Smooth pull.
  • Roll at the pin, not the person.
  • Cross the cone line; never touch the wall.
  • Fallen pin: freeze, park, then help.

Variations

  • Grade 2: Shorter route, four cargo pairs and fewer balls. Roll only.
  • Grades 3–4: Six cargo pairs; score clean crossings of the turn line.
  • Grades 5–6: Plan who pulls, spots and guards. Keep walk-only travel and roll-only sending.

Teaching tips

  • Practise balancing a pin on a stationary scooter, then walking it without opponents. Ask: “What helped the pin stay upright?”
  • With about 60: use two waves of about 30, with 6–8 cargo pairs per wave. Use a separate supervised coaching or stretching area for the waiting wave. Do not create 32 scooters of traffic.
  • Support: offer rolling from a fixed accessible spot or scoring, then rotate. Adjust travel distance and follow ISP supports in the class binder.
  • Before teaching: check the class medical alert list and ISP supports in the class binder. Demonstrate the stop signal and follow school policy and SPHEReS guidance.

Grades 1–2. Grade 2: shorten the route, four cargo pairs and fewer balls.
Grades 3–4. Smooth pulls and accurate rolls; count clean crossings.
Grades 5–6. Plan roles, then explain which change helped the cargo stay balanced.

Safety. Our safety adaptation: walk-only, roll-only, plastic pins, cone turn lines and freeze-to-park. No riding, wall touches, kicks or airborne throws. No hands near wheels; no rope loops around any body part. Tie long hair and check footwear. Keep ropes short and clear of feet. No grabbing scooters or other players. Maximum one standing guard beside a cargo. “Freeze”: stop scooters, hold balls still, eyes on the teacher. Follow school equipment guidance and SPHEReS; do not run if attachment or space cannot be made safe.

2. Birds in a Nest

Locomotor & listen

New this week

Grades: K–4 · Best fit: Grades 1–2, also good for 3–4

Equipment: 1 hula hoop per student (24 for 24); optional floor markers for accessible nests. Remove 2–4 hoops for the final part.

Agree on a partner switch, first with words and then with signs. Walk to the new hoop nest. In the final part, a few hawks claim empty nests and everyone keeps changing roles.

Suggested month: November · also fits January · Slot: 16–30 · Up to 24 per play area · three 8-minute parts · about 25–30 minutes with demonstration

Set-up: Lay one hoop per student flat, with at least 2 m between nests and from walls or the stage. Leave clear walking paths. Reduce group size if that spacing does not fit. Model an agreed switch and calm arrival before starting.

How we play

  1. Part 1 (8 minutes): Find a partner using a look, wave or spoken invitation: “Can we switch?” Both agree before leaving their nests. Eye contact is optional.
  2. Walk to swap nests. Keep arms comfortably close and use a clear route around other hoops. No running or jumping into a nest.
  3. Part 2 (8 minutes): Agree with signs instead of voices: point, wave or thumbs up. Both confirm before walking.
  4. Part 3 (8 minutes): Remove 2–4 hoops. Students without nests are hawks. Hawks may walk to an empty nest during an agreed switch. No tagging or contact.
  5. A bird who loses a nest becomes a hawk; a hawk who reaches an empty nest becomes a bird. If two arrive together, both stop outside and agree who stays; the other takes the hawk role. Never race or push for a hoop.
  6. Freeze between parts to model the next rule. Everyone remains involved.

If this happens

  • Arrivals become a race: freeze and return to partner switches without hawks.
  • A hawk waits beside one nest: ask them to find open space away from occupied nests.
  • Two students disagree: stop outside the hoop and use a calm choice or teacher help.

How a round ends. About 8 minutes per part, with brief demonstrations and resets. Shorten to one 5–8 minute part for a warm-up; nobody is eliminated.

Cues

  • Look, ask or sign, agree, then walk.
  • Find a clear path.
  • Stop outside a busy nest.
  • “Freeze”: feet still, hands to yourself, eyes on the teacher.

Variations

  • Kindergarten–Grade 1: Partner switches only on the first day; teacher models each step.
  • Grades 2–4: Add non-verbal agreements, then hawks only when calm walking is reliable.
  • Grades 5–6 enrichment: a short communication warm-up with partner switches. No extra outcome claimed.

Teaching tips

  • Ask: “Which sign made your plan clear?” Observe agreed switches and safe paths.
  • Related to Switch / Chicken Checkers in the final nest-claiming stage. This card keeps the distinct verbal and non-verbal partner-agreement progression; no second Switch card.
  • With about 60: use waves of 20–30 rather than filling a tight gym with 60 nests. Waiting students practise signs with a partner in a separate supervised area. Two play areas need adequate spacing and supervision.
  • Support: model invitation signs or provide simple picture cards. Use floor markers around a larger clear nest for mobility aids; do not wheel over hoop rims. Offer a partner and follow ISP supports in the class binder.
  • Before teaching: check the class medical alert list and ISP supports in the class binder. Demonstrate the stop signal and follow school policy and SPHEReS guidance.
