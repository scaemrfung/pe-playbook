1. Solar System

Cooperative & circus

New this week

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: Hula Hoops - 30" : 4 / 10; Softex (yellow) : 4 / 10, plus 2 spares; Whistle : 1 (24 students / 60 students).

Small groups stand in a circle around a hoop. One player spins the hoop on its edge in the middle. While it spins, the group throws and catches a soft ball across the circle and counts passes out loud. When the hoop falls, the group records its score, picks a new spinner and tries to beat its record. Nobody is ever out.

Suggested month: January · also fits March, June · Slot: 10–25 · small groups of 5–6 · 10–25 minutes

Set-up: Make groups of 5–6. Each group stands in a circle about 4 m across, with one hoop lying flat in the centre. Keep circles at least 3 m apart and 2 m back from walls and the stage.

How we play

  1. One player is the spinner. They lift the hoop and spin it on its edge in the middle of the circle (source).
  2. As soon as the hoop spins, the rest of the group throws and catches the ball around the circle, counting each catch out loud (source).
  3. You may not pass to the person beside you. Throw across the circle (source).
  4. When the hoop falls, stop and remember your score (source).
  5. Choose a new spinner and try to beat your group record (source). Everyone takes a turn as spinner (our adaptation).

How a round ends. When the hoop falls, the group records its score, picks a new spinner and tries to beat its record. Nobody is ever out.

Variations

  • Grades 1–2 (support): roll or bounce-pass the ball instead of throwing. The teacher or a helper spins the hoop if students can't yet.
  • Grades 3–4: underhand and two-hand chest passes only. Add "call the name of the person you throw to."
  • Grades 5–6: one-hand catches only, or add a second ball when the group reaches 10 passes.
  • 60 students: run 10 groups of 6 in two rows of 5 across the full gym, each circle 3 m from the next. Same rules. One teacher signal starts and stops all groups.

Teaching tips

  • With about 60: run 10 groups of 6 in two rows of 5 across the full gym, each circle 3 m from the next. Same rules. One teacher signal starts and stops all groups.

2. Flag Frenzy RPS

Locomotor & listen

New this week

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 1–2, also good for 3–4

Equipment: Flag Football Tails (yellow, blue, red) : 36 / 90 (one per student plus spares in the hoops); Hula Hoops - 30" (flag stations) : 4 / 6; Pylons - saucer (boundaries) : 8 / 16; Whistle : 1 (24 students / 60 students).

Everyone tucks a flag at the waist and travels around the gym. Anyone can challenge anyone to rock, paper, scissors, and a challenge must be accepted. The winner collects one flag from the other player. Out of flags? Jog to a hoop, do 5 jumping jacks, take a starter flag and jump back in. Nobody sits out.

Suggested month: October · also fits September · Slot: 0–10 · whole class · 0–10 minutes (warm-up)

Set-up: Mark the playing area with saucer pylons, 2 m in from the walls and the stage. Place the hoops just inside the boundary, spread around the area, each with spare tails inside. Each student tucks one tail into the back of their waistband with about 30 cm hanging.

How we play

  1. Travel around the area. Challenge anyone you meet; if someone challenges you, you must accept (source).
  2. Play one round of rock, paper, scissors. Say it together and throw on "shoot."
  3. The winner takes one flag from the loser (source). The loser hands it over; nobody grabs (our adaptation).
  4. Out of flags? Jog to a hoop, do 5 jumping jacks, take one starter flag and jump back in (source). Nobody sits out.
  5. Play 2–3 minute rounds. At “Freeze,” count your flags, return them to the hoops, start again with one flag, and change the way students travel (our adaptation).

How a round ends. Play 2–3 minute rounds. At “Freeze,” count your flags, return them to the hoops, start again with one flag, and change the way students travel.

Variations

  • Grades 1–2: walk only. Teacher calls the travel each round (walk, tiptoe, gallop).
  • Grades 3–4: skip, gallop or side-step. Winners must find a new partner each time.
  • Grades 5–6: best of three, and players with no flags jog one lap of the boundary before the hoop.
  • 60 students: split the gym in half with saucer pylons and run two games of 30, each with 3 hoops of spare tails. Same rules and signals.

Teaching tips

  • With about 60: split the gym in half with saucer pylons and run two games of 30, each with 3 hoops of spare tails. Same rules and signals.

3. Five in a Row

Target & send

New this week

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: Hula Hoops - 30" and Hula Hoops - 24" (B-10, Y-10, R-5) : 30 / 50 in total (5 per team; 6 teams of 4 / 10 teams of 6); Bean Bags : 30 / 50 (5 per team, one for each hoop); Pylons - saucer (start lines) : 6 / 10; Whistle : 1 (24 students / 60 students).

Each team tosses a bean bag so it lands inside a hoop. Every success earns another hoop in a line. Then the team has to land a bean bag in each hoop in order, one toss per player: two, three, four, then five hoops in a row. Nobody is out. Teams keep the hoops they have earned. (Our adaptation: the source bounces a ball; this version uses bean bags, which land and stay in the hoop, and no bounce.)

Suggested month: January · also fits March, June · Slot: 10–20 · teams of 4–6 · 10–20 minutes

Set-up: Give each team a lane across the gym with a saucer pylon start line and a stack of 5 hoops. The team places its first hoop flat about 2 m in front of the start line. Lanes are at least 2 m apart and end 2 m short of the wall. With 60 students, give each team 3–4 of the 30" hoops and 1–2 of the 24" hoops, and put the smaller hoops at the end of the line (our adaptation).

How we play

  1. The first player tosses a bean bag underhand so it lands inside the hoop (adapted from the source's bounce).
  2. A bean bag that lands in earns the team another hoop. Add it in a line behind the first (source).
  3. Now the team has to land a bean bag in each hoop in order, one toss per player: the first hoop, then the second, and so on, with a bean bag left in each hoop. Keep adding hoops until there are five (adapted from the source).
  4. Miss? Collect the bean bags and the next player starts again from the team's current level. You never lose hoops you have earned (source).
  5. A team that finishes all five starts again with a harder challenge from the variations, so nobody waits (our adaptation; in the source, the first team to five wins).

How a round ends. Teams keep the hoops they have earned. A team that finishes all five starts again with a harder challenge from the variations, so nobody waits.

Variations

  • Grades 1–2: one hoop only, a closer start line, two-hand underhand toss. Count successful landings as a team.
  • Grades 3–4: underhand toss. Move the start line back 1 m after each full set.
  • Grades 5–6: toss with the non-dominant hand on the next set, or toss from a kneeling start.
  • 60 students: 10 teams of 6 in 10 lanes across the full gym (two rows of 5 lanes facing the side walls if needed). Turns rotate quickly, so lines stay short.

Teaching tips

  • With about 60: 10 teams of 6 in 10 lanes across the full gym (two rows of 5 lanes facing the side walls if needed). Turns rotate quickly, so lines stay short.
