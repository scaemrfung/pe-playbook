1. 2-Ball

Target & send

New this week

Grades: 3–6 · Best fit: Grades 5–6, also good for 3–4

Equipment: Full basketball court with both hoops (the side hoops can be used too); cones to mark four corner jails; pinnies in 4 colours; 1 soft dodgeball as the throwing ball (one colour); 1 soft ball of a different colour as the kicking / jailbreak ball (slightly bigger if you have one). For big classes, add a second pair (one more throwing ball and one more kicking ball in the same two colours).

Four teams, two balls: a throwing ball and a kicking ball. Hit players go to a corner jail and get out with a tag, a throw from jail or a jailbreak basket

Suggested month: October · also fits January · Slot: 16–25 · 4 even teams, one per corner, in pinnies · timed rounds of 4–5 minutes · 16–25 minutes

Set-up: Cone a jail in each of the four corners of the basketball court, at least 1.5 m in from the walls. Make four teams that are as even as you can, one per corner, in pinnies. Place the throwing ball and the kicking ball in the middle. Everyone starts inside their corner jail. Name the two balls out loud before Go.

How we play

  1. On "Go" (or the whistle), everyone leaves the jails and play begins. House rule start: the first player to grab a ball from the centre carries it back to your team's corner before you throw or kick.
  2. The throwing ball is thrown to hit players, like dodgeball. After the start, you may not walk or run with it. If you do, you go to jail.
  3. The kicking ball is kicked to hit players. You may not pick it up or kick it up into your hands. If you do, you go to jail.
  4. If you are hit by either ball, you go into any jail you choose. A catch is a save. The thrower is out (to jail).
  5. No friendly fire: a hit on your own teammate doesn't count. Pass to teammates to move the ball around.
  6. If you step into a jail (for example while backing up without looking), you are in jail.
  7. Ways out of jail: tag a player who comes too close to your jail (you are free, and they go to jail); or, if the throwing ball rolls into your jail, throw it from the jail and hit a player in the game to be free.
  8. If the kicking ball goes into a jail, it becomes the jailbreak ball. Anyone in that jail may pick it up and shoot at the basketball hoop. A basket frees everyone in that jail. Only the kicking ball can be used for a jailbreak shot.
  9. Players in jail may move to any other jail at any time, for example to reach the jail where a ball has landed. They may not touch a ball that is in play while they move.
  10. Our no-elimination version: the video plays until one team is left. We don't. Play timed rounds of 4–5 minutes, then everyone resets to the corners.
  11. 30-second jail limit: if you are still in jail after about 30 seconds (a slow count, or one song chorus), do 5 jumping jacks and walk back in along the sideline. If more than half the class is in jail, call "Jailbreak all": everyone returns after 5 jacks.

If this happens

  • Lots of players are stuck in jail: call "Jailbreak all". Everyone does 5 jacks and comes back in.
  • Players walk or run with the throwing ball, or pick up the kicking ball: that player goes to jail. Repeat: throw ball = hands, kick ball = feet.
  • Players back into a jail without looking: freeze and remind them, "Eyes up when you back away from a jail."
  • The class is mixing up the two balls: play a round with the throwing ball only, then add the kicking ball.
  • A ball at head height: it doesn't count, and the thrower goes to jail.

How a round ends. Timed rounds of 4–5 minutes, then everyone resets to the corners. Each team earns 1 point for every jailbreak basket. At the end of a round, every team counts its players in the game. Add the two for the round score. There is no winner by elimination. This card replaces the house rule about all the balls ending up on one side.

Cues

  • Throw ball = hands. Kick ball = feet. Never switch.
  • Pass before you throw. Move after you send.
  • Eyes up when you back away from a jail.
  • Ball in your jail? Throw ball = hit to free yourself. Kick ball = shoot to free your whole jail.
  • Freeze on "Freeze", ball still.

Variations

  • Easier: one ball only, larger soft balls, a closer or lower basket (backboard counts), shorter rounds, a teacher-led countdown for the jail limit.
  • Harder: add a second pair of balls, the shot must go in, require one pass before a throw, rotate one "captain" who alone may make jail tags.

Teaching tips

  • Teach it in two stages: play round 1 with only the throwing ball and the jail tag-out, then add the kicking ball and the jailbreak shot in round 2.
  • Use clearly different colours for the two balls, and name them out loud before Go.
  • Our dodgeball house rules apply (start, hit zone, catches) unless this card says otherwise. The game ends on the timer, not when all the balls are on one side.
  • Supports and inclusion: For students with an Individual Support Plan (ISP), check the plan first. Options: a set role (a jail shooter who stays near a hoop, the scorekeeper, the jail-limit timer), a buddy on the same team, a softer or larger ball, or a quieter corner of the court.
  • Sub notes: Plan A, run the no-elimination version with timed 4–5 minute rounds. Set up the cones, pinnies and two balls before class. Plan B, if the class is unsettled or the basketball court isn't free, play the Grades 1–2 roll-and-kick version in half the gym: one soft ball rolled at shoes, a hit = 5 jumping jacks in a corner and back in, no jail tags or shots.
  • Related on the site (not the same game): Backboard Dodgeball (a backboard hit brings players back), Jail-catch (prisonball), Four-square dodgeball (four teams).
  • Year plan: October, January. Fits the Dodgeball unit and its house rules.

2. Hockey Dribbling Shapes

Relays & stations

New this week

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 1–2

Equipment: 1 floor-hockey stick and 1 soft ball per student (or 1 per pair taking turns), about 30 low cones, 4 tall cones.

A stick-handling progression where everyone dribbles at once: up and back a cone line, around a circle of gates, then around a star of cones

Suggested month: November · also fits October · Slot: 6–15 or 16–25 · Everyone at once, each student in their own lane or shape (or pairs taking turns) · 10–15 minutes · good first-week hockey lesson

Set-up: Set up three kinds of shapes around the gym, at least 3 m apart and 2 m from the walls, the stage and the doors: cone lines (5–6 cones in a row), circles of cone gates (pairs of cones), and stars (one tall cone in the middle with 4 small cones around it). You can also use the gym floor lines as circles, triangles and squares. Practise “Sticks down!” before handing out sticks.

How we play

  1. Step 1, simple dribble: dribble up the line of cones with small touches, stop the ball at the end cone, then dribble back.
  2. Add on: at the end cone, stop, step and push-shoot into a cone goal, then dribble back.
  3. Step 2, circle dribble: dribble through each gate around the circle, then turn and go the other way. You can also dribble along a gym line in a circle, triangle or square.
  4. Step 3, star dribble: start at an outside cone, dribble in around the middle cone, out to the next outside cone, back to the middle, and keep going until you are back at the start.
  5. Ball-handling extra: in your own space, small touches forward and back, then left and right.
  6. Everyone works at the same time. When sharing a shape, one student goes at a time.

If this happens

  • Balls fly away from big hits: freeze and say “Small touches, ball close to the blade.”
  • Students look at the ball the whole time: call out a number of fingers; dribblers say it back.
  • Lanes get crowded: spread the shapes out or turn some into feet-only stations.
  • A student is new to sticks: dribble with feet first, then add the stick.

How a round ends. No outs. Each step runs 2–3 minutes, then everyone does “Sticks down!” and moves to the next step or shape. Grades 5–6 can time one star lap for a personal best.

Cues

  • Stay low, knees bent.
  • One hand on top of the stick, the other halfway down.
  • Small touches with the middle of the blade, both sides.
  • Head up to see where you are going.
  • “Sticks down!”: stick flat, ball still.

Variations

  • Easier: walk pace, steps 1 and 2 only, feet only for students new to sticks.
  • Harder: change direction on the whistle, backhand side of the blade only, finish with a push-shot.
  • Personal best: time one star lap and try to beat it.

Teaching tips

  • Teach the grip and “Sticks down!” first, then hand out sticks.
  • Use it as the first lesson of the hockey unit: it gives every student lots of touches before any game.
  • Related on the site (not the same game): Hockey hurdle dribble (Big-Group Games) is a relay through a line of low cones.
  • Supports and inclusion: a student new to sticks can use feet only, a shorter stick or a bigger, slower ball. A student who uses a mobility aid can work in a wider lane with closer targets. A student who needs a break can be the counter or timer, then rejoin.
  • Sub notes: check the class medical alert list in the sub binder, and follow the ISP supports listed in the class binder. Hand out sticks only after the “Sticks down!” signal has been practised.
  • Year plan: November, October. Fits the first floor hockey week (October Week 4, Oct 26–30).

3. Gates & Hurdles Dribble

Invasion & team

New this week

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: 1 stick and 1 soft ball per student, 20–30 cones (10–15 gates), 6–10 low hurdles (or pool noodles on cones).

Everyone dribbles at once and taps the ball through scattered cone gates and under low hurdles for points; Grades 4–6 add gatekeepers

Suggested month: November · also fits October · Slot: 6–15 or 16–25 · Whole class at once, each with a stick and ball · 45–60 second rounds · 10–15 minutes

Set-up: Scatter cone gates (pairs of cones about 1 m apart) and low hurdles around the gym, at least 2 m from the walls, the stage and the doors. Each student starts in open space with a stick and a ball.

How we play

  1. On “Go”, everyone dribbles at the same time with small touches.
  2. Tap the ball through a gate or under a hurdle, then move to a different one.
  3. Score 1 point for every gate or hurdle you get the ball through before time is up (45–60 seconds).
  4. You cannot use the same gate twice in a row.
  5. Play again and try to beat your own score.
  6. Gatekeepers (Grades 4–6): half the class stands in a gate with the blade on the floor. Dribblers tap through open gates. Gatekeepers block with the blade only, with no reaching into the dribbler. Swap roles every round.

If this happens

  • Students crowd one gate: say “One dribbler through a gate at a time; find an open one.”
  • Students race and lose control: count only gates where the ball stays close.
  • Gatekeepers reach or hook at players: blade stays in the gate, on the floor.
  • Scores get argued: each student counts their own points out loud.

How a round ends. No outs. Each round is 45–60 seconds; everyone counts their own points, then tries to beat that score next round.

Cues

  • Small touches, ball close.
  • Eyes up and find an open gate.
  • Slow down near others.
  • Blade on the floor.
  • “Sticks down!”: stick flat, ball still.

Variations

  • Easier: walk pace, wide gates (1.5 m), hurdles only if low.
  • Harder: narrow gates, backhand side only for one round.
  • Gatekeepers for Grades 4–6.

Teaching tips

  • Count out loud together on the first round so everyone learns to keep score.
  • Related on the site (not the same game): Hockey hurdle dribble (a lane relay) and Through the Gates (partner passing) on Big-Group Games, and Avoid the Mines.
  • Supports and inclusion: a student new to sticks can use feet only, a shorter stick or a bigger, slower ball. A student who uses a mobility aid can work in a wider lane with closer targets. A student who needs a break can be the counter or timer, then rejoin.
  • Sub notes: check the class medical alert list in the sub binder, and follow the ISP supports listed in the class binder. Hand out sticks only after the “Sticks down!” signal has been practised.
  • Year plan: November, October. A good second activity in the floor hockey week.

4. Zigzag & Shoot

Target & send

New this week

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: 1 stick and 1 soft ball per lane (2–3 students per lane), 3 small cones per lane, 1 folded mat or turned-over bench per lane.

Zigzag around two cones, circle a third, then push-shoot back at a target beside the start; count hits in 1 minute

Suggested month: November · also fits October · Slot: 16–25 · Lanes of 2–3 · 1-minute turns · 10–15 minutes as a station or whole-class lanes

Also called: Broomstick Hockey Skills

Set-up: Each lane has a target beside the start (a folded mat on its side or a bench turned over), a small cone about 0.6 m out, a second about 1.2 m out and a third about 1.8 m out. Lanes at least 3 m apart and 2 m from the walls, the stage and the doors.

How we play

  1. Start with the ball next to the target.
  2. Zigzag around the first two cones, then circle the third cone.
  3. In line with the third cone, push-shoot back at the target.
  4. A hit is 1 point. Get the ball and go again.
  5. Play for 1 minute and count your points. Then rotate: one goes, one counts, one stick-handles in place behind the start line.
  6. Push the ball with the blade. Do not put the blade on top of the ball and drag it.

If this happens

  • Students drag the ball with the blade on top: show “push from behind the ball”.
  • Shots get lifted or slapped: the point doesn't count; push along the floor.
  • Waiting players drift into the lane: they stick-handle in place behind the start line.
  • A student can't reach the target: move it closer.

How a round ends. 1-minute turns, then rotate jobs. Each student tries to beat their own score. No outs.

Cues

  • Shake hands with the stick in the middle, thumb down, other hand on top.
  • Small touches around the cones.
  • Push the ball, don't drag it.
  • Shoot only at your own target.
  • “Sticks down!”: stick flat, ball still.

Variations

  • Easier: target close, walk the course, count hits together.
  • Harder: move the target further away, add a fourth cone, zigzag on the backhand side.

Teaching tips

  • Started as an at-home broomstick challenge; in the gym it works as a lane station.
  • Related on the site (not the same game): Hockey pin knockdown (Big-Group Games) is a tunnel-dribble relay at a pin.
  • Supports and inclusion: a student new to sticks can use feet only, a shorter stick or a bigger, slower ball. A student who uses a mobility aid can work in a wider lane with closer targets. A student who needs a break can be the counter or timer, then rejoin.
  • Sub notes: check the class medical alert list in the sub binder, and follow the ISP supports listed in the class binder. Hand out sticks only after the “Sticks down!” signal has been practised.
  • Year plan: November, October.

5. Move Up Hockey

Target & send

New this week

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 1–2

Equipment: Per lane: 1 stick, 1 soft ball, 4 spot markers, 1 empty plastic bottle or small cone (Cup Stack: 4 stackable cups or small cones).

Push-pass to knock over a target; each hit moves the target one marker further away, then dribble back and shoot again

Suggested month: November · also fits October · Slot: 16–25 · Partners in lanes · 1-minute turns · 10–15 minutes as a station or whole-class lanes

Also called: Cup Stack Hockey

Set-up: Each lane has a start line and four markers about 1.2 m, 1.8 m, 2.4 m and 3 m from the start. Put a light target (empty plastic bottle or small cone) on the first marker. Lanes at least 3 m apart and 2 m from the walls, the stage and the doors.

How we play

  1. Push-pass from the middle of the start line to knock over the target.
  2. Hit: move the target to the next marker, dribble the ball back to the start and shoot again.
  3. Miss: get the ball, dribble back and shoot again.
  4. After you knock over the target on the last marker, dribble back to the start to finish.
  5. Play a 1-minute challenge, or time how long it takes and try to beat your personal best. Partners swap: one shoots, one resets the target and counts.
  6. Cup Stack Hockey: put four stackable cups (or small cones) in a row 1.8–2.4 m from the start, about 30–45 cm apart. Any touch counts. When you hit one, stack it on another cup and dribble back. You finish when all the cups are in one stack.

If this happens

  • The partner stands behind the target: they wait beside the lane until the ball stops.
  • Shots get lifted or slapped: the hit doesn't count; push along the floor.
  • Students skip the dribble back: the next shot only counts from the start line.
  • A student is stuck on one marker: move the markers closer.

How a round ends. 1-minute turns or a timed finish for a personal best, then partners swap. No outs.

Cues

  • Push-pass from the middle of the start line.
  • Look at the target, then the ball.
  • Follow through low, toward the target.
  • Dribble back, don't carry.
  • “Sticks down!”: stick flat, ball still.

  • Easier: fewer targets, closer markers, big cones.
  • Harder: more distances, further away, partner sets the target.

7. Jump Shot Handball

Invasion & team

Grades: 4–6 · Best fit: Grades 5–6, also good for 3–4

Equipment: 1 small soft ball per game (foam or gator-skin, about 15–18 cm); 4 portable goals, 2 side by side at each end (or 4 floor hockey nets, or cone goals 1.5 m wide); cones or floor tape to mark an arc about 6 m from each pair of goals (the basketball three-point line works); pinnies in 4 colours; 1 wall target (tape square) for the waiting teams

Pass-only team handball: one pass before a shot, a slow 3 seconds to pass or shoot, and attackers shoot from outside the arc (jump shots allowed if you take off before the line)

Suggested month: December · also fits January · Slot: 16–25 · 4 teams of 6 (5 on court, 1 sub) · 4-minute games · 15–20 minutes

Set-up: Use the basketball court. Put two goals side by side on each end line, at least 2 m in from the end wall. Mark the arc (the defensive zone) in front of each pair of goals with cones or tape. The free-throw lane is the goalie area. With 24 students, make 4 teams of 6. Two teams play while the other two practise passing and jump-shot form at a wall target beside the court, behind a waiting line, then rotate. If the gym fits two courts across its width, run two games at once with one goal at each end of each court.

How we play

  1. Each team puts 5 players on the court and 1 sub on the sideline. The sub swaps in after every goal.
  2. One player per team is the goalie and stays in the free-throw lane. Nobody else enters the goalie area except to get a loose ball.
  3. Move the ball only by passing and catching. No running with the ball. The player with the ball may pivot.
  4. You have a slow 3 seconds to pass or shoot. Count it aloud: "One-thousand-one, one-thousand-two, one-thousand-three." If time runs out, put the ball down. The other team takes it.
  5. The ball must be passed once, player to player, before anyone can shoot. A pass from the goalie does not count.
  6. Only defenders may stand inside the arc. Attackers shoot from outside it. For a jump shot, take off from outside the line and release the ball in the air.
  7. Defenders guard the ball carrier from one arm's length, arms up to block passes and shots. No contact and no grabbing the ball.
  8. A goal scores 1 point. The goalie restarts play with a pass from the goalie area.
  9. Play 4-minute games, then rotate teams so the wall-station teams come on.

If this happens

  • One player takes every shot: add a rule that three different players must touch the ball before a shot.
  • Defenders crowd the arc: no more than 3 defenders inside the arc at once.
  • Contact or ball stripping: free pass to the other team, and that defender restarts one arm's length away.
  • A shot goes high at the goalie: no goal, other team's ball, and remind everyone that shots stay below shoulder height.

How a round ends. 4-minute games. Rotate teams so everyone plays. Do not keep a league table.

Cues

  • Pass and move to open space.
  • Show a target with your hands.
  • One-thousand-one, one-thousand-two, one-thousand-three: pass or shoot.
  • Take off before the line, shoot in the air, land on two feet.
  • Freeze on the whistle. Ball holder freezes too.

Variations

  • Every player on the court must touch the ball before a shot.
  • Shoot at a target zone only (low corners of the goal) for accuracy.
  • Add a second ball for Grades 5–6 once spacing is good.
  • Play without goalies and use cone targets.

Teaching tips

  • Teach the three rules before the first game: one pass before a shot, the 3-second count, and attackers stay outside the arc.
  • Run a 3-minute passing drill first (pass, pivot, pass) so the game does not stop for travelling calls.
  • Shooting in the air: practise "take off, catch your balance, land on two feet" at the wall station before the game.
  • The Front Range clip uses gym mats around the arc. Cones or floor tape are safer for jump shots, because landings stay on the flat floor. If you use mats, lay them flat and well inside the arc so nobody lands on a mat edge.
  • Scoring: 1 point per goal. Optional: a goal from a jump shot counts 2 for Grades 5–6.
  • Supports and inclusion: Larger, softer ball; a 5-second count; a "free pass" rule where a player who has not touched the ball receives an unguarded pass; a role as sub-rotation leader or score keeper for a student who needs a movement break, with a return after one game. Model every rule with a walk-through before play.
  • Year plan: December, January. An invasion game for games month that also leads into basketball spacing and passing.

8. Spin the Pin Tag & Cone Flip Mixer

Tag & chase

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: 2 bowling pins (or plastic bottles); 2 hoops; 2 short foam noodle tag sticks; 8–10 small cones for cone flip (one per pair, plus spares); a row of upright gym mats or a volleyball net to divide the gym; 8–12 boundary cones; optional tape X's for younger grades

Half the class plays Cone Flip Mixer in pairs. Win twice and you move to Spin the Pin Tag: the pin picks the tagger, who has 5 slow seconds to make a tag, and whoever loses the exchange goes back to cone flip

Suggested month: October · also fits September, December · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class split in two areas · 2 tag groups of 4 plus cone-flip pairs · 10–15 minutes · good for sub days

Also called: Spin the Pin Super Tag · Spin the Pin Cone Flip

Set-up: Divide the gym in half with standing mats or the volleyball net. Cone-flip side: scatter pairs with one small cone each, well spaced. Tag side: cone two separate tag zones (each about the size of a volleyball half-court), at least 2 m from the walls and stage. Put one hoop with a pin lying in it in the centre of each zone, with one noodle beside each hoop. Start with 4 students in each tag zone (8 total) and the other 16 at cone flip (8 pairs).

How we play

  1. Cone flip side: partners take turns flipping their cone (toss it with one flick so it spins). The first player to land it standing upright twice wins.
  2. The winner walks to the tag side and joins the tag zone that needs a player (the teacher points). The other player finds a new cone-flip partner.
  3. Tag side: four players stand around the hoop. The newest player spins the pin.
  4. When the pin stops, the player it points to is the tagger. They pick up the noodle while everyone else moves away inside the zone.
  5. The tagger has 5 slow seconds to make a soft noodle tag on the back or shoulder. The group counts aloud: "Five, four, three, two, one."
  6. If the tagger makes a tag, the tagged player goes to the cone-flip side. If the tagger misses, the tagger goes to the cone-flip side instead.
  7. The noodle goes back beside the hoop. The three who stay wait at the hoop for the next player from cone flip, who spins the pin.
  8. Both sides run at the same time, nonstop.

If this happens

  • The pin lands between two players: spin again.
  • Taggers swing the noodle: that player hands the noodle back and goes to cone flip; remind "touch, not swing".
  • A tag zone has a line of players waiting: send the extra player to the other zone, or call a 30-second cone-flip-only round.
  • Pairs argue about a flip: rock, paper, scissors decides, then flip again.

How a round ends. Continuous play for the set time (8–12 minutes). Freeze, then everyone does one final cone flip together.

Cues

  • Two upright flips, then go.
  • Spin, point, move.
  • Count it out loud: five to one.
  • Touch with the noodle, back or shoulder.
  • Freeze on the whistle, noodle down.

Variations

  • Change the locomotor move on the tag side (skip, side-gallop, power walk).
  • Cone flip with the weaker hand only.
  • Three-cone flip: flip a small cone into a larger upturned cone.
  • Rock, paper, scissors replaces cone flip for a classroom or quiet version.

Teaching tips

  • Teach cone flip with the whole class first (2 minutes), then demo one tag exchange with four students while everyone watches.
  • Tape X's around each hoop so younger players start spaced out.
  • This is a routine game. Once a class knows it, a substitute can run it with one demo.
  • Related cards: Fishbowl Tag also uses a cone flip, but as a re-entry task. This game uses cone flip as a separate mixer.
  • Scoring: No score needed. Optional: students count their own cone-flip wins.
  • Supports and inclusion: Pair a student who needs support with a buddy who moves with them between sides. Offer a larger cone or a "flip onto the mat counts" rule. A student who needs a break can be the pin spinner for a zone for two turns, then rejoin. Use a picture card of the rotation at each side.
  • Year plan: October, September, December. A tag game and a sub-day game for any month.

9. Fire the Cannons

Target & send

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: Volleyball net lowered to about 1–1.2 m (or a rope between two standards); 4 throwing cones (one per team); targets on the far side: 8 hoops (1 point), 6 tall cones (2 points), 6 spot markers (5 points), 4 buckets or bins (10 points); per team, a set of soft throwables: 2 beanbags, 1 foam ball, 1 tennis ball, 1 foam football, 1 soft flying disc, 1 soft volleyball or trainer ball; tally sheet or whiteboard

Team lines throw over a chest-high net at point targets (hoop 1, tall cone 2, spot marker 5, bucket 10) on the teacher's "Fire!", then collect together, with a new type of throw each round

Suggested month: December · also fits May, March · Slot: 16–25 · 4 teams of 6 in lines · 10–15 minutes · teacher calls every throw

Set-up: Set the net across the gym. Put 4 throwing cones in a row about 3–4 m from the net, at least 3 m apart. Each team lines up behind its cone. Scatter the targets 2–8 m beyond the net. Put the hoops closest, then tall cones, spot markers, and buckets farthest. Keep all targets at least 2 m from the far wall.

How we play

  1. The first player in each line steps up to the throwing cone with the team's object for that round.
  2. Ready position: "Point to your target, scratch your ear with the ball, wait for my call."
  3. The teacher calls "Fire!" All four throwers send their object over the net at a target.
  4. Score: lands in a hoop = 1, knocks or hits a tall cone = 2, lands on a spot marker = 5, lands in a bucket = 10. The thrower calls their score; a teammate records it.
  5. The teacher calls "Collect." Only the four throwers go under or around the net, fetch their object, return, and hand it to the next player. They then go to the back of the line.
  6. The teacher calls "Fire!" again only when every collector is back behind the throwing line.
  7. Every round, the teacher names a new way to fire the cannon: Beanbag: underhand throw (swing back, point, step, follow through). Large foam ball: two-hand overhead throw. Tennis ball or foam football: overhand throw (opposite-foot step). Soft disc: backhand fling. Soft volleyball: underhand serve (fist, swing straight through). Foam football: drop punt. Foam soccer ball: bounce and kick (Grades 3–6).

If this happens

  • Someone throws before "Fire!": that throw does not count; that player waits one turn.
  • A collector is still past the net: no "Fire!" until everyone is back. Count the collectors aloud.
  • Objects fly at people: switch the whole class to underhand only for the round.
  • Lines get long and slow: add a fifth cone and team, or move targets closer so more throws score.

How a round ends. One round = every player in each line has one turn with the round's object. Then change the throw.

Cues

  • Point, scratch your ear, step, throw.
  • Step with the opposite foot.
  • Follow through to your target.
  • Fire on the call. Collect on the call.
  • Freeze on the whistle, objects down.

Variations

  • Target choice: before throwing, call the target you are aiming for; a called hit doubles the points.
  • Team goal: reach 50 points as a class across all four teams.
  • Moving targets: one student per team slides a hoop slowly along the floor (Grades 5–6, underhand throws only).
  • Longer distance: move the throwing cones back 1 m after each round.

Teaching tips

  • Demo each throw before its round with the three cues: ready, step, follow through.
  • Put the 10-point buckets at the farthest distance so younger students aim at closer targets first.
  • The "Fire!" / "Collect" calls are the safety routine. Practise them once with no objects.
  • Related cards: Coconut Knockdown and Hidden Fleet are also throwing games. This one stands out for its point zones and the change of sending skill each round.
  • Scoring: Teams keep a running total and try to beat their own score from the last round. Optional: Grades 5–6 add their points mentally and report the total.

10. Rope Hoop Scoop

Cooperative & circus

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 1–2

Equipment: 24 skipping ropes (2 per pair); 24–36 hoops (mixed colours if you have them); 12 cones (one home cone per pair); optional colour-to-points chart (for example red 1, blue 2, yellow 3)

Partners share two skipping ropes to scoop a hoop off the floor and carry it home without touching it with hands or feet; if it drops, stop and scoop again

Suggested month: February · also fits December · Slot: 16–25 · 12 pairs · one cone per pair · 8–12 minutes · rounds end when the last hoop is home

Set-up: Place 12 home cones around the outside of the gym, at least 2 m from the walls and about 3 m apart. Each pair starts at its cone with two skipping ropes. Scatter the hoops flat on the floor in the middle of the gym, spaced so pairs can reach them from different sides.

How we play

  1. Each partner holds one end of each rope, so the two ropes run side by side between you, like a stretcher.
  2. On "Go", walk to any hoop on the floor.
  3. Slide your ropes under the hoop without using hands or feet on the hoop. Lift it together.
  4. Carry the hoop home to your cone and lower it next to the cone. That hoop is yours.
  5. If the hoop falls, stop where you are, slide the ropes under it again, and keep going. Nobody restarts from home.
  6. Go back for another hoop. The round ends when the last hoop on the floor has been collected.
  7. Count your hoops (or add up the colour points). Then play the "put back" round: carry your hoops back to the middle the same way.

If this happens

  • Pairs grab hoops with hands: that hoop goes back to the middle; try again with ropes only.
  • Two pairs go for the same hoop: the pair that touched it first with a rope keeps it; the other pair picks another.
  • Pairs run and swing the ropes: freeze; walking only while carrying.
  • One partner does all the work: switch who calls "lift" and "lower" each hoop.

How a round ends. When the last hoop on the floor has been collected. Count, then play the put-back round.

Cues

  • Ropes low, slide under.
  • Ready, lift, together.
  • Walk when you carry.
  • Drop? Stop and scoop.
  • Freeze on the whistle, ropes on the floor.

Variations

  • One rope only (harder) or three-person teams with three ropes.
  • Carry two hoops at once (Grades 5–6).
  • Silent round: partners use only gestures.
  • Obstacle path: carry the hoop around one cone before going home.

Teaching tips

  • Demo with one pair first, showing the slide, the lift on "ready, lift" and the walk home.
  • Use colour points to build in quick addition (Grades 1–3) or a running total (Grades 4–6).
  • Related cards: Hoop Hut and Hoop Hut Knock Down use hoops for building. This game uses ropes as tools to lift and carry them.
  • Scoring: 1 point per hoop, or colour points (1, 2 or 3). Pairs try to beat their own count in the next round. Do not rank pairs aloud.
  • Supports and inclusion: Thicker ropes or pool noodles are easier to slide and grip. A student who uses a mobility aid can hold one end of each rope while a partner guides the lift. Allow groups of three. Post a picture card showing the rope "stretcher" grip.

12. NitroBall 2.0

Target & send

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 5–6, also good for 3–4

Equipment: Volleyball standards with the net lowered to waist height (or a rope between the standards); 1 four square / playground ball per court (beach balls or trainer balls for younger groups); floor tape or poly spots numbered 1–7 on each side; the free-throw lines as serve lines; pinnies in 3 colours; cones for a waiting line about 2 m from the court; 2–3 extra balls for the waiting team's wall passes

Volleyball meets four square: a waist-high net, a bounce before every hit, and the back wall and ceiling in play; 7 per side on numbered spots in a 3-1-3, rotate like volleyball, games to 11, winners stay on

Suggested month: March · also fits December · Slot: 16–25 · 3 teams of 8 (7 on court, 1 rotates in; the third team waits with a job) · games to 11 · 16–25 minutes

Also called: NitroBall

Set-up: Set the net across the middle of the court at waist height. For a class of 24, make three teams of 8: seven play and one waits to rotate in, and the third team waits to challenge the winner. Each side sets up seven players in a 3-1-3 (three in front, one in the middle, three in the back) on spots numbered 1 to 7, so students know exactly where to stand. Spot 4, the middle player, is the server. Cone a waiting line at least 2 m from the court, next to a side wall that is away from play, for the waiting team's wall passes.

How we play

  1. Spot 4 serves underhand from behind the free-throw line. The serve has to land before the other team's free-throw line.
  2. The ball must bounce before every hit. Hit with an open hand or forearms.
  3. There is no out of bounds. The back wall and the ceiling are in play. If the ball bounces off and you can get to it, keep it alive.
  4. The ball must go over the net between the poles to count. Around the outside is no good.
  5. A server gets one mulligan (a redo) on their first serve after a rotation, and a deep serve counts. After you have served for a point, there is no mulligan.
  6. Teams rotate one spot when the receiving team wins the point, as in volleyball. The player waiting on the side rotates in.
  7. Off the backboard, onto bags or equipment, or into a corner: redo the serve, no argument.
  8. Games go to 11. The winners stay on and the waiting team rotates in. The team that comes off becomes the waiting team and starts its job right away: bump-set wall passes at the side wall, with one player keeping score for the court.

If this happens

  • Players crowd the ball: freeze, everyone back to their numbered spot, and remind them to call "mine".
  • Someone hits the ball before it bounces: the point goes to the other team. Demo "let it bounce, then hit".
  • The waiting team stands around: give each player a job (wall passes, scorekeeper, rotation caller), or run two courts with 4 teams of 6.
  • Arguments about serves off the backboard or into a corner: redo the serve, no argument.

How a round ends. Games go to 11 (7 for Grades 3–4). The winners stay on, the waiting team rotates in, and the team that comes off takes the waiting-team job.

Cues

  • Let it bounce, then hit.
  • Spot numbers first, then play.
  • Between the poles counts.
  • Call "mine" and move your feet.
  • Freeze on the whistle, ball still.

Variations

  • Optional rule from the original NitroBall (not in the 2.0 clip): 3 hits per side, no two hits in a row by the same player, and no kicking or heading.
  • Two courts: 4 teams of 6, so every team plays at once.
  • Easier: a bigger, lighter ball, a closer serve line, catch-and-toss allowed, fewer players per side.
  • Harder: 3 hits per side, no catching, and students run the rotation and the score themselves.

Teaching tips

  • Walk the 3-1-3 rotation once with no ball, so students know where each spot moves next.
  • Teach the bounce rule first: one rally where everyone catches after the bounce, then switch to hits.
  • Keep everyone moving: winners stay on, so the waiting team always has a job (bump-set wall passes, scorekeeping, calling the rotation). Two courts with 4 teams of 6 cut the wait to zero.
  • Scoring: Games to 11 (7 for Grades 3–4). The waiting team's scorekeeper calls the score before each serve.
  • Supports and inclusion: For students with an Individual Support Plan (ISP), check the plan and adjust the ball, the court size or the role (server, rotation caller, scorekeeper) as needed.
  • Sub notes: Plan A, run the game as written, with the spots and the net set up before class. Plan B, if the net isn't available or the class is unsettled, play the Grades 1–2 lead-up on a rope or bench net in small groups, or one-bounce partner passing over a line.

13. Old Grey Cat

Tag & chase

Grades: 1–4 · Best fit: Grades 1–2, also good for 3–4

Equipment: None. Optional: 8–12 cones to mark two safe lines (the “trees”); 1 pinny or scarf for each cat.

A chant-and-chase circle game: the birds circle the cat and chant, then run for the trees (safe lines) while the cat chases; the first bird tagged becomes the new cat

Suggested month: September · also fits October · Slot: 0–5 or 6–15 · Whole class in one circle (two cats for 24 students) · 5–10 minutes · no equipment needed · good for sub days

Set-up: Use the centre circle (or mark one with cones). Mark a safe line (“the trees”) across each end of the gym with cones or use existing floor lines. Keep each safe line at least 3 m in from the end wall and the stage, so runners have room to slow down. Teach the chant before the first round.

How we play

  1. The birds hold hands in a big circle. One player stands in the middle. That player is the old grey cat (give them a pinny or scarf).
  2. The birds walk around the cat in one direction and chant together: “We are weak little birds way up in a tree, you're an old grey cat and you can't catch me!”
  3. On the last word, the birds let go of hands and run to either safe line (the trees). The cat chases.
  4. Birds behind a safe line are safe. The cat tags with two fingers on the back or shoulder.
  5. The first bird tagged becomes the new cat. The old cat joins the circle as a bird.
  6. If nobody is tagged, the teacher picks a new cat.
  7. Everyone walks back to the circle, joins hands, and the next round starts.

If this happens

  • The same fast runner is never tagged and never gets a turn: let the cat choose the next cat, or give each student one turn as cat in rotation.
  • Birds let go early or run before the chant ends: the round restarts; the chant must finish first.
  • The circle gets pulled or swung: freeze; walk the circle slowly, arms relaxed.
  • The cat cannot catch anyone: add a second cat, or have the birds skip or gallop instead of running.
  • Birds crowd one safe line: say “Use both trees.”

How a round ends. When the cat tags a bird or every bird is behind a safe line. Rounds take about 30 seconds. Play 8–12 rounds.

Cues

  • Hold hands, walk the circle, chant together.
  • Let go, then go.
  • Eyes up, run to the trees.
  • Two fingers on the back or shoulder.
  • Freeze on the whistle, eyes on the teacher.

Variations

  • Change the circle travel each round: skip, gallop, side-slide, tiptoe.
  • Change the chase travel for one round: skip or gallop only.
  • Two cats for a class of 24; three cats in a big gym.
  • Cat's choice: the cat calls which safe line is open (“North tree only!”) just before the chase.
  • Classroom or no-gym day: walk-only version with one safe wall, desks pushed back.

Teaching tips

  • Teach the chant first, sitting down, then walk it once with no chase.
  • Model the tag and the slow-down after the safe line with one student before the first round.
  • Use it to teach fleeing and dodging: “Look for open space before you run.”
  • Related cards: Wake-Up Bear and Bakery Run Tag are also chant-and-chase games, and What time is it Mr. Fox? (Warm Up Games) is a close cousin. Old Grey Cat is simpler: no secret count or surprise call, one tag swaps roles, and the circle keeps moving during the chant.
  • The original game has no safe zone. The coned trees are our adaptation, so each round ends quickly and nobody runs into a wall.
  • Supports and inclusion: A student who needs support can walk the circle with a partner and flee together. Offer a closer safe spot (a hoop near the circle) for a student who uses a mobility aid, or make every chase a walk or skip round. Post the chant on a picture card for language learners. A student who needs a break can be the “chant leader” who starts each round, then rejoin.
  • Sub notes: check the class medical alert list in the sub binder, and follow the ISP supports listed in the class binder.
  • Year plan: September, October, any month. A quick K–3 opener or a sub-day warm-up.

17. Mooshake

Cooperative & circus

Grades: K–3 · Best fit: Grades 1–2, also good for 3–4

Equipment: A stuffed cow (or a soft ball) for each circle

Pass a stuffed cow (or soft ball) around the circle without a drop; every clean lap, everyone steps back; drop it and everyone yells “Moo-shake!”, wiggles, steps back in and starts again

Suggested month: December · also fits September · Slot: 0–5 or 16–25 · Circles of 8–12 · one stuffed cow or soft ball per circle · 5–8 minutes

Set-up: Students stand in a circle, a little more than arm's length apart.

How we play

  1. Stand in a circle. Pass the cow (or soft ball) from player to player around the circle with a soft underhand toss or hand-off.
  2. Keep the pass going without dropping it.
  3. After each clean lap, everyone takes one small step back, so the circle gets bigger.
  4. When the cow drops, everyone yells “Moo-shake!”, does a quick shake and wiggle together, steps back in to the starting circle and starts the pass again.
  5. With a big group, run several circles.

If this happens

  • Hard or high throws: switch to rolling for one round.
  • The same few get the cow: everyone must get it once before a repeat.

How a round ends. Play for the set time. Count clean laps (steps back) as a class goal. No one leaves the circle.

Cues

  • Eyes on the cow.
  • Soft underhand toss to someone ready.
  • Say their name before you pass.
  • Freeze on the whistle — hold the cow.

Variations

  • Use a soft ball instead of a stuffy.
  • Pass in order around the circle, then across.

Teaching tips

  • Watch the clip before the first play to see the moo-shake.

Suggested month: October · also fits September · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class · 1 bear and 2 wolves · 60–90 second rounds · 6–10 minutes total

Set-up: Cone a large rectangle at least 2 m in from the walls. Put the two exercise mats just outside one sideline.

How we play

  1. Choose 1 Big Bear and 2 Wolves. Everyone else is a Pronghorn (a fast prairie animal).
  2. The two Wolves hold one hoop together, or each holds a hoop and they stay side by side as a pack.
  3. On “Go”, the bear and wolves try to tag Pronghorn with a two-finger tag on the back or shoulder.

  • Eyes up, find open space.
  • Two fingers, back or shoulder.
  • Wolves move as one.

  • Count the Hungry Bear burst aloud with the class so it stays to 5 seconds.

19. Four Corner Tag

Tag & chase

Grades: 3–6 (est.) · Best fit: Grades 5–6, also good for 3–4

Equipment: 4 upright gym mats or 4 large cones as corners; 2 short foam noodle tag sticks (one per team); optional floor mats for waiting players; pinnies in 2 colours

Two teams run a noodle relay around four corner markers, trying to catch and tag the other team's runner

Suggested month: October · also fits September · Slot: 16–25 · Two teams · one runner each at a time · 8–12 minutes · good for sub days

Set-up: Stand the four mats in a large square in the middle of the gym, at least 3 m from the walls. Each team lines up at its own starting corner, on opposite corners.

How we play

  1. Two teams. The first runner on each team holds that team's noodle.
  2. On “Go”, runners travel around the outside of the four corners, in the same direction, trying to catch and tag the other team's runner with the noodle.
  3. Runners make clean handoffs of the noodle to the next teammate at their team's corner, so fresh runners keep the chase going.
  4. One clean noodle tag on the back or shoulder earns one point. Restart from the starting corners after each point and rotate the lines forward.
  5. Rules: never run past a tag (if you are tagged, the point counts and you stop); no cutting inside the corners; no interfering with the other team's runner or handoff; no stalling at your corner.

If this happens

  • One team keeps scoring: balance the teams and go again.
  • A runner cuts inside the corners: no point; restart from the starting corners.

How a round ends. Restart from the starting corners after each point. Play for the set time and rotate the lines forward.

Cues

  • Outside the mats every time.
  • Hand the noodle, don't throw it.
  • Next runner ready, hand out.
  • Freeze on the whistle — lower the noodle.

Variations

  • Change direction on a double whistle.
  • Add a third team with a third noodle.

Teaching tips

  • Watch the clip before the first play to see the handoff timing.
  • Balance teams if one side starts to win every point, and check that runners stay outside the mats.
  • Year plan: October, September — a tag game that works well on sub days.

Set-up: Mark three zones in front of each hoop: close (1 point), middle (2 points), far (3 points).

How we play

  1. Split the class into 3 teams of 8 (or 4 teams of 6 and run two side-court games if the gym has side hoops).
  2. Two teams play; the third team practises passing at a wall station beside the court, then rotates in.
  3. Move the football only by throwing and catching. No running with the ball (like travelling in basketball). A player may pivot.
  4. Work the ball down the court and shoot by throwing the football at the hoop. A made shot scores the point value of the zone it was thrown from.

  6. Play 3-minute games, then rotate teams.

  • Pass and move to open space.
  • Show a target with your hands.
  • Step and spiral, point to your target.
  • Freeze on the whistle — ball holder freezes too.

  • Three-pass rule before a shot.
  • Score into a hoop on the floor instead of a basket.
  • All players on a team must receive a pass before that team scores twice.

Teaching tips

  • Practise the no-running rule in a short passing drill first so the game does not stop for travelling calls.

Two teams hide hoop-and-pin ships behind a parachute wall over the net and lob soft balls over to knock down the other team's pins

Suggested month: December · also fits May · Slot: 16–25 · Two teams of about 12 · 10–15 minutes · rounds of about two songs

Set-up: Hang the parachute over the net so the two halves cannot see each other. Block the side gaps with mats.

How we play

  1. Split the class into two teams of about 12, one on each side of the wall.
  2. Each team builds three ships with its 9 hoops: one of 4 hoops, one of 3 and one of 2, hoops touching in a line. Stand one pin in each hoop. Ships can go anywhere on that half.
  3. Each team gets 5–6 balls. On “Go”, players lob balls over the wall to try to knock down the other team's pins.
  4. A fallen pin stays down, even if your own team knocked it over.
  5. Get balls on your own side only and throw again.
  6. If a round drags, the teacher acts as “radar” and holds a pool noodle above one ship so the other side knows roughly where to aim.
  7. Play until one fleet is sunk, or for about two songs, then count the pins still standing. Rebuild ships in new spots and play again.

If this happens

  • Someone reaches under or around the parachute: freeze, reset the wall, restart.
  • A throw goes under the wall at players: that player lobs only for the round.

How a round ends. About two songs, or when one fleet is sunk. Count pins still standing, rebuild in new spots, play again.

Cues

  • Step with the opposite foot.
  • High arc to clear the wall.
  • Call “ball” if one lands near you.
  • Freeze on the whistle — balls down.

Variations

  • Mixed shapes (L-shaped ships).
  • Each team gets one “repair” to stand a pin back up.
  • Partner throws only.

Teaching tips

  • Set the net, parachute and side mats before class; it is the longest part of the set-up.
  • Year plan: December, May — a throwing game for games month or before track throwing.

24. Hoop Run & Spin

Locomotor & listen

Grades: 1–6 (est.) · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: 1 hoop per student (or one shared hoop per pair if stock is short); open floor space; music optional

Partners stand facing each other, each holding a hoop upright; on “3-2-1 go” both spin their hoops, swap places and catch the partner’s hoop before it falls; each success = both take a step back to lengthen the run

Suggested month: October · also fits September, May · Slot: 0–5 or 16–25 · Whole class in pairs · one hoop each · 4–8 minutes · freeze on the signal

Set-up: Students find a partner and stand facing each other with enough space to spin a hoop. Each partner holds their own hoop upright (or one hoop if sharing). Clear a few metres of space behind each person for the swap run.

How we play

  1. On GO, both partners spin their hoops on the floor (or give them a gentle spin so they stay upright as long as possible).
  2. As soon as the hoops are spinning, partners run to swap places.
  3. Each person tries to catch their partner’s hoop before it falls flat.
  4. If both catch successfully, take one big step farther apart and try again.
  5. If a hoop falls, reset closer together, practise one calm spin-and-catch, then build distance again.
  6. Freeze on the whistle; new partners optional for the next round.

If this happens

  • Hoops flying or thrown: freeze, remind floor-spin only, sit that pair out one turn as helpers.
  • Pairs crash in the middle: mark two spots with cones and require a run around the outside.

How a round ends. Short bursts on the teacher's signal. End on a calm freeze and one breath. No ranking of pairs.

Cues

  • Spin, then go — do not wait for a perfect hoop.
  • Eyes on your partner’s hoop as you run.
  • Soft hands to catch; two feet ready.
  • Success? One step farther. Miss? Step in and try again.

Variations

  • One shared hoop: partner A spins, both swap, partner B catches, then reverse.
  • Add a locomotor rule for the swap (side-gallop, skip) once catching is reliable.
  • Challenge: how many successful catches in a row before a drop.
  • Grades 5–6: start farther apart or add a 360 turn during the swap.

Teaching tips

  • Practise a still spin-and-catch with no run first, then add the swap.
  • Scatter pairs across the gym so run paths do not cross. House rules: no elimination; a drop means step closer and stay in.
  • Year plan: October, September, May — a quick reaction warm-up any month, especially before invasion or athletics.

25. Pancake Relay

Relays & stations

Grades: 1–6 (est.) · Best fit: Grades 1–2, also good for 3–4

Equipment: Per team: 1 tennis racket (or soft racket), 1 beanbag “pancake”, 1 turn cone about 8–12 m away; pinnies optional

Small teams; each has a tennis racket and a beanbag “pancake”; on signal the first runner balances the beanbag on the racket, travels around the team’s marker cone and back (if it drops, pick it up, replace it and carry on), then carefully hands racket and pancake to the next runner; continue until all have gone

Suggested month: May · also fits October · Slot: 0–5 or 16–25 · Even teams of 4–6 · one lane per team · 8–12 m to a turn cone · 8–12 minutes · two rotations then a quality round

Set-up: Make a lane for each team. Put a turn cone at the far end of each lane. Line each team up behind a start cone. Give the first runner a racket with a beanbag balanced on the face (the pancake).

How we play

  1. On GO, the first runner walks or jogs down the lane with the pancake balanced on the racket, around the turn cone, and back to the start.
  2. If the beanbag falls, the runner stops, places it back on the racket, and continues from that spot — no restart from the beginning.
  3. At the start line they carefully hand the racket and pancake to the next runner (no tossing).
  4. Every teammate goes once. Finish with a quality round where the goal is a smooth hand-off and a pancake that stays on, not a sprint ranking.
  5. Freeze on the whistle between rounds; reset pancakes and swap who goes first.

If this happens

  • Pancakes keep falling: slow everyone to a walk and practise a 5-step balance before the next relay.
  • Early starts: that runner goes back behind the cone and waits for a clear high-five.

How a round ends. Two full rotations, then one quality round focused on balance and calm hand-offs. Celebrate form, not a ranking.

Cues

  • Eyes up, soft knees, racket flat like a plate.
  • If it falls, stop, replace, continue — no panic sprint.
  • Hand off gently at the cone; next runner waits behind the line.
  • Stay in your lane; celebrate a clean hand-off.

Variations

  • Younger grades walk only; older grades may jog once balance looks solid.
  • Use a soft foam racket or a flat board if tennis rackets are scarce.
  • Add a second cone so runners weave, or swap the beanbag for a soft foam ball.
  • Partner version: one holds the racket, the other coaches balance cues from the side of the lane.

Teaching tips

  • Demo a slow lap yourself so the class sees replace-and-continue, not a restart.
  • Mark wide lanes so rackets never cross. House rules: no elimination; a drop is a pause, then back in.
  • Year plan: May, October — athletics balance and a fall fitness relay that also leads into racket skills.

26. Post the Parcel

Relays & stations

Grades: 3–6 (est.) · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: Per team: 1 turn cone, 5 beanbags stacked or placed at that cone, 5 tennis balls (or soft balls) at the start line; pinnies optional

Small teams, each runner holding a tennis ball; a cone 5–10 m away hides five beanbags underneath; one at a time each runner lifts the cone, leaves their tennis ball, takes a beanbag and races back to tag the next; done when every beanbag has been swapped for a tennis ball

Suggested month: May · also fits October · Slot: 0–5 or 16–25 · Even teams of 4–6 · one lane per team · cone 5–10 m away · 8–12 minutes · finish when all beanbags are swapped

Set-up: Make a lane for each team. Place a cone 5–10 m from the start line with five beanbags ready under or beside it (the “parcels” waiting to be collected). Give the first runner one tennis ball. Extra balls stay in a pile at the start for the next runners.

How we play

  1. On GO, the first runner carries a tennis ball to the far cone.
  2. They leave the ball at the cone (post the parcel) and pick up one beanbag.
  3. They sprint back to the start and hand the beanbag to the team pile, then the next runner takes a new tennis ball.
  4. Keep going until all five beanbags have been brought back and five balls sit at the far cone.
  5. When a team finishes, they cheer other teams in. Reset balls and beanbags, then play a second round with a quality focus (smooth place-and-pick, not wild throws).

If this happens

  • Balls get kicked or thrown at the cone: freeze, remind place-down only, restart that leg.
  • Lanes collide at the cone: stagger start times by two seconds or widen lanes.

How a round ends. A team is done when all five beanbags are back and five balls are at the far cone. Celebrate every team; then reset for a quality second round.

Cues

  • Carry, place, pick, sprint — one job at a time.
  • Ball goes down gently at the cone; beanbag comes home.
  • Next runner waits behind the line with the next ball ready.
  • Cheer every team to the finish — no ranking shout-outs.

Variations

  • Fewer beanbags (3) for younger grades or a shorter time.
  • Carry the ball on a racket for a pancake-style link, or dribble a soccer ball instead of carrying.
  • Add a locomotor rule on the way out (skip) and a sprint on the way back.
  • Make the cone farther for grades 5–6 once the place-and-pick is clean.

Teaching tips

  • Count beanbags aloud as they return so teams can self-check.
  • Demo one full leg: place the ball, take one beanbag only, sprint back. House rules: no elimination; early finishers become cheerleaders, not winners who sit out.
  • Year plan: May, October — sprint fitness that fits track month and a sharp fall warm-up.

27. Zone Tag

Tag & chase

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: 8–12 cones or floor markers to mark four zones; pinnies for the four taggers (optional); a whistle or music to start and stop

Cones split the space into four zones with one tagger locked into each; everyone else runs freely across the zones; a tag swaps the tagger and runner; last taggers do a short fitness challenge

Suggested month: October · also fits September · Slot: 0–5 or 16–25 · Whole class · 4 taggers, one per zone · 60–90 second rounds · 6–10 minutes total

Set-up: Use cones or lines to cut the playing area into four zones (think four quarters of a gym floor or field). Pick four taggers and send one to stand in each zone. Everyone else scatters anywhere in the space.

How we play

  1. On the signal, the runners move around freely and may run through any zone.
  2. Each tagger must stay inside their own zone. They can lean and reach, but they may not step across a cone line to chase.
  3. When a tagger gently tags a runner, they swap on the spot: the runner becomes the tagger for that zone, and the old tagger is now a runner and can go anywhere.
  4. Keep playing until the time is up. Freeze on the whistle.
  5. At the end, the four players who are taggers lead a short fitness challenge for the group (for example 10 push-ups, squats, or jumping jacks). Then pick four new taggers and go again.

If this happens

  • A tagger keeps stepping over the line: warning, then swap that tagger out.
  • Same few runners get tagged over and over: add a tagger-swap rule or shorten the round.

How a round ends. Timed rounds (60–90 seconds). The four current taggers lead a quick fitness challenge, then new taggers are chosen. No winner needed.

Cues

  • Taggers: stay in your zone — you can reach, but not cross the line.
  • Runners: look up, cross the zones, change direction.
  • If you get tagged, swap places on the spot and take the next job.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.

Variations

  • Change the way runners travel between rounds: skip, gallop, hop, or backward jog.
  • Add a fifth or sixth zone for a bigger class, with one more tagger for each.
  • Make the zone lines trickier: taggers may only move sideways, or must keep one foot on a spot.
  • Let the class vote on the end-of-round challenge so it feels fun, not like a punishment.

Teaching tips

  • Mark the zones before class so there is no wasted time. Four equal zones keep it fair.
  • Watch the corners where all four zones meet — remind runners to slow down and look before crossing.
  • Choose the end challenge together; a fun leader moment keeps tagged students positive. House rules: soft tags, no elimination, taggers always swap back into play.
  • Year plan: October, September — a fast warm-up before any football or invasion lesson.

28. Mat Tag

Tag & chase

Grades: 2–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: 4 mats (one per base); 2 soft foam balls or soft tags for the taggers (no hard dodgeballs — those stay on the Dodgeball page); pinnies for taggers

Four mats laid out like baseball bases; runners circle the bases and are safe on a mat; two taggers patrol between bases and a runner who is hit becomes a tagger

Suggested month: June · also fits October · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class · 4 mats as bases · 2 taggers (more with larger classes) · 2–3 minute rounds · 8–12 minutes total

Set-up: Lay the four mats out like a baseball diamond, spaced far enough apart for a good run. Spread the class evenly across the bases, with any extras standing between bases. Choose one direction of travel (all runners go the same way). Pick two taggers, give each a soft ball, and send them into the space between bases.

How we play

  1. On the signal, runners move from mat to mat in the chosen direction, one base after the next, round and round the diamond.
  2. A runner standing on a mat is safe. Set a limit of how many can stand on one mat at a time (for example six) so there is always room to move on.
  3. Runners leave a mat when the way is clear and run to the next one. They cannot go backward.
  4. Taggers try to touch a runner who is between mats, or gently toss or roll a soft ball at the runner's legs (below the waist).
  5. A runner who is tagged between mats becomes a tagger, and the tagger who made the tag becomes a runner. Hand the soft ball over, then everyone keeps playing.
  6. Stop at the whistle, change direction or taggers, and play another round.

29. Capture the Flag Relay

Relays & stations

Grades: 1–6 · Best fit: Grades 3–4, also good for 5–6

Equipment: Per team: 1 cone for the team base, 3 hoops (near, middle and far), 15 flags or beanbags in a pile at the far end of the lane, 1 ball. Use foam or soft balls for grades 1–2 and foam footballs for grades 3–6.

Team relay lanes with near, middle and far hoops; a thrower sends the ball to a catcher standing in a hoop; each catch earns a run for a flag or beanbag; the hoop moves farther back after five catches; first team to 15 flags wins

Suggested month: October · also fits September · Slot: 0–5 or 16–25 · Teams of 4–6 · one lane per team · 12–15 minutes · first team to 15 flags

Set-up: Make a lane for each team. At one end, put a cone as the team base and have the team line up behind it. Lay the three hoops in a line in front of the team at near, middle and far distances (for example about 3, 6 and 9 metres). Put the pile of flags or beanbags at the far end of the lane. One player stands in the near hoop as the catcher and the next in line is the first thrower.

How we play

  1. The thrower stands behind the team cone and throws the ball to the catcher standing in the near hoop.
  2. If the catcher catches it, they run out to grab one flag or beanbag and carry it back to the team cone. That is a point for the team. The runner then joins the back of the line.
  3. At the same time the thrower steps in to be the new catcher. The next player in line becomes the new thrower.
  4. If the catch is missed, the thrower fetches the ball and throws again. The catcher stays in the hoop until a catch is made.
  5. After five flags are collected from the near hoop, the catcher moves back to the middle hoop. After five more, they move to the far hoop.
  6. Five more catches from the far hoop make 15 flags. The first team with 15 flags wins; everyone cheers for the other teams to finish.

If this happens

  • The throw is missed several times: move the hoop closer for a few catches, then back.
  • Too many missed catches slow the team: teacher or a student coach gives a quick throwing cue.

How a round ends. First team to bring 15 flags to its cone. Celebrate all teams; then reset the flags and swap roles.

Cues

  • Step, point, throw — to the catcher's chest.
  • Catch with soft hands and a stable stance in the hoop.
  • Run the flag back to the team cone.
  • Cheer on every team, not only your own.

Variations

  • Change the throw: underhand toss, chest pass, or football-style throw.
  • Catchers who step out of the hoop do not count the catch.
  • Make the hoop spots closer for younger students or farther for grades 5–6.
  • Set a target of 10 flags instead of 15 if time is short.

Teaching tips

  • Use real football-style skills in October: pointing the lead hand, stepping and following through.
  • Remind the catcher to keep both feet inside the hoop until the catch is complete.

How a round ends. When the group finishes the task or the time is up. Celebrate the group, then try a harder version.

Cues

  • Complete a cooperative balance or movement challenge on the mat.
  • Everyone must be on the mat for it to count.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Use names. Ask before you grab a hoop, rope, or balloon.
  • If it drops, restart together — no blame.
  • Quiet voices so the group can hear the next job.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Challenge Mat.
