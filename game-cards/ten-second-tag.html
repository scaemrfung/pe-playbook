27. Ten-Second Tag

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class · 4–6 min · new taggers every 10 s.

Send a new small group of taggers in every 10 seconds, and tagged runners do 5 jumping jacks, then keep running. Use it for short run-and-recover bursts so no one is the tagger for long.

Equipment: Centre line of cones, whistle.

Set-up: Split the gym. Taggers on one side, runners on the other. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. On the first whistle, a small group of taggers enters the runner half.
  2. Every 10 seconds, whistle again. Those taggers freeze. A new group goes in.
  3. A tagged runner does 5 jumping jacks on the spot, then keeps running.
  4. Rotate so every student is a tagger once.
  5. Play Ten-Second Tag in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Soft tag.
  • Jacks, then back in.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.

Variations

  • Walk-tag for 1–2.
  • Walk-tag first, then jog. Add a second tagger only when space is clean.
  • Safe islands: three hoops that freeze a tagger for three seconds if a runner reaches one.
  • Tagged players become helpers (peelers, medics, or extra taggers) instead of sitting.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Ten-Second Tag.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Half gym. Whistle starts a tagger group. Next whistle: they freeze, a new group goes.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Ten-Second Tag for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Runs or walks on the whistle.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Ten-Second Tag.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Ten-Second Tag; does not crowd a hospital or jail.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Ten-Second Tag.
  • Safety. Soft tag; tagged player re-enters after 5 jacks.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch, hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk-tag. Teacher plus one student tagger. 30–45 second bursts. Re-enter with a high-five or the game’s hospital rule.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Full speed with two-finger tags. Add one tactic (fake, safe-zone worth one use, or blob split). Students can referee a boundary.

Safety. Soft tag. Taggers wait behind the line until the whistle. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
