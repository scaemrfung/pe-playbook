182. Sneak & React

Locomotor & listen

When: September, May · Slot: 0–5

Tiptoe toward the leader on the sneak cue; react fast on the react cue. Reaction timing, no equipment.

Also called: Knock and Run

Equipment: None

Set-up: Class scattered, facing a leader at one end.

How we play

  1. On the sneak cue, tiptoe forward.
  2. On the react cue (clap, colour, “TURN”), sprint back or change action.
  3. Mix in freeze, plank, jump-turn, partner high-five.
  4. A false start is 3 jumping jacks, then rejoin. Let older students lead the cues.

Variations

  • Knock and Run (shoulder-tap chase): Mark a start line and a far line about 15 m apart, shorter for Grades 1–2. The first player walks to the far line and faces the wall. The next player creeps up quietly and gives one light tap on the shoulder, then turns and sprints back to the start line while the tapped player turns and chases. A two-finger tag on the back before the line means the two swap roles, or the tagged player does a quick stretch, then rejoins. Run through the line and slow down. Both players then walk to the back of a different line.

More details · Grades K-8

Equipment: None

A reaction-timing game for all ages. Use it as an instant activity, a warm-up or a stand-alone game. No equipment needed.

  1. Class faces a leader in scattered formation.
  2. On a sneak cue, students tiptoe forward. On a react cue (clap, colour, “TURN”) they sprint back or change action.
  3. Mix in freeze, plank, jump-turn, partner high-five.
  4. False start = 3 jumping jacks and rejoin. Let older students lead cue sequences.

Source · Video

Grades 1–2. Teacher calls. Two or three commands only. Walk before you jog.
Grades 3–4. Add commands and speed. Let a student call one round.
Grades 5–6. Add fitness moves or a sequence to remember. Students lead.

Safety. Spread out to arm's-length space. Look where you go. Freeze on the whistle.
