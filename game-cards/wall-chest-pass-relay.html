152. Wall chest-pass relay

Relays & stations

When: January · Slot: 16–25 · 1 wall panel per team.

Students run to the hoop, make three chest passes off the wall, then high-five the next runner. Use it to practise the step-and-push chest pass.

Equipment: Hoop, soccer or basketball, wall space.

Set-up: Hoop 2 m from a wall. Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. One runner at a time. Next player starts behind the home cone and goes on a high-five — not a shout from halfway.
  2. Run to the hoop. Three chest-passes off the wall. Ball back in the hoop. High-five.
  3. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  4. Play Wall chest-pass relay in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Thumbs down.
  • Step to the wall.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Catch-bounce for 1–2.
  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Wall chest-pass relay.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: January — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Three chest-passes off the wall. Ball back in the hoop.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Wall chest-pass relay for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Steps and pushes from the chest.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the high-five, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Waits behind the line; high-fives the next runner kindly.
  • Character Development. No extra steps past the line; a drop is picked up and the leg continues.
  • Safety. One team per wall panel.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a drop.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. One bounce-pass in the three.

Safety. Pass on the right. No diving at a hoop, pin, or beanbag. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
