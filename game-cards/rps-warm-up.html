232. RPS Warm-up

Fitness & landings

When: February, April · Slot: 16–25 · Pairs · home lines · no kit

Rock-paper-scissors decides who chases or who takes the object.

Also called: RPS Rounders · Carraig Páipéar Siosúr

Equipment: None · optional cone between pairs

Set-up: Pairs meet at the centre. Home lines behind each.

How we play

  1. Play one RPS on a count of three.
  2. Winner chases loser toward the loser’s home line.
  3. Tag before the line = point to the winner. Safe over the line = point to the runner.
  4. New partner next bout.

If this happens

  • RPS argument: teacher calls it, play on.
  • Push instead of a tag: no point.
  • Need a classroom version: Boom / Clap / Snatch on an object between two seated pupils.

How a round ends. Best of five bouts, or 3–5 minutes.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Quiet landings. Bend ankles, knees, and hips.
  • Quality beats speed. Stop if a wrist, knee, or back complains.
  • Breathe out on the work; do not hold a breath to rush.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Hop hoop-to-hoop until you meet, then RPS (Daly hop version).
  • Cone between pairs; grab on the word ‘cone.’
  • RPS Rounders: winner bats, loser fields a short diamond.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: RPS Rounders; Carraig Páipéar Siosúr.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

More details · Grades 1-8

Equipment: 4 cones

A simple warm-up that becomes an instant activity once students know it. It needs only 4 cones, works almost anywhere, and suits large groups.

  1. Mark a centre meeting strip and two home end lines with 4 cones.
  2. Students pair up and meet at the centre. They play one round of Rock-Paper-Scissors.
  3. Winner chases loser toward the loser’s home line. A tag before the line scores for the winner; reaching the line first scores for the runner.
  4. Both jog back to centre and play again immediately.
  5. After 2–3 minutes, switch partners and repeat.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in the bout for the set time; names breath or warmth after.
  • Movement Skill Development. Matches the called movement (run, skip, jump, freeze).
  • Skill focus. Paces the set so they can finish, not sprint and collapse.
  • Skill focus. Works beside a partner or in a hoop without bumping others out.
  • Character Development. Records a private number honestly, or skips the number and names effort.
  • Safety. Soft landings; freeze on the signal.
  • Healthy Relationships. Lets a classmate have a hoop or spot; no blocking a landing.
Grades 1–2. Half the distance or fewer reps. Knees-down options. Stop if wrists or backs complain.
Grades 3–4. Full lane or full set. Quiet landings. Partner counts.
Grades 5–6. Show three quality reps. Add a half-turn, opposite hand, or a hoop target on the last set.

Safety. Quiet landings. Stop a station if a wrist, knee, or back complains — switch to a walk option. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
