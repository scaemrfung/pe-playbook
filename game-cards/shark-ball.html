126. Shark Ball

Strike & field

When: June · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · 4-8 · 5–8 min rounds.

A minnow kicks the foam ball and runs for the far line while the sharks field the ball and try to tag. Use it as a kickball game mixed with Sharks and Minnows (idea from Kevin Williams).

Equipment: 1 foam ball

Set-up: Mark two safe end lines with a large open “ocean” in the middle. One foam ball only. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Pick 2–4 sharks as fielders / taggers. Everyone else is a minnow / kicker.
  2. A minnow kicks the foam ball and immediately runs toward the far safe line.
  3. Sharks field the ball. With the ball they may tag a runner (ball-in-hand tag or a below-shoulder throw — decide first).
  4. A tagged runner becomes a shark. A runner who reaches the far line is safe and rejoins the kicking line.
  5. Keep the next kicker ready so the game never stops.
  6. Balls come back along the side. Restart on a teacher call, not a scramble through the middle.
  7. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Ready position in the field — feet still, hands out.
  • Hit, then run the path. Do not watch your own hit.
  • Fielders send to the base or hoop, not at a runner.

Variations

  • Tee or a bounced teacher-toss for grades 1–2.
  • Three-pitch maximum, then a run-out anyway so the line keeps moving.
  • Fielders rotate one spot after each batter.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Shark Ball.
  • Watch for a long batting line. Use a continuous format or a 3-pitch max.
  • Year plan: June — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Shark Ball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Strikes or kicks to a space; ready hands on a catch.
  • Skill focus. Knows when to run and when to hold.
  • Skill focus. Takes batting order and a fielding spot; rotates so the same student is not always pitcher.
  • Character Development. Honest out or safe; no arguing a force.
  • Safety. Looks up after contact; throws at a runner stay low.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls ‘mine’ on a fly; helps a runner who is unsure of the next hoop.
Grades 1–2. Tee or a bounced toss. Run to one base or hoop and stay. Teacher fields the first few.
Grades 3–4. Live send (kick, strike, or throw). Three chances, then a run so the line moves.
Grades 5–6. Fielders call ‘mine.’ Rotate the strike job every three batters. Add a force-out at one base.

Safety. No slide tackles, no body checks. The ball is not thrown at a face. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
