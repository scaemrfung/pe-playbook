101. Punt to the line

Target & send

When: January, March · Slot: 16–25 · Sport ball, one lane.

Students drop-kick a football toward a line 15–20 m away and keep kicking until it crosses. Use it to practise the punt.

Equipment: Football or playground ball, a line.

Set-up: 15–20 m to a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. One shooter at a time. Next waits behind the home cone. Fetchers stay to the side of the pin.
  2. Drop and kick before the bounce. Chase. Kick again until it crosses. Run it home.
  3. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  4. Play Punt to the line in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Drop, then kick.
  • Chase your own ball.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.

Variations

  • Place-kick for 1–2.
  • Step back one giant step after each make (grades 3–6).
  • Opposite hand or weaker foot for the last round.
  • Team score is ‘makes in two minutes’ — not a ranking of throwers.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Punt to the line.
  • Watch for fetchers walking through the throwing line. Make them a job to the side.
  • Year plan: January, March — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Kick toward the line. Kick again if needed. Run it home.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Punt to the line for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Drops and kicks before a bounce, or places it.
  • Skill focus. Chooses a target and a force — close and soft, or far and firm — not a wild kick.
  • Skill focus. Waits a turn; collects on the call so the next player can send.
  • Character Development. Records or resets honestly; a miss is another try.
  • Safety. Kicks away from other lanes.
  • Healthy Relationships. Cheers a classmate’s send; does not block someone from a turn.
Grades 1–2. Short throwing line. Underhand or roll is allowed. Teacher fetches with the first group.
Grades 3–4. Standard distance. Opposite-foot step. Fetchers stay to the side.
Grades 5–6. Step back after a make. Weaker hand or weaker foot on the last round. A student official on the line.

Safety. Throwing line is closed until fetchers are to the side. One object in the air per lane. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
