3. Backyard Dog Tag

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · K-4 · 5–8 min rounds.

Each dog guards a small yard while visitors try to sneak in, and steal the bone if you use one, without being tagged. Use it to practise changing speed and safe tagging (idea from Deric Hafer).

Equipment: Cones, 4 foam balls

Set-up: Cone off several small “yards.” Place a foam ball (bone/toy) in each yard if you want a steal object. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Assign one dog per yard. Dogs may act like dogs but stay in or right beside their own yard.
  2. The rest of the class are visitors trying to sneak into a yard (and optionally steal the bone) without being tagged.
  3. A tagged visitor leaves that yard. Optional swap: the tagged player becomes the new dog.
  4. Play 45–90 second rounds and rotate dogs often.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.

Variations

  • Walk-tag first, then jog. Add a second tagger only when space is clean.
  • Safe islands: three hoops that freeze a tagger for three seconds if a runner reaches one.
  • Tagged players become helpers (peelers, medics, or extra taggers) instead of sitting.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Backyard Dog Tag.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Backyard Dog Tag for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Changes speed and pathway to avoid a tag in Backyard Dog Tag.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Backyard Dog Tag.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Backyard Dog Tag; does not crowd a hospital or jail.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Backyard Dog Tag.
  • Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk-tag. Teacher plus one student tagger. 30–45 second bursts. Re-enter with a high-five or the game’s hospital rule.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Full speed with two-finger tags. Add one tactic (fake, safe-zone worth one use, or blob split). Students can referee a boundary.

Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tags on the back or shoulder — never the head. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
