103. Skittle Ball

Target & send

When: January, March · Slot: 16–25 · Standing pins or cones · balls · a fetch line

Knock a pin, then either count it or collect it.

Also called: Skittles · Knock the Pin · Build Your Tower · Pin Knockover · Hockey pin knockdown · Roll to hit the pin

Equipment: Standing pins or cones · balls · a fetch line

Set-up: Pins out. Throwers on a mark. One fetcher at a time.

How we play

  1. Underhand throw or roll at a pin.
  2. Knockdown = 1 point, or collect that pin and stack it at your base.
  3. Fetcher resets or delivers. Next thrower.
  4. Continue until pins are gone or time ends.

If this happens

  • Two pins from one ball: take both only if you have said so.
  • Human wall in front of every pin: send them back to the mark.
  • Tower collapses: rebuild from the fallen pins, no extra collect.

How a round ends. Most pins, tallest tower, or points in a time cap.

Cues

  • One throw, then fetch.
  • Safe at the pin line.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Run a small triangle before the throw (Daly Knock the Pin).
  • Kick the ball (Pin Galore).
  • Build a tower from collected cones.
  • Hockey pin knockdown on hockey days.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Skittles; Knock the Pin; Build Your Tower; Pin Knockover; Pin Galore Soccer; Hockey pin knockdown; Roll to hit the pin.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

More details: Pin Knockover · Grades 2-8

Equipment: Plastic bowling pins, dodgeballs

A target throwing or rolling game for two teams, one on each side of the gym. Each team tries to be first to knock over all of the other team’s pins. Skills include blocking, goaltending, running, and underhand and overhand throwing. Set up the same number of pins on both sides. Mark a line in front of the pins so students cannot guard right behind it. Add the dodgeballs. The first team to knock over all of the other team’s pins wins. A pin a player knocks over on their own side stays down. Play many rounds.

  1. Two teams, each with a row of plastic pins on its end. Foam dodgeballs.
  2. Throw or roll to knock down the other team’s pins. First team to knock all opponent pins wins, or most pins down at time.
  3. Players may block and “goal tend” in front of their own pins. No crossing mid-line if you want a throwing-only game.
  4. Reset pins and play again. Skills: underhand, overhand, blocking, ducking.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Skittle Ball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Underhand or below-shoulder send to a target.
  • Skill focus. Chooses a target and a force — close and soft, or far and firm — not a wild throw.
  • Skill focus. Waits a turn; collects on the call so the next player can send.
  • Character Development. Records or resets honestly; a miss is another try.
  • Safety. Fetchers are not in the throwing lane.
  • Healthy Relationships. Cheers a classmate’s send; does not block someone from a turn.
Grades 1–2. Short throwing line. Underhand or roll is allowed. Teacher fetches with the first group.
Grades 3–4. Standard distance. Opposite-foot step. Fetchers stay to the side.
Grades 5–6. Step back after a make. Weaker hand or weaker foot on the last round. A student official on the line.

Safety. Throwing line is closed until fetchers are to the side. One object in the air per lane. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
