191. Caterpillar Walk

Cooperative & circus

When: December, October · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class or groups of 6–10 · 6–10 minutes · celebrate the group task, not a winner.

Teams of 5–7 move as one line along a cone path, holding waists or sharing hoops. Use it to practise moving together and taking turns as leader.

Equipment: Cones. Optional shared hoop.

Set-up: Teams of 5–7 in a file. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Hold the waist of the person in front, or each hold a hoop between you.
  2. Walk a cone path. If the line breaks, freeze and rejoin.
  3. Trade the leader each lap.
  4. A drop or a tangle is a restart from the last good shape, not a loss.
  5. Play Caterpillar Walk in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Stay together.
  • Freeze if it breaks.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Use names. Ask before you grab a hoop, rope, or balloon.
  • If it drops, restart together — no blame.
  • Quiet voices so the group can hear the next job.

Variations

  • Shoulders for 1–2.
  • Smaller groups of 6 if the whole-class version tangles.
  • Silent round: only gestures, then talk again to plan.
  • Add one extra object (second balloon, extra hoop) when the first task is easy.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Caterpillar Walk.
  • Watch for one student doing all the work. Give every group a ‘must-touch’ rule.
  • Year plan: December, October — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Hold waist or a shared hoop. Walk the cones. New leader each lap.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Caterpillar Walk for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Matches the line’s speed.
  • Skill focus. Plans a simple order or role with teammates before rushing.
  • Skill focus. Rejoins after a break.
  • Character Development. Restarts a drop without blaming a classmate.
  • Safety. Moves at a speed the group can control. Consent for partner shapes — a student may say no.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses names and kind words; waits a turn without pulling equipment away.
Grades 1–2. Small groups of 4–6. Teacher in the group if the task needs an extra pair of hands.
Grades 3–4. Whole-class or groups of 8. Plan for 20 seconds, then try.
Grades 5–6. Add a hoop the line must step through.

Safety. Ask before you pull a hoop, rope, or teammate. Stop if hair, glasses, or a wrist is caught. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
