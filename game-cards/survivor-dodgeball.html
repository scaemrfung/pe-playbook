106. Survivor Dodgeball

Target & send

When: January, March · Slot: 16–25 · Grades 3–6 · short rounds.

A below-the-head hit means the player switches teams or does a 10-count stretch and stays in. A caught throw puts the thrower out. Use it to practise throwing, catching and dodging.

Equipment: Many dodgeballs.

Set-up: Two sides or a circle. Same house rules as Poison Ball. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Throwing line behind a cone. Fetchers wait to the side — never in the throwing lane.
  2. Hit below the head and that player switches teams or does a 10-count stretch and stays.
  3. A catch is a save. The thrower is out.
  4. Start: on GO, take a ball and carry it back to your wall before you throw.
  5. If all the balls end up on one side, the game ends; the side without the balls wins.
  6. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  7. Play Survivor Dodgeball in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Same house rules as Poison Ball.
  • Re-enter after a stretch.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.

Variations

  • Doctor: one teammate may unfreeze with a two-hand touch.
  • Catch = the thrower is out.
  • Step back one giant step after each make (grades 3–6).
  • Opposite hand or weaker foot for the last round.
  • Team score is ‘makes in two minutes’ — not a ranking of throwers.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Survivor Dodgeball.
  • Watch for fetchers walking through the throwing line. Make them a job to the side.
  • Year plan: January, March — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • A hit is 10 jumping jacks, then back in, or join the thrower’s team.
  • Keep 4–6 balls in play. Freeze on the whistle.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Survivor Dodgeball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Dodgeballs.
  • Skill focus. Chooses a target and a force — close and soft, or far and firm — not a wild throw.
  • Skill focus. Waits a turn; collects on the call so the next player can send.
  • Character Development. Records or resets honestly; a miss is another try.
  • Safety. Hits stay low; a hit is a stretch, then back in.
  • Healthy Relationships. Cheers a classmate’s send; does not block someone from a turn.
Grades 1–2. Do not play. Use Clean Your Room with rolls.
Grades 3–4. Standard distance. Opposite-foot step. Fetchers stay to the side.
Grades 5–6. Timed sides. No last-one-standing.

Safety. Dodgeballs. Below the head. No head shots. A hit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
