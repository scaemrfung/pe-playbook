136. Hoop Relay

Relays & stations

When: February · Slot: 16–25 · Lane of hoops · optional ball, stick, or shared hoop

Advance hoop to hoop with the agreed action. Next runner waits for the signal.

Also called: Toe-tap hoop relay · Circle Hoop Pass · Circle Hoop · Hop-the-hoops relay · Rabbit Relay · Hoop Stick · Caterpillar Relay

Equipment: Lane of hoops · optional ball, stick, or shared hoop

Set-up: Hoops in a lane. Show the action once (jump in, pass then jump, stick-push, shared carry, step through).

How we play

  1. Complete the action in hoop 1, then hoop 2, and so on.
  2. Drop or miss = redo that hoop.
  3. Sit or high-five at the end to release the next runner.

If this happens

  • Early start: back to hoop 1.
  • Hands on a no-hands carry: restart that pair.

How a round ends. First team with everyone through.

Cues

  • Taps in the hoop.
  • Switch feet.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Bag must stay in the hoop before you jump in.
  • Hoop pinched between two stomachs, drop it over a cone.
  • Growing caterpillar steps through after each out-and-back.
  • Circle Hoop Pass: pass the hoop down a line without breaking hands.
  • Hop-the-hoops: two-foot jumps only.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Toe-tap hoop relay; Circle Hoop Pass; Circle Hoop; Hop-the-hoops relay; Rabbit Relay; Hoop Stick; Caterpillar Relay.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Toe-tap hoop relay for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Sole stays on the ball for the taps.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the high-five, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Waits behind the line; high-fives the next runner kindly.
  • Character Development. Finishes the taps before running.
  • Safety. Runs in a lane. No diving at the line. Equipment carried, not thrown at people.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a drop.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Skip one way, jog back — or weaker foot/hand home. A student starter on the whistle.

Safety. Pass on the right. No diving at a hoop, pin, or beanbag. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
