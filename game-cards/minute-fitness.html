227. Minute Fitness

Fitness & landings

When: February, April · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · 3-8 · 5–8 min rounds.

Students work for 1 minute at a strong effort, then rest for 20 seconds, for 8–12 rounds. Use it as an interval fitness activity.

Equipment: None (or a variety)

Set-up: Students spread out in personal space. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Optional: put up exercise posters so they can choose movements. Free posters: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1clLqzemKN-USZ7tOMx2oXah6YKJVVBC3/view
  2. Work 1 minute at a strong effort (jumping jacks, squats, high knees, plank, mountain climbers, etc.).
  3. Rest 20 seconds (walk / shake out).
  4. Repeat 8–12 rounds. Students may change exercises each work interval.
  5. Cool down with easy walking and name one exercise that raised heart rate.
  6. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  7. After two rounds, 20 seconds of a named stretch, then one more quality set.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Quiet landings. Bend ankles, knees, and hips.
  • Quality beats speed. Stop if a wrist, knee, or back complains.
  • Breathe out on the work; do not hold a breath to rush.

Variations

  • Half distance or fewer reps for grades 1–2. Quality landings stay the same.
  • Partner count: one moves, one counts out loud, then swap.
  • Last round is ‘show your best three’ — not a race.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Minute Fitness.
  • Watch for sloppy landings when they race. Sit the race, keep the quality set.
  • Year plan: February, April — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in the bout for the set time; names breath or warmth after.
  • Movement Skill Development. Matches the called movement (run, skip, jump, freeze).
  • Skill focus. Paces the set so they can finish, not sprint and collapse.
  • Skill focus. Works beside a partner without bumping others.
  • Character Development. Records a private number honestly, or skips the number and names effort.
  • Safety. Soft landings; freeze on the signal.
  • Healthy Relationships. Lets a classmate have a spot; no blocking a landing.
Grades 1–2. Half the distance or fewer reps. Knees-down options. Stop if wrists or backs complain.
Grades 3–4. Full lane or full set. Quiet landings. Partner counts.
Grades 5–6. Show three quality reps. Add a half-turn, opposite hand, or a hoop target on the last set.

Safety. Quiet landings. Stop a station if a wrist, knee, or back complains — switch to a walk option. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
