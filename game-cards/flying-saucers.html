86. Flying Saucers

Target & send

When: January, March · Slot: 16–25 · Pairs or teams · 6 min · discs or rings.

Students send a disc or a long underhand throw at hoops placed at two or three distances. Use it to practise accuracy and safe fetching.

Equipment: Discs or rings, hoops, cones.

Set-up: Targets at two or three distances. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Throwing line behind a cone. Fetchers wait to the side — never in the throwing lane.
  2. Send a disc at a hoop. Fetch behind the line.
  3. Team count of fair lands.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. Play Flying Saucers in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Release flat.
  • Fetchers wait behind a line.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.

Variations

  • Catch in a scoring hoop (Frisbee Flingers).
  • Opposite-hand sends.
  • Step back one giant step after each make (grades 3–6).
  • Opposite hand or weaker foot for the last round.
  • Team score is ‘makes in two minutes’ — not a ranking of throwers.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Flying Saucers.
  • Watch for fetchers walking through the throwing line. Make them a job to the side.
  • Year plan: January, March — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Send discs at hoop targets. Fetch only after the whistle or from a safe line.
  • Count hoop hits as a team.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Flying Saucers for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Catch, then throw — call it.
  • Skill focus. Chooses a target and a force — close and soft, or far and firm — not a wild throw.
  • Skill focus. Waits a turn; collects on the call so the next player can send.
  • Character Development. Records or resets honestly; a miss is another try.
  • Safety. Sends toward the target, not a face. Walks to collect. Freeze on the whistle.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses a name or ‘mine’ before the send.
Grades 1–2. Short throwing line. Underhand or roll is allowed. Teacher fetches with the first group.
Grades 3–4. Standard distance. Opposite-foot step. Fetchers stay to the side.
Grades 5–6. Step back after a make. Weaker hand or weaker foot on the last round. A student official on the line.

Safety. Throwing line is closed until fetchers are to the side. One object in the air per lane. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
