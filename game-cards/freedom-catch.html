10. Freedom Catch

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · 1-6 · 5–8 min rounds.

Taggers freeze runners, and a teammate frees a frozen player by completing a throw and catch to them. Use it with large groups to practise tagging, throwing and catching together (game by Jennifer Holub).

Also called: Catch and Thaw Tag

Equipment: Cones, foam balls

Set-up: Large coned field. Several taggers; free players have foam balls. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Taggers freeze runners in place with hands ready to catch.
  2. A teammate frees a frozen player by completing a catch to them.
  3. A drop leaves the player frozen (or freezes the thrower — pick one and teach it).
  4. Timed rounds; rotate taggers. Built for 50+ if space is big.
  5. Large coned field.
  6. Several taggers; free players have foam balls.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Catch and Thaw Tag: one thrower carries the only soft thaw ball and cannot be tagged; a catch frees the frozen player, who becomes the new thrower (taggers use a foam stick).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Freedom Catch.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Freedom Catch for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Changes speed and pathway to avoid a tag in Freedom Catch.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Freedom Catch.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Freedom Catch; does not crowd a hospital, jail, or throwing line.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Freedom Catch.
  • Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk-tag. Teacher plus one student tagger. 30–45 second bursts. Re-enter with a high-five or the game’s hospital rule.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Full speed with two-finger tags. Add one tactic (fake, safe-zone worth one use, or blob split). Students can referee a boundary.

Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tags on the back or shoulder — never the head. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
