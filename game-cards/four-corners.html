167. Four Corners

Locomotor & listen

When: September, December · Slot: 16–25 · Four labelled corners · class in the centre

Get to a corner on the call. Everyone stays in the game.

Also called: Four Corners Stay-In

Equipment: Four labelled corners (numbers or colours)

Set-up: Label 1–4 or by colour. Pupils start centre.

How we play

  1. Call a travel style and a corner: ‘Skip to blue.’
  2. Everyone travels. The named corner (or last arrivals, your choice) does 5–8 jumping jacks or a stretch.
  3. Back to centre. New call.

If this happens

  • Sliding into corners: send them back to travel again.
  • Two corners called: they must choose one.
  • Use the last call to line up.

How a round ends. 3–6 minutes, then into the main game.

Cues

  • Walk to a corner.
  • Called corner does a skill, then stays.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Move the way I named (walk, skip, gallop) — not a race.
  • Stop in a balanced shape: two feet, knees soft.
  • Give neighbours a hoop of space.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Numbers.
  • Colours.
  • Maths: even corners only.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Four Corners Stay-In.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Four Corners Stay-In for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Gets to a corner before the count ends.
  • Skill focus. Finds an open pathway on the go signal; does not cut a neighbour in Four Corners Stay-In.
  • Skill focus. Does the skill and stays in.
  • Character Development. Freezes honestly on the signal even if it is fun to keep running.
  • Safety. Heads up, soft landings, freeze on the whistle. No pushing to a line.
  • Healthy Relationships. Moves around people, not through them; helps a classmate who missed the cue.
Grades 1–2. Walk first. Two commands only (go and freeze). Teacher models every shape.
Grades 3–4. Add skip, gallop, and a shape at the freeze. Students may suggest one command.
Grades 5–6. A student DJ or caller for two minutes. Teacher keeps freeze and safety.

Safety. No diving at a corner. First two feet on the spot keep it. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
