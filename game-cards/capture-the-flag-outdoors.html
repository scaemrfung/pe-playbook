41. Capture The Flag Outdoors

Invasion & team

When: June, May · Slot: 16–25 outdoor

The standard Capture the Flag on a big outdoor field: hide your flag, steal theirs, bring it home.

Equipment: 2 flags, cones, a large outdoor area

Set-up: Split a large outdoor area in half. Two teams, one flag each. Teams get about a minute to hide the flag on their own half.

How we play

  1. No guarding right over your flag.
  2. On GO, try to steal the other flag and carry it back across the middle line.
  3. Tagged on the other half: go to jail. A teammate frees you with a tag (see the video for their jail sequence).
  4. Add house rules for boundaries and hiding limits. Watch the video before the first play.

More details · Grades 3-8

Equipment: 2 Flags, large playing area

The standard Capture the Flag game for PE class. Find a large area (field, community centre, playground, bushes or forest) and make 2 teams. Teams take a minute to hide their flag on their half (no peeking), then play. Each team tries to capture the other team’s flag and bring it across the middle line. A player tagged on the other side goes to jail for 2 minutes; the tagger taps the jail (a bench or similar) and then goes back into play. No guarding right over the flag. Add your own rules as needed; the PHYSEDGAMES video shows more detail. Credit: set up by Paul Grosskopf.

  1. Find a large outdoor area and split it in half. Two teams, one flag each.
  2. Give teams about a minute to hide their flag on their own half (no peeking). No “puppy-guarding” the flag.
  3. On GO, teams try to steal the other flag and carry it back across the middle line.
  4. If you are tagged on the opponent’s half, go to jail for 2 minutes. A teammate can free you by tagging the jail object after a tagger taps it — follow the video for the exact jail sequence used at their school.
  5. Add house rules as needed (boundary, flag-hide limits). Watch the official video before the first play.

Source · Video

Grades 1–2. Walk-through first. Small field, one object, teacher referees.
Grades 3–4. Full rules, larger area. Rotate roles every round.
Grades 5–6. Add strategy talk between rounds. Students set house rules.

Safety. Soft two-finger tags on the back or shoulder, never the head. Clear boundary and freeze signal first. Rotate taggers so the same students are not always chasing. Outdoor boundaries you can see; no running near fences or roads.
