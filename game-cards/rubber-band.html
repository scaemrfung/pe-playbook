178. Rubber Band

Locomotor & listen

When: September, December · Slot: 0–5 · Whole class · 4–5 min · a long rope or elastic, or just a circle of hands.

Students travel away from the circle on the first signal and sprint back on the second. Use it to practise locomotor skills and reaction.

Equipment: A centre circle of spots or a centre cone.

Set-up: Class stands on a circle facing out. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. First signal: travel away using the named locomotor.
  2. Second signal: sprint back, jump, and shout.
  3. Repeat with a new locomotor.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. Play Rubber Band in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • The band expands and shrinks on the call.
  • Do not let go.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Move the way I named (walk, skip, gallop) — not a race.
  • Stop in a balanced shape: two feet, knees soft.
  • Give neighbours a hoop of space.

Variations

  • Add a shape in the middle.
  • Whisper round, then a music round. Same skills, different energy.
  • Let a student call two commands (teacher stays on freeze and safety).
  • Add a shape at the freeze: tuck, star, or stork.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Rubber Band.
  • Watch for crashing on a freeze. Practise a walk-freeze before any sprint command.
  • Year plan: September, December — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Class holds a rope circle or joins hands. On ‘stretch,’ step back; on ‘snap,’ step in; on ‘travel,’ the whole band jogs.
  • If the band breaks, freeze, rejoin, continue.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Rubber Band for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Finds a group or a spot without diving.
  • Skill focus. Finds an open pathway on the go signal; does not cut a neighbour in Rubber Band.
  • Skill focus. Leftovers stay in — they join the teacher or the next round.
  • Character Development. Freezes honestly on the signal even if it is fun to keep running.
  • Safety. Heads up, soft landings, freeze on the whistle. No pushing to a line.
  • Healthy Relationships. Moves around people, not through them; helps a classmate who missed the cue.
Grades 1–2. Walk first. Two commands only (go and freeze). Teacher models every shape.
Grades 3–4. Add skip, gallop, and a shape at the freeze. Students may suggest one command.
Grades 5–6. A student DJ or caller for two minutes. Teacher keeps freeze and safety.

Safety. Eyes up on the return. Leave a gap at the circle. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
