131. Crab-bear walk relay

Relays & stations

When: December, February · Slot: 16–25 · Short lanes · 3 rounds.

Students crab walk to the far cone and bear walk home in short lanes. Use it to practise two animal walks in a short bout.

Equipment: Two cones per lane.

Set-up: 8–10 m lanes. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. One runner at a time. Next player starts behind the home cone and goes on a high-five — not a shout from halfway.
  2. Crab walk (belly up) to the far cone. Bear walk (hips up) home. High-five.
  3. Wrists get a stretch after two rounds.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. Play Crab-bear walk relay in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Belly up on the crab.
  • Hips up on the bear.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Knees down if wrists complain.
  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Crab-bear walk relay.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: December, February — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Crab out, bear back, stretch wrists.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Crab-bear walk relay for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Hips stay off the floor on the crab.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the high-five, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Waits behind the line; high-fives the next runner kindly.
  • Character Development. No extra steps past the line; the leg continues to the end.
  • Safety. Stops when wrists complain.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a slow leg.
Grades 1–2. Very short. Knees down if wrists complain.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Switch which animal goes first.

Safety. Hips stay off the floor on the crab. Stop if wrists hurt. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
