21. Shadow Tag

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · Pairs or whole class · 4 min · sun or poly-spot ‘shadows’.

Players tag by stepping on a shadow or a floor spot, never a body. Use it to practise changing speed and pathway.

Equipment: Poly spots if you have no sun.

Set-up: Outdoor shadows, or indoor spots as pretend shadows. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Show the boundary, the freeze signal, and who starts as tagger before GO.
  2. Tag by stepping on a shadow or spot.
  3. Tagged player becomes the tagger, or does 5 jumps and re-enters.
  4. Rotate taggers every 45–90 seconds so the same three students are not chasing all period.
  5. Play Shadow Tag in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Soft feet on the shadow.
  • Switch taggers often.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.

Variations

  • Pairs: stay in your partner’s shadow.
  • Spot-shadows only.
  • Walk-tag first, then jog. Add a second tagger only when space is clean.
  • Safe islands: three hoops that freeze a tagger for three seconds if a runner reaches one.
  • Tagged players become helpers (peelers, medics, or extra taggers) instead of sitting.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Shadow Tag.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Outdoors: tag by stepping on a shadow. Indoors: each player drags a poly-spot on a string or just plays freeze-tag on spots.
  • Soft feet. No stomps on ankles.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Shadow Tag for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Soft feet on a shadow or spot.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Shadow Tag.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Shadow Tag; does not crowd a hospital or jail.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Shadow Tag.
  • Safety. No stomps on ankles.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch, hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk-tag. Teacher plus one student tagger. 30–45 second bursts. Re-enter with a high-five or the game’s hospital rule.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Must tag and say the person’s name.

Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tags on the back or shoulder — never the head. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
