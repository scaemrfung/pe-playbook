25. Survivor Tag

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 0–5 · Whole class · 5 min · 2–3 taggers.

Tagged players do a named skill, such as 5 jumps or a stretch, then re-enter. Use it to practise safe tagging with no elimination.

Equipment: 2–3 pinnies.

Set-up: Choose taggers. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Show the boundary, the freeze signal, and who starts as tagger before GO.
  2. Tagged players do a named skill (5 jumps or a stretch) then re-enter.
  3. Change taggers each minute.
  4. Rotate taggers every 45–90 seconds so the same three students are not chasing all period.
  5. Play Survivor Tag in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • A tag is a skill, then you are back in.
  • Change taggers each minute.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.

Variations

  • Skill is a balance until a helper taps.
  • Two skills on the second tag.
  • Walk-tag first, then jog. Add a second tagger only when space is clean.
  • Safe islands: three hoops that freeze a tagger for three seconds if a runner reaches one.
  • Tagged players become helpers (peelers, medics, or extra taggers) instead of sitting.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Survivor Tag.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Tagged players do a named skill (5 jumps, a stretch, a log roll on a mat) then re-enter.
  • Do not play last-survivor. The game is the movement.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Survivor Tag for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. A tag is a short skill, then back in.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Survivor Tag.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Survivor Tag; does not crowd a hospital or jail.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Survivor Tag.
  • Safety. Soft tag.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch, hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk-tag. Teacher plus one student tagger. 30–45 second bursts. Re-enter with a high-five or the game’s hospital rule.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Skill is a skip-30 or a peer stretch.

Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tags on the back or shoulder — never the head. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
