127. Strikeball

Strike & field

When: June · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · K-8 · 5–8 min rounds.

In a circle, players strike or roll the ball to send it between another player’s legs while that player tries to stop it. Use it to practise striking, goaltending and reaction time.

Equipment: Ball

Set-up: Players stand in a circle, feet wide, one ball in play. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. The player with the ball strikes / rolls it, trying to send it between another player’s legs.
  2. That player tries to stop it with hands or by closing the gap (agree the legal save before you start).
  3. A ball that goes through scores a point for the striker, or the defender does a quick exercise — pick one.
  4. Keep the ball moving. Works in several circles for a large class.
  5. Balls come back along the side. Restart on a teacher call, not a scramble through the middle.
  6. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Ready position in the field — feet still, hands out.
  • Hit, then run the path. Do not watch your own hit.
  • Fielders send to the base or hoop, not at a runner.

Variations

  • Tee or a bounced teacher-toss for grades 1–2.
  • Three-pitch maximum, then a run-out anyway so the line keeps moving.
  • Fielders rotate one spot after each batter.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Strikeball.
  • Watch for a long batting line. Use a continuous format or a 3-pitch max.
  • Year plan: June — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

More details · Grades K-8

Equipment: Ball

A circle game for goaltending, fielding, striking and reaction time. Groups of 4–8 stand in a circle, each player’s feet touching the feet of the players beside them. Give each group a ball. Keeping the ball inside the circle, players strike it with a hand and try to send it between another player’s legs. Players may stop the ball with their hands or arms but may not close their legs. The player scored on gets the ball. Play for a set time or score.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Strikeball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Strikes or rolls to a gap; ready hands on a stop.
  • Skill focus. Knows when to strike and when to close the gap.
  • Skill focus. Takes a turn with the ball; passes it on so everyone strikes.
  • Character Development. Honest about a score or a stop; no arguing a save.
  • Safety. Looks up after contact; the ball stays away from faces.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls a name before sending; helps a classmate who is unsure of the legal save.
Grades 1–2. Tee or a bounced toss. Run to one base or hoop and stay. Teacher fields the first few.
Grades 3–4. Live send (kick, strike, or throw). Three chances, then a run so the line moves.
Grades 5–6. Fielders call ‘mine.’ Rotate the strike job every three batters. Add a force-out at one base.

Safety. The ball is not thrown at a face. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
