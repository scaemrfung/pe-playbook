163. Elements Sprint

Locomotor & listen

When: September, December · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class in open space · 4–8 minutes · freeze on the signal, then the next command.

Call Fire, Earth or Water, or an animal that belongs there, and students run to the matching wall. Use it to practise listening and a quick sprint.

Equipment: Three wall signs: Fire, Earth, Water.

Set-up: Students in the middle. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Call “Fire,” “Earth,” or “Water,” or an animal that belongs there (fish → water).
  2. Last group to the wall does 5 jumping jacks and stays in.
  3. Students may take a turn as the caller.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. If you miss a signal, skip the next one and rejoin — no extra laps as punishment.

Cues

  • Hear the word.
  • Hands up at the wall.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Move the way I named (walk, skip, gallop) — not a race.
  • Stop in a balanced shape: two feet, knees soft.
  • Give neighbours a hoop of space.

Variations

  • Two walls for 1–2.
  • Whisper round, then a music round. Same skills, different energy.
  • Let a student call two commands (teacher stays on freeze and safety).
  • Add a shape at the freeze: tuck, star, or stork.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Elements Sprint.
  • Watch for crashing on a freeze. Practise a walk-freeze before any sprint command.
  • Year plan: September, December — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Fire, Earth, Water — or an animal. Late group: 5 jacks, stay in.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Elements Sprint for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Changes direction on the call.
  • Skill focus. Finds an open pathway on the go signal; does not cut a neighbour in Elements Sprint.
  • Skill focus. Listens with the group; nobody is ever out.
  • Character Development. Freezes honestly on the signal even if it is fun to keep running.
  • Safety. Stops with hands up.
  • Healthy Relationships. Moves around people, not through them; helps a classmate who missed the cue.
Grades 1–2. Walk first. Two commands only (go and freeze). Teacher models every shape.
Grades 3–4. Add skip, gallop, and a shape at the freeze. Students may suggest one command.
Grades 5–6. Add “air” as the centre freeze.

Safety. Give a hoop of space. Freeze means still feet — not a last-second shove. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
