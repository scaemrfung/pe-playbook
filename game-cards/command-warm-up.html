160. Command Warm-Up

Locomotor & listen

When: February, April · Slot: 16–25 · Scatter space · no kit · a short word list

Hear the word. Do that shape or travel. Mix the order.

Also called: Red Line · The Video Camera Game · Video Camera · Captain’s Deck / Shipwreck · Shipwreck · Signals / shapes · Laps and Lines / Signals

Equipment: None · optional music

Set-up: Teach 4–6 commands before you speed up.

How we play

  1. Call a command for about 5 seconds.
  2. Call the next. Do not pattern them.
  3. A miss = skip the next command and rejoin. No extra laps.

If this happens

  • Space collisions: smaller shapes, more room.
  • Need a pulse-raiser: add music and travel the commands.

How a round ends. 2–4 minutes, then the main game.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Quiet landings. Bend ankles, knees, and hips.
  • Quality beats speed. Stop if a wrist, knee, or back complains.
  • Breathe out on the work; do not hold a breath to rush.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Winter shapes.
  • Traffic speeds and red light (Rush Hour / Red Line).
  • Ship deck commands (Captain’s Deck / Shipwreck).
  • Video Camera: freeze in the pose that was called.
  • Signals / Laps and Lines: named gym lines.
  • Student caller.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Rush Hour; Red Line; The Video Camera Game; Video Camera; Captain’s Deck / Shipwreck; Shipwreck; Signals / shapes; Laps and Lines / Signals.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

More details: The Video Camera Game · Grades K-5

Equipment: None

A simple warm-up. Call video camera actions, and students do the matching movement: play – walk; rewind – travel backwards; pause – jump; fast forward – run; stop – stop moving; slow motion – move slowly; delete – lie face down on the floor. Call them in random order. After a while, let student leaders make their own sequences and invent new actions.

  1. Teach the camera words and actions, for example: PLAY = jog forward; STOP = freeze; REWIND = run backward; FAST FORWARD = sprint; PAUSE = freeze in a pose; SLOW MOTION = exaggerated slow move; RECORD = wave an imaginary camera.
  2. Caller mixes the words. Students perform the matching action in the playing area.
  3. After a minute, switch callers so students invent sequences.
  4. Use as a K–5 warm-up.

More details: Red Line · Grades K-6

Equipment: Cones, balls

A commands-and-directions game with a race element. It can be used at most grade levels. Idea credit: James Barton.

  1. Use court lines or cone several parallel lines. Students start on a baseline.
  2. Teach a small set of commands (example: “red line,” “back,” “spin,” “ball”).
  3. Call commands in mixed order. Students race to the correct line or perform the action.
  4. Add a team race: first team to finish a sequence correctly scores.
  5. For older students add two-part commands or a fitness hold on arrival.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in the bout for the set time; names breath or warmth after.
  • Movement Skill Development. Matches the called movement (run, skip, jump, freeze).
  • Skill focus. Paces the set so they can finish, not sprint and collapse.
  • Skill focus. Works beside a partner without bumping others.
  • Character Development. Records a private number honestly, or skips the number and names effort.
  • Safety. Soft landings; freeze on the signal.
  • Healthy Relationships. Lets a classmate have a spot; no blocking a turn.
Grades 1–2. Half the distance or fewer reps. Knees-down options. Stop if wrists or backs complain.
Grades 3–4. Full lane or full set. Quiet landings. Partner counts.
Grades 5–6. Show three quality reps. Add a half-turn, opposite hand, or a hoop target on the last set.

Safety. Quiet landings. Stop a station if a wrist, knee, or back complains — switch to a walk option. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
