70. Traffic Lights (kick)

Invasion & team

When: October, January · Slot: 5–16 or 0–5 · Whole class · 4–5 min · one sport ball each if you have them.

Students dribble on green, trap on yellow and stop with a foot on the ball on red. Use it to practise ball control and listening.

Equipment: Soccer balls, one per student if you can.

Set-up: Teach green / yellow / red with the sole trap. Pinnies on one team. Show the two attacking directions before GO. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Name the two jobs (attack / defend or the two end-lines) and which ball we are using.
  2. Green: dribble. Yellow: trap and wait. Red: stop, foot on the ball.
  3. Restart on green.
  4. Play 4–6 minute periods. Extra players are bumpers, walls, or the next wave — they stay in a job.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Green dribble, yellow trap, red sole-stop.
  • Eyes up.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Head up. Pass or move — do not crowd the ball.
  • Find a passing lane, not a pile-up.
  • Soft send; the ball stays below the chest unless the sport needs a high set.

Variations

  • Add blue = change direction.
  • No balls: locomotor only.
  • Must complete one pass before a shot or a score.
  • Add a second foam ball if more than 24 students so more players get a touch.
  • Extra players are walking walls — they may deflect a ball with feet but may not score.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Traffic Lights (kick).
  • Watch for one student holding the ball. Cue a pass or a 3-second pivot.
  • Year plan: October, January — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Scatter. Each student with a sport ball, or share 1:2.
  • Call colours. Add weak-foot on a second round.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Traffic Lights (kick) for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Shows the named locomotor or shape.
  • Skill focus. Spreads out, passes then moves, or defends a line — one tactic, not a crowd around the ball.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role (attacker, defender, wall, sideline) and rotates without fuss.
  • Character Development. Honest score; tries again after a miss.
  • Safety. Changes movement on the first signal.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls a teammate’s name; includes a player who has not touched the ball.
Grades 1–2. Walk-dribble. Hands may help a trap.
Grades 3–4. Full game as written, foam or real sport ball as the unit says. One pass before a score if the class crowds.
Grades 5–6. Student captains or officials. Offside-lite or a must-pass rule. Keep score without ranking classmates.

Safety. No slide tackles, no body checks. The ball is not thrown at a face. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
