123. Longball

Strike & field

When: June · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · 4-8 · 5–8 min rounds.

Play this kickball-style game with foam balls, or a foam ball and plastic bat, and extra running rules so more players move more often. Use it to practise kicking, running and fielding.

Equipment: 2 mats, indoor soccer balls or Softex foam balls

Set-up: Two mats as far bases / safe zones. Indoor soccer balls or Softex foam balls (or a foam ball + plastic bat). Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Kicker puts the ball in play and runs. Extra movement rules keep more people running than classic kickball (watch the video for the exact running pattern they use).
  2. Fielders play the ball to stop the run. Use a tag-with-ball or throw-to-mat out, not a hard hit.
  3. Kick the next ball quickly so the field never waits. Switch sides after a time cap rather than 3 outs if you want more activity.
  4. Balls come back along the side. Restart on a teacher call, not a scramble through the middle.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Ready position in the field — feet still, hands out.
  • Hit, then run the path. Do not watch your own hit.
  • Fielders send to the base or hoop, not at a runner.
  • Small touches. Foot stays near the ball.

Variations

  • Tee or a bounced teacher-toss for grades 1–2.
  • Three-pitch maximum, then a run-out anyway so the line keeps moving.
  • Fielders rotate one spot after each batter.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Longball.
  • Watch for a long batting line. Use a continuous format or a 3-pitch max.
  • Year plan: June — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Longball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Strikes or kicks to a space; ready hands on a catch.
  • Skill focus. Knows when to run and when to hold.
  • Skill focus. Takes batting order and a fielding spot; rotates so the same student is not always pitcher.
  • Character Development. Honest out or safe; no arguing a force.
  • Safety. Looks up after contact; tags with the ball or throws to the mat, not a hard hit.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls ‘mine’ on a fly; helps a runner who is unsure of the next base.
Grades 1–2. Tee or a bounced toss. Run to one base or hoop and stay. Teacher fields the first few.
Grades 3–4. Live send (kick, strike, or throw). Three chances, then a run so the line moves.
Grades 5–6. Fielders call ‘mine.’ Rotate the strike job every three batters. Add a force-out at one base.

Safety. No slide tackles, no body checks. The ball is not thrown at a face. Walk on and off mats. No diving onto a house mat. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
