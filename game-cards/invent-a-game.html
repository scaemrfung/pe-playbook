200. Invent-a-game

Cooperative & circus

When: December, October · Slot: 16–25 · Groups of 4 · 15–20 min across a lesson.

Groups of four write three fair rules for a new game and teach it to the class. Use it to practise co-operation and fair play.

Equipment: Whatever the group chooses from a teacher-approved pile.

Set-up: Groups of 4. One clipboard or whiteboard. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Write: how to start, how to score or tag, how to stay safe and included.
  2. Teach in 60 seconds. Class plays for 4 minutes.
  3. Ask: did everyone get a turn?
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. A drop or a tangle is a restart from the last good shape, not a loss.

Cues

  • Start, score, safety — three sentences.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Use names. Ask before you grab a hoop, rope, or balloon.
  • If it drops, restart together — no blame.
  • Quiet voices so the group can hear the next job.

Variations

  • Must include a send.
  • Must include a freeze.
  • Smaller groups of 6 if the whole-class version tangles.
  • Silent round: only gestures, then talk again to plan.
  • Add one extra object (second balloon, extra hoop) when the first task is easy.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Invent-a-game.
  • Watch for one student doing all the work. Give every group a ‘must-touch’ rule.
  • Year plan: December, October — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Give each group a sport ball and 4 cones. They invent a game with a start, a way to score, and one safety rule.
  • Play-test for 2 minutes, change one rule, then teach another group.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Invent-a-game for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the needed skill (pass, step-through, carry, balance) so the group can finish.
  • Skill focus. Can say start, score, and safety in three sentences.
  • Skill focus. Takes turns and solves a small problem without grabbing.
  • Character Development. Teaches those three things to another group in a short turn.
  • Safety. Moves at a speed the group can control. Consent for partner shapes — a student may say no.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses names and kind words; waits a turn without pulling equipment away.
Grades 1–2. Small groups of 4–6. Teacher in the group if the task needs an extra pair of hands.
Grades 3–4. Whole-class or groups of 8. Plan for 20 seconds, then try.
Grades 5–6. Add an equipment rule and a referee.

Safety. Teacher vetoes any elimination or hard-throw rule. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
