153. Zig-zag touch relay

Relays & stations

When: December, February · Slot: 16–25 · Teams of 4–6 · 4–6 min.

Students touch each cone in a zig-zag, take one beanbag from the far hoop and high-five the next runner. Use it to practise changing direction.

Equipment: 5 cones and a hoop of beanbags per team.

Set-up: Zig-zag cones, hoop at the far end. Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. One runner at a time. Touch each cone. Take one beanbag from the hoop. High-five the next.
  2. First team with everyone home and sitting wins the round — then play again for quality.
  3. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  4. Play Zig-zag touch relay in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Touch each cone.
  • High-five, then go.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Walk for 1–2. Skip for 5–6.
  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Zig-zag touch relay.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: December, February — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • One runner. Zig-zag. Beanbag from the last hoop. Sit when your team is done.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Zig-zag touch relay for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Changes direction without cutting the cones.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the high-five, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Waits for the high-five.
  • Character Development. No extra steps past the line; a drop is picked up and the leg continues.
  • Safety. Runs in a lane. No diving at the line. Equipment carried, not thrown at people.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a drop.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Skip one way, jog back — or weaker foot/hand home. A student starter on the whistle.

Safety. Pass on the right. No diving at the hoop. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
