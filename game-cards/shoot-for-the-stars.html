234. Shoot for the Stars

Fitness & landings

When: January, May · Slot: 8-15 · Daly Exercise · slot 8-15.

One rolls at a cone. Partner works behind it. Then swap. Daly Exercise game. Nobody sits the period.

Also called: Daly Exercise: Shoot for the Stars

Equipment: One ball and one cone per pair

Set-up: Cone in the middle. Roller faces it. Partner behind it marches or jacks.

How we play

  1. Roll along the floor at the cone.
  2. Hit = 1 and swap.
  3. Miss = retrieve and try again.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset kit, then the next round.

If this happens

  • Kit knocks a person: pause, widen space, restart.
  • Argument over a point: teacher call, play on.
  • Someone sits out after a miss: they rejoin on the next turn with a short task instead.

How a round ends. Play 3–6 minutes, or first team/player to the posted target, then rotate jobs.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • One job at a time. Extra players fetch or count.
  • A miss is a short task, then back in.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • March behind the cone.
  • Jumping jacks. Swap after every hit.
  • Two-cone gate.

Teaching tips

  • Source: Daly Exercise. Same house rules as the rest of the playbook.
  • Watch for crowding at one piece of kit. Add a second station before you add speed.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Shoot for the Stars for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the taught action in Shoot for the Stars with control, not a rush that knocks kit or people.
  • Skill focus. Chooses space, timing, or a partner job in Shoot for the Stars instead of crowding.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares kit in Shoot for the Stars.
  • Character Development. Honest about a miss in Shoot for the Stars.
  • Safety. Freeze on the whistle. No throws at a face.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a classmate try again after a miss.
