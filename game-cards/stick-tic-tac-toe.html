236. Stick Tic-Tac-Toe

Fitness & landings

When: April · Slot: 16-25 · Daly Exercise · slot 16-25.

A stuck landing claims the hoop. Three in a row wins. Daly Exercise game. Nobody sits the period.

Also called: Daly Exercise: Stick Tic-Tac-Toe

Equipment: Nine hoops in a 3x3; agreed shapes

Set-up: Agree legal shapes. One player in the grid at a time.

How we play

  1. Perform the shape and stick the landing in a free hoop.
  2. That hoop is yours. Other team plays.
  3. Three in a row or the grid is full.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset kit, then the next round.

If this happens

  • Kit knocks a person: pause, widen space, restart.
  • Argument over a point: teacher call, play on.
  • Someone sits out after a miss: they rejoin on the next turn with a short task instead.

How a round ends. Play 3–6 minutes, or first team/player to the posted target, then rotate jobs.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • One job at a time. Extra players fetch or count.
  • A miss is a short task, then back in.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Step-in counts. Teacher names the shape.
  • Landing must stick.
  • Teacher calls the shape just before they jump.

Teaching tips

  • Source: Daly Exercise. Same house rules as the rest of the playbook.
  • Watch for crowding at one piece of kit. Add a second station before you add speed.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Stick Tic-Tac-Toe for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the taught action in Stick Tic-Tac-Toe with control, not a rush that knocks kit or people.
  • Skill focus. Chooses space, timing, or a partner job in Stick Tic-Tac-Toe instead of crowding.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares kit in Stick Tic-Tac-Toe.
  • Character Development. Honest about a miss in Stick Tic-Tac-Toe.
  • Safety. Freeze on the whistle.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a classmate try again after a miss.
