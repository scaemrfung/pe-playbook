208. Voice Path

Cooperative & circus

When: December, October · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class or groups of 6–10 · 6–10 minutes · celebrate the group task, not a winner.

A caller uses only words to guide a partner along a path, then the partners swap. Use it to practise giving and following clear directions.

Equipment: Scattered cones. No benches.

Set-up: Pairs. Walker on one end, caller on the side. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Caller uses only words: “two steps left, stop, one step right.”
  2. Walker looks at the floor. Eyes-closed is optional and only if that student says yes.
  3. Swap roles at the far line. A bump on a cone is a pause, not a fail.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. A drop or a tangle is a restart from the last good shape, not a loss.

Cues

  • Words only.
  • Eyes-closed is a choice.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Use names. Ask before you grab a hoop, rope, or balloon.
  • If it drops, restart together — no blame.
  • Quiet voices so the group can hear the next job.

Variations

  • Eyes on the floor for most classes.
  • Smaller groups of 6 if the whole-class version tangles.
  • Silent round: only gestures, then talk again to plan.
  • Add one extra object (second balloon, extra hoop) when the first task is easy.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Voice Path.
  • Watch for one student doing all the work. Give every group a ‘must-touch’ rule.
  • Year plan: December, October — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Caller on the side. Walker to the far line. Swap.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Voice Path for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the needed skill (pass, step-through, carry, balance) so the group can finish.
  • Skill focus. Plans a simple order or role with teammates before rushing.
  • Skill focus. Takes turns and solves a small problem without grabbing.
  • Character Development. Restarts a drop without blaming a classmate.
  • Safety. No run; consent for eyes-closed.
  • Healthy Relationships. Gives short, kind directions.
Grades 1–2. Small groups of 4–6. Teacher in the group if the task needs an extra pair of hands.
Grades 3–4. Whole-class or groups of 8. Plan for 20 seconds, then try.
Grades 5–6. Optional eyes-closed with consent.

Safety. No running. Caller stays on the perimeter. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
