145. Rescue Relay

Relays & stations

When: December, February · Slot: 16–25 · Teams of 4–5 · 6 min.

A runner crosses, takes a waiting teammate by the hand and brings them home, and the rescued player goes next. Use it to practise running and teamwork.

Equipment: None, or a beanbag to carry.

Set-up: Relay teams. Half the team on the far line. Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. First runner crosses, takes a waiting teammate by the hand, and both return.
  2. The rescued player then becomes the next rescuer.
  3. Continue until the whole team is home.
  4. High-five the next runner at home. First team sitting quietly after two rotations earns a cheer, then play a quality round.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Go get one teammate and bring them home.
  • Two-hand join or a hoop for two.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Must crab the last rescue.
  • Hoop trains only.
  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Rescue Relay.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: December, February — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • One runner brings the next teammate back (joined hands or sharing a hoop).
  • Then those two rescue the third, and so on.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Rescue Relay for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Waits on the far line for a teammate’s hand.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the hand-hold, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Hands off — does not throw at a teammate.
  • Character Development. No extra steps past the line; a drop is picked up and the leg continues.
  • Safety. Runs in a lane. No diving at the line. Equipment carried, not thrown at people.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a drop.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Silent rescue or weak-hand hold.

Safety. Hands only, no pulling a teammate off their feet. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
