221. Jump The River

Fitness & landings

When: April, May · Slot: 0–5 or 16–25

Two ropes are the river banks. Jump across, and the river gets wider after each full-class success.

Equipment: 2 skipping ropes (or court lines)

Set-up: Lay two ropes parallel as river banks, starting close together.

How we play

  1. Jump the river: two-foot take-off and landing, or a running long jump for older grades.
  2. After the whole class gets across, widen the ropes a little.
  3. Touching a rope or the “water” means a quick swim motion or 5 bank jumps, then try again — nobody is out.
  4. Vary the approach: hop, jump-turn, partner jump.

More details · Grades K-8

Equipment: 2 skipping ropes

A simple jumping activity, useful for long jump practice. Idea credit: Deric Hafer.

  1. Lay two jump ropes parallel as river banks, starting close together.
  2. Students jump the river (two-foot takeoff and landing, or a running long jump for older grades).
  3. After a successful full-class pass, widen the ropes a little.
  4. Touching a rope or the “water” means a quick swim motion or 5 bank jumps, then try again — do not eliminate anyone.
  5. Vary the approach: hop, jump-turn, partner jump.

Source · Video

Grades 1–2. Short distance, two-foot take-off, focus on a quiet landing.
Grades 3–4. Widen the challenge a little after the whole class succeeds.
Grades 5–6. Add a run-up or a one-foot take-off. Measure against your own best.

Safety. Land on two feet with bent knees. One jumper per lane; wait until the landing area is clear.
