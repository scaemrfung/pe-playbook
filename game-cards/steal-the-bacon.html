64. Steal the Bacon

Invasion & team

When: October, January · Slot: 16–25 · Two numbered lines · one object in the middle

Your number is called. Beat the other player to the object and get home.

Also called: Oyster Shell · Sneaky Races · Reaction Speed

Equipment: One object in the middle · two numbered lines

Set-up: Two teams on opposite lines, same numbers. Object centre.

How we play

  1. Call a number (or two). Those players race the object.
  2. First hand on it tries to get back over their line. The other may tag.
  3. Soft tag only. Then reset.

If this happens

  • False start: send them back.
  • Two numbers collide: dead ball, replay.
  • Object kicked away: replace and redo.

How a round ends. First team to 5 clean returns, or play a set list of numbers.

Cues

  • Grab the object, not the person.
  • Soft tag the carrier.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Head up. Pass or move — do not crowd the ball.
  • Find a passing lane, not a pile-up.
  • Soft send; the ball stays below the chest unless the sport needs a high set.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Oyster: one beanbag on the centre line.
  • Sneak & React: tiptoe on ‘sneak,’ sprint home on the clap.
  • Drop-catch at chin height instead of a sprint.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Oyster Shell; Sneak & React; Sneaky Races; Reaction Speed.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

More details: Oyster Shell · Grades 2-8

Equipment: 1 beanbag, floor lines (or cones)

A reaction and running game for all ages that needs little equipment. Split the group into 2 teams (they do not need to be even). With cones, you can play it outside too.

  1. Two teams on opposite sidelines facing a centre line. Teams do not need even numbers.
  2. Place one beanbag on the centre line (the oyster).
  3. Number the players on each side. Call a number; those two sprint to the beanbag.
  4. Whoever grabs it tries to run it home across their sideline. The other player tries to tag them.
  5. 1 point for a successful return OR 1 point for a tag. Reset and call a new number. Call two numbers for 2-v-2.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Steal the Bacon for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Reacts on the number.
  • Skill focus. Spreads out or defends a line — one tactic, not a crowd around the object.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role (attacker, defender, wall, sideline) and rotates without fuss.
  • Character Development. Honest score and honest tags; tries again after a miss.
  • Safety. Tags the carrier, does not grab clothes.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls a teammate’s name; includes a player who has not touched the object.
Grades 1–2. Small space, big targets, walking or jogging. Teacher may be a walking bumper. Hands may help a trap if the ball is soccer.
Grades 3–4. Full game as written, foam or real sport ball as the unit says. One pass before a score if the class crowds.
Grades 5–6. Student captains or officials. Offside-lite or a must-pass rule. Keep score without ranking classmates.

Safety. No slide tackles, no body checks. The ball is not thrown at a face. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
