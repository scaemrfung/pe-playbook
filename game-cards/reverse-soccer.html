58. Reverse Soccer

Invasion & team

When: September · Slot: 16–25 · Two teams · 6–8 min · soccer ball, two goals.

Field players may only tap or pass with their hands, and keepers save with their feet. Use it as an invasion game that flips the usual soccer rules.

Equipment: Soccer ball, pinnies, two cone goals.

Set-up: Two teams. One keeper each. Goals can face out if you want a harder angle. Pinnies on one team. Show the two attacking directions before GO. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Field players may only tap or pass with hands — no carrying.
  2. Keepers save with feet only.
  3. Score in the other team’s goal. Rotate keepers every 2 minutes.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. Play Reverse Soccer in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Hands tap or pass — no carry.
  • Keepers use feet only.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Head up. Pass or move — do not crowd the ball.
  • Find a passing lane, not a pile-up.
  • Soft send; the ball stays below the chest unless the sport needs a high set.

Variations

  • Everyone touches before a shot.
  • Walking only.
  • Must complete one pass before a shot or a score.
  • Add a second foam ball if more than 24 students so more players get a touch.
  • Extra players are walking walls — they may deflect a ball with feet but may not score.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Reverse Soccer.
  • Watch for one student holding the ball. Cue a pass or a 3-second pivot.
  • Year plan: September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Field players may only send with hands (volleyball tap or two-hand pass). They cannot run with the ball.
  • Keepers save with feet. Rotate keepers every 2 minutes.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Reverse Soccer for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Ball stays close; trap or dribble under control.
  • Skill focus. One ball at a time; looks before sending.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role (attacker, defender, wall, sideline) and rotates without fuss.
  • Character Development. Honest score; tries again after a miss.
  • Safety. Heads up. No slide tackles or body checks. Equipment stays below the agreed height.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls a teammate’s name; includes a player who has not touched the ball.
Grades 1–2. Skip or play walking hand-ball.
Grades 3–4. Must pass once before a shot.
Grades 5–6. Everyone on the team touches before a shot.

Safety. No slide tackles, no body checks. The ball is not thrown at a face. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
