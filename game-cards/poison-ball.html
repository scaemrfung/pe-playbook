96. Poison Ball

Target & send

When: January, March · Slot: 16–25 · Grades 3–6 · 6 min · 6–10 dodgeballs.

Throwers outside the circle aim below the waist, and a hit dodger switches places with the thrower. Use it to practise throwing and dodging with nobody out for the game.

Equipment: Dodgeballs, a cone circle.

Set-up: 6–8 dodgers inside. Others throw from outside the circle. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Throws stay below the waist.
  2. A hit = switch places with that thrower.
  3. A catch = the thrower comes in and the catcher throws.
  4. Balls come back along the side. Restart on a teacher call, not a scramble through the middle.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Below the waist or a two-hand catch is safe.
  • A hit walks you to the other team — you stay in the game.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.

Variations

  • 4-corner dodge.
  • Must bounce the ball first.
  • Step back one giant step after each make (grades 3–6).
  • Opposite hand or weaker foot for the last round.
  • Team score is ‘makes in two minutes’ — not a ranking of throwers.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Poison Ball.
  • Watch for fetchers walking through the throwing line. Make them a job to the side.
  • Year plan: January, March — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Four teams or two. Dodgeballs.
  • A below-waist hit sends you to the team that threw it (or to a sideline stretch, then back in).

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Poison Ball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Dodgeballs.
  • Skill focus. Chooses a target and a force — close and soft, or far and firm — not a wild throw.
  • Skill focus. Waits a turn; collects on the call so the next player can send.
  • Character Development. Records or resets honestly; a miss is another try.
  • Safety. Hits stay low; a hit is a stretch, then back in.
  • Healthy Relationships. Cheers a classmate’s send; does not block someone from a turn.
Grades 1–2. Do not play with throws at people. Use Battleships.
Grades 3–4. Below the waist. Continuous switch.
Grades 5–6. Timed 2-minute waves. Count how long a group stays in.

Safety. No last-one-standing as the only version. Dodgeballs. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
