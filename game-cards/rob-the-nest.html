59. Rob the Nest

Invasion & team

When: September, January · Slot: 16–25 · Centre pile of objects · team hoops as nests · one object per trip

Get objects into your nest. One per trip.

Also called: Rob the Nest (dribble) · Capture the Egg · Capture the Flag · Bucket Ball · Robin’s Nest · Capture the Ball · Fishing · Capture the Cone

Equipment: Centre pile of objects · team hoops as nests

Set-up: Objects in the middle or in other nests. Teams at their hoop. Show the carry or dribble you want.

How we play

  1. First player runs in, takes one object, gets it into the nest, tags.
  2. Wrong action (two objects, no dribble if required) = object goes back.
  3. Later round: you may steal from another nest. No standing in a hoop to guard it.

If this happens

  • Two hands on the same object: first hand keeps it.
  • Centre traffic jam: one way in, other way out.
  • Puppy-guarding the nest: send them out.

How a round ends. Most objects in the nest, or first to a target number.

Cues

  • One ball at a time.
  • No standing in a hoop.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Head up. Pass or move — do not crowd the ball.
  • Find a passing lane, not a pile-up.
  • Soft send; the ball stays below the chest unless the sport needs a high set.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Dribble a ball (Rob the Nest).
  • Hook cones with a noodle (Fishing).
  • Outdoor flag and a jail.
  • Carry an egg; tagged = hand it over.
  • Bucket Ball: drop in a bucket instead of a hoop.
  • Robin’s Nest: same one-per-trip rule.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Rob the Nest (dribble); Capture the Egg; Capture The Flag Outdoors; Capture the Flag; Bucket Ball; Robin’s Nest; Capture the Ball; Fishing; Capture the Cone.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Rob the Nest (dribble) for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Ball stays close; trap or dribble under control.
  • Skill focus. One ball at a time; looks before sending.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role (attacker, defender, wall, sideline) and rotates without fuss.
  • Character Development. Honest score and honest tags; tries again after a miss.
  • Safety. Heads up. No slide tackles or body checks. Equipment stays below the agreed height.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls a teammate’s name; includes a player who has not touched the ball.
Grades 1–2. Small space, big targets, walking or jogging. Teacher may be a walking bumper. Hands may help a trap if the ball is soccer.
Grades 3–4. Full game as written, foam or real sport ball as the unit says. One pass before a score if the class crowds.
Grades 5–6. Student captains or officials. Offside-lite or a must-pass rule. Keep score without ranking classmates.

Safety. No slide tackles, no body checks. The ball is not thrown at a face. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle.
