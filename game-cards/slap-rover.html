23. Slap Rover

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class · 5 min · two end lines.

A visitor walks over, slaps one hand on the other team’s line, and races home before the slapped player can tag them. Use it to practise a quick reaction and a safe chase.

Equipment: Cones for two team lines.

Set-up: Two teams on opposite lines. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. One team calls a name: that player walks over with style.
  2. The receiving team holds out hands. The visitor slaps one hand, then sprints home.
  3. The slapped player chases. If the visitor makes it, the chaser joins them. If tagged, the visitor switches teams.
  4. Take turns down the line so everyone is called.
  5. Rotate taggers every 45–90 seconds so the same three students are not chasing all period.
  6. A tagged player takes the next role or uses the game’s re-enter rule right away — nobody sits out.
  7. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Cross on ‘rover come over.’
  • Soft tag. Re-enter next call.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.

Variations

  • Send a colour only.
  • Must skip across.
  • Walk-tag first, then jog. Add a second tagger only when space is clean.
  • Safe islands: three hoops that freeze a tagger for three seconds if a runner reaches one.
  • Tagged players become helpers (peelers, medics, or extra taggers) instead of sitting.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Slap Rover.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • A classic crossing game. Taggers in the middle. Call ‘Red rover, red rover, send everyone over’ (or a colour, or a clothing detail).
  • Tagged players become taggers, then stretch and re-enter as runners after two crossings so it does not become last-one-standing.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Slap Rover for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Crosses on the call, not after a wait.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Slap Rover.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Slap Rover; does not crowd a hospital or jail.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Slap Rover.
  • Safety. Tagged player re-enters next wave — no last-one-standing.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch, hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk both ways. Teacher calls names in order.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Beat-box from the calling team.

Safety. Slap hands only, not arms. No diving tags. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
