31. Wake-Up Bear

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · 1 bear · 45 s rounds.

The class chants until the bear wakes on a secret count, then the bear chases runners toward the walls. Use it to practise listening, a quick start and a short chase.

Equipment: A circle of cones. No blindfold.

Set-up: Bear sits in the middle facing away. Runners on the circle. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Teacher whispers a number 1–12 to the bear, or holds fingers the bear can see.
  2. The circle chants “Wake up, bear!” The bear stands on the secret count.
  3. Bear tags toward the walls. Tagged players do 5 jacks and become a second bear for the next round.
  4. Change the bear every 45 seconds.
  5. Rotate taggers every 45–90 seconds so the same three students are not chasing all period.
  6. A tagged player takes the next role or uses the game’s re-enter rule right away — nobody sits out.
  7. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Bear faces away.
  • Chase to the wall.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.

Variations

  • No blindfold — ever.
  • Walk-tag first, then jog. Add a second tagger only when space is clean.
  • Safe islands: three hoops that freeze a tagger for three seconds if a runner reaches one.
  • Tagged players become helpers (peelers, medics, or extra taggers) instead of sitting.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Wake-Up Bear.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Chant. Bear stands on the secret count. Tagged: 5 jacks, then a turn as bear.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Wake-Up Bear for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Runs to a wall, not a pile.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Wake-Up Bear.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Wake-Up Bear; does not crowd a hospital or jail.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Wake-Up Bear.
  • Safety. Bear is not blindfolded.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch, hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk chase. Teacher is the count.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Full speed with two-finger tags. Add one tactic (fake, safe-zone worth one use, or blob split). Students can referee a boundary.

Safety. No blindfold. Chase to the wall, not into a pile. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
