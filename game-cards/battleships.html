75. Battleships

Target & send

When: January, March · Slot: 16–25 · 2–4 teams · 8–10 min · pins, playground balls, optional standing mats.

Four teams throw playground balls to knock down other teams’ pins while protecting their own. Use it to practise an accurate send and quick defending.

Equipment: 4 hoops, 4 pins, playground balls, cones for zones.

Set-up: Four squares. A pin in a hoop at the back of each. Teams stay in their zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Throw playground balls at other pins. 5-second hold.
  2. Stand a pin back up as soon as it falls. Count knockdowns at the whistle.
  3. Nobody stands in their own hoop.
  4. Balls come back along the side. Restart on a teacher call, not a scramble through the middle.
  5. Play Battleships in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Stay in your zone.
  • Reset a pin; do not throw it back.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.

Variations

  • Four-corner ships.
  • Must hit the pin on a bounce.
  • Step back one giant step after each make (grades 3–6).
  • Opposite hand or weaker foot for the last round.
  • Team score is ‘makes in two minutes’ — not a ranking of throwers.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Battleships.
  • Watch for fetchers walking through the throwing line. Make them a job to the side.
  • Year plan: January, March — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Each team has a pin in a hoop (the ship) and a throwing line.
  • Throw playground balls to knock other ships. A downed pin is stood up by that team after a count of 3, or you keep a knockdown tally.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Battleships for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Underhand or below-shoulder send to a target.
  • Skill focus. Chooses a target and a force — close and soft, or far and firm — not a wild throw.
  • Skill focus. Waits a turn; collects on the call so the next player can send.
  • Character Development. Records or resets honestly; a miss is another try.
  • Safety. Fetchers are not in the throwing lane.
  • Healthy Relationships. Cheers a classmate’s send; does not block someone from a turn.
Grades 1–2. Short throwing line. Underhand or roll is allowed. Teacher fetches with the first group.
Grades 3–4. Standard distance. Opposite-foot step. Fetchers stay to the side.
Grades 5–6. Catch a thrown ball to freeze that thrower for a 10-count.

Safety. Throwing line is closed until fetchers are to the side. One object in the air per lane. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
