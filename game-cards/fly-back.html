85. FLY BACK

Target & send

When: January, March · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class or two teams · 6 min · sport ball or beanbag.

A runner takes a disc from the safe zone and throws it to teammates, who keep it only if they catch it. Use it to practise throwing and catching under a little pressure.

Equipment: Discs or balls, cones, pinnies.

Set-up: Two teams. A far safe zone holds the discs. A home catching area for each team. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. A runner crosses, picks one disc from the safe zone, and throws it toward teammates.
  2. A catch at home keeps the disc. A drop sends it back.
  3. First team to catch its set, or play to a whistle.
  4. Balls come back along the side. Restart on a teacher call, not a scramble through the middle.
  5. Play FLY BACK in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Throw, fly out, come back on a catch.
  • Call it.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.

Variations

  • Must catch one-handed for a bonus (Grade 5–6).
  • Step back one giant step after each make (grades 3–6).
  • Opposite hand or weaker foot for the last round.
  • Team score is ‘makes in two minutes’ — not a ranking of throwers.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in FLY BACK.
  • Watch for fetchers walking through the throwing line. Make them a job to the side.
  • Year plan: January, March — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • A thrower sends the ball. Fielders catch to send the thrower ‘back’ or to score a team point.
  • Use it as a Newcomb cousin without a net: catch and throw to a home hoop.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in FLY BACK for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Catch, then throw — call it.
  • Skill focus. Chooses a target and a force — close and soft, or far and firm — not a wild throw.
  • Skill focus. Waits a turn; collects on the call so the next player can send.
  • Character Development. Records or resets honestly; a miss is another try.
  • Safety. Sends toward the target, not a face. Walks to collect. Freeze on the whistle.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses a name or ‘mine’ before the send.
Grades 1–2. Walk the cross. Beanbags. No defender.
Grades 3–4. Standard distance. Opposite-foot step. Fetchers stay to the side.
Grades 5–6. Add a defender who may intercept in the air only.

Safety. Throwing line is closed until fetchers are to the side. One object in the air per lane. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
