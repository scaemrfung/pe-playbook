114. Castleball

Strike & field

When: June · Slot: 16–25 · Two halves · structures (pins, cone-castles, benches) · soft balls

Stay on your half. Knock the other team’s structures. Rebuild and keep playing.

Also called: Bombardment · Star Wars · Snowball Fight · Castleball 2.0

Equipment: Equal castles each side · soft balls

Set-up: Equal castles each side. Balls on each half. Below-the-head or structure-only throws.

How we play

  1. On go, throw at the other castles.
  2. 1 point per castle down. That team rebuilds while play continues.
  3. Whistle. Count standing castles or points.

If this happens

  • Throw at a person: no point, warning.
  • Cross the centre: that throw does not count.
  • All down in 10 seconds: play two bouts and add.

How a round ends. Most standing castles, or most knockdowns.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.
  • Below the head. A high ball is dead.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Beach ball only, push it onto the other bench (Star Wars).
  • Cone targets on a centre bench; touch your wall before you throw.
  • Retrievers vs throwers with a target hoop (Snowball Fight).
  • Bombardment / Battleball: same halves, same rebuild.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Bombardment; Battleball; Star Wars; Snowball Fight; Castleball 2.0.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).

More details · Grades 4-8

Equipment: Dodgeballs, hoops

A target game with a strong teamwork and strategy component. It also uses throwing, catching, blocking, building and awareness. Make two teams, one on each half of the gym; players never leave their own side. First, have students practise building castles from hoops: each castle uses 6 hoops (1 base, 4 sides, 1 top). It takes patience and teamwork to build a sturdy one. Each team then builds 2 castles at the back of its area. Add the dodgeballs. On the signal, players throw at the other team’s castles. Score a point each time a castle falls. Players rebuild a fallen castle while the game continues. Play as long as you like and add changes as needed.

  1. Two teams, each must stay on its own half.
  2. Practice building a 6-hoop castle: 1 hoop base, 4 standing side hoops, 1 roof hoop.
  3. Each team builds 2 castles toward the back of its area, then add foam dodgeballs.
  4. On the signal, throw at the other team’s castles to topple them. 1 point per castle down.
  5. When a castle falls, that team rebuilds while play continues. Play a set time.

More details: Castleball 2.0 · Grades 3-8

Equipment: balls, hoops, cones

A variation of Castleball with set roles. Set up the castles, choose roles, throw the “cannon balls” and rebuild together, so there is a job for everyone. Idea credit: Deric Hafer.

  1. Each team builds hoop castles in its back zone (6 hoops: 1 base, 4 walls, 1 roof).
  2. Assign roles: builders, throwers/cannons, gatherers, guards.
  3. On GO, cannons throw foam balls to topple the other castle. Builders only rebuild — they do not throw.
  4. Rotate roles every 2–3 minutes.
  5. 1 point each time a castle is fully toppled. Rebuild and continue.

More details: Bombardment · Grades 3-7

Equipment: variety

A multi-skill game that mostly uses basketball skills and can be adapted. Idea credit: Dan Kirsch.

  1. Two teams, each defending a half and a row of targets (pins, cones, or stacked items) near their end line.
  2. Scatter basketballs or foam balls at mid-court.
  3. On the whistle, players dribble/pass (basketball skills) and throw or shoot to knock down the other team’s targets.
  4. Score 1 point per target down, or capture the fallen target and add it to your side (pick one scoring rule).
  5. No player-to-player contact. Defenders may block balls and gather rebounds.
  6. Reset targets after a time cap or when one side is cleared. Modify to soccer-style (feet only) if you want.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Castleball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Throws low at a structure; ready hands to block or catch.
  • Skill focus. Knows when to throw and when to rebuild.
  • Skill focus. Takes a turn throwing and rebuilding; rotates jobs without fuss.
  • Character Development. Honest count of castles down; no arguing a call.
  • Safety. Looks up after contact; hits stay below the head.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls ‘mine’ on a loose ball; helps a teammate rebuild a castle.
Grades 1–2. Short throwing line. Underhand or roll is allowed. Teacher fetches with the first group.
Grades 3–4. Standard distance. Opposite-foot step. Fetchers stay to the side.
Grades 5–6. Step back after a make. Weaker hand or weaker foot on the last round. A student official on the line.

Safety. Throwing line is closed until fetchers are to the side. One object in the air per lane. Foam dodgeballs. Hits below the head. A high ball is dead. A hit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
