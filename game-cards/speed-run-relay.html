148. Speed Run Relay

Relays & stations

When: December, February · Slot: 16–25 · Many teams of 3–4 · 5 min.

Students sprint to a cone, do 3 jumps or a touch, then sprint back to tag the next runner. Use it for short shuttles with a skill at the turn.

Equipment: Cones, optional batons.

Set-up: Many teams of 4. Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. One runner at a time. Next player starts behind the home cone and goes on a high-five — not a shout from halfway.
  2. Sprint to a cone, 3 jumps or a touch, sprint back, tag.
  3. Sit when the team is done.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. Play Speed Run Relay in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Short shuttle.
  • Skill at the cone, then back.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Weak-foot dribble.
  • Carry a beanbag on the head.
  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Speed Run Relay.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: December, February — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Out to a cone, do a named skill (3 jumps, a trap, a touch), back, tag.
  • Keep teams short so the wait is one person.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Speed Run Relay for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Waits behind the line for the tag.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the tag, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Hands off — does not throw at a teammate.
  • Character Development. No extra steps past the line; a drop is picked up and the leg continues.
  • Safety. Runs in a lane. No diving at the line. Equipment carried, not thrown at people.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a drop.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Skip one way, jog back — or weaker foot/hand home. A student starter on the whistle.

Safety. Pass on the right. No diving at a hoop, pin, or beanbag. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
