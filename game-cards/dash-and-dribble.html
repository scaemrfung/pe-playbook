132. Dash and Dribble

Relays & stations

When: September, January · Slot: 16–25 · Relay teams of 4 · 6 min.

Students dribble to the cone, trap the ball, then sprint back to tag the next player. Use it to practise dribbling and trapping.

Equipment: Soccer balls, cones.

Set-up: Relay lines. A cone 8–12 m out. Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. One runner at a time. Next player starts behind the home cone and goes on a high-five — not a shout from halfway.
  2. Dribble to the cone, trap, sprint back, tag.
  3. Next player goes.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. Play Dash and Dribble in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Trap at the cone before you turn.
  • Hand off — don’t roll at a teammate.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Weak foot only.
  • Add a hoop step-in.
  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Dash and Dribble.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: September, January — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Dribble to a cone, trap, sprint back, tag the next hand.
  • If the ball runs away, recover it and keep the turn — no sitting out.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Dash and Dribble for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Ball stays close; trap or dribble under control.
  • Skill focus. One ball at a time; looks before sending.
  • Skill focus. Waits behind the line; tags the next runner kindly.
  • Character Development. No extra steps past the line; a drop is picked up and the leg continues.
  • Safety. Runs in a lane. No diving at the line. Equipment carried, not thrown at people.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a drop.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Skip one way, jog back — or weaker foot/hand home. A student starter on the whistle.

Safety. Eyes up at the turn. No long lines — more teams. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
