129. Circle run relay

Relays & stations

When: December, February · Slot: 16–25 · Even teams of 4–6 · 8–12 m lanes · two full rotations then a quality round (not a ranking).

One runner laps the circle, then high-fives the next runner. Use it for sustained running with fair hand-offs.

Equipment: Cone circle.

Set-up: Large loop, start cone. Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. One runner at a time. Next player starts behind the home cone and goes on a high-five — not a shout from halfway.
  2. One runner laps the circle. High-five. Next goes.
  3. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  4. Play Circle run relay in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Pass outside.
  • High-five.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Walk-jog for 1–2.
  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Circle run relay.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: December, February — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • One lap. Next runner.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Keeps a pace they can finish.
  • Movement Skill Development. Repeats the named locomotor or send with control to the line and back.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the high-five, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Tags the next runner, not a shortcut.
  • Character Development. No extra steps past the line; the leg continues to the end.
  • Safety. Runs in a lane. No diving at the line.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a slow leg.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Choose a pace they can keep.

Safety. Pass on the right. No diving at a hoop, pin, or beanbag. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
