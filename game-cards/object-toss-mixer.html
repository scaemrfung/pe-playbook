201. Object Toss mixer

Cooperative & circus

When: December, October · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class · 5 min · mixed balls, beanbags, and rings.

Students say a name and toss a scarf, adding objects until several are in the air. Use it to practise names, tossing and catching.

Equipment: Scarves, then beanbags.

Set-up: One circle, or two if the class is large. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Start with one scarf. Say a name and toss.
  2. Add a second object, then a third.
  3. Drops are part of circus — restart that object, do not stop the game.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. Play Object Toss mixer in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Match the send to the object.
  • Call the name before you throw.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Use names. Ask before you grab a hoop, rope, or balloon.
  • If it drops, restart together — no blame.
  • Quiet voices so the group can hear the next job.

Variations

  • Non-dominant hand.
  • Must send to a moving partner.
  • Smaller groups of 6 if the whole-class version tangles.
  • Silent round: only gestures, then talk again to plan.
  • Add one extra object (second balloon, extra hoop) when the first task is easy.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Object Toss mixer.
  • Watch for one student doing all the work. Give every group a ‘must-touch’ rule.
  • Year plan: December, October — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Beanbags, light balls, rings in a middle pile.
  • On the whistle, take one, find a partner, complete 5 sends, switch objects.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Object Toss mixer for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Matches the send to the object.
  • Skill focus. Plans a simple order or role with teammates before rushing.
  • Skill focus. Takes turns and solves a small problem without grabbing.
  • Character Development. Restarts a drop without blaming a classmate.
  • Safety. Moves on the call, not before.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses names and kind words; waits a turn without pulling equipment away.
Grades 1–2. Small groups of 4–6. Teacher in the group if the task needs an extra pair of hands.
Grades 3–4. Whole-class or groups of 8. Plan for 20 seconds, then try.
Grades 5–6. Groups design one extra rule that still includes everyone, then teach it to another group.

Safety. Toss to a spot just above your head, not at faces. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
