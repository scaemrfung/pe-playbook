218. Hoop Balance

Fitness & landings

When: October, February · Slot: 8-15 · Daly Exercise · slot 8-15.

Carry a hoop between two stomachs with no hands and drop it over a cone. Daly Exercise game. Nobody sits the period.

Also called: Daly Exercise: Hoop Balance

Equipment: Hoops; a target cone

Set-up: Pair face each other, hoop pinched at the waist. Cone ahead.

How we play

  1. Walk to the cone without hands on the hoop.
  2. Drop it so it lands around the cone.
  3. Run back and tag the next pair.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset kit, then the next round.

If this happens

  • Kit knocks a person: pause, widen space, restart.
  • Argument over a point: teacher call, play on.
  • Someone sits out after a miss: they rejoin on the next turn with a short task instead.

How a round ends. Play 3–6 minutes, or first team/player to the posted target, then rotate jobs.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • One job at a time. Extra players fetch or count.
  • A miss is a short task, then back in.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Allow one fingertip.
  • Shorter distance.
  • Three people, one hoop.

Teaching tips

  • Source: Daly Exercise. Same house rules as the rest of the playbook.
  • Watch for crowding at one piece of kit. Add a second station before you add speed.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Hoop Balance for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the taught action in Hoop Balance with control, not a rush that knocks kit or people.
  • Skill focus. Chooses space, timing, or a partner job in Hoop Balance instead of crowding.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares kit in Hoop Balance.
  • Character Development. Honest about a miss or a tag in Hoop Balance.
  • Safety. Freeze on the whistle. Soft tags.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a classmate re-enter after a miss or a tag.
Grades 1–2. Allow one fingertip.
Grades 3–4. Shorter distance.
Grades 5–6. Three people, one hoop.

Safety. No elimination. Freeze on the whistle. Soft two-finger or noodle tags on the back or shoulder — never the head. A miss is a short task, then stay in.
