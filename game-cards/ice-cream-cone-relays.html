140. Ice Cream Cone Relays

Relays & stations

When: December, February · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · K-4 · 5–8 min rounds.

Run three ice-cream relays: scoop stack, build the cone and take the order. Use them for team building, a single lesson or a field-day rotation.

Equipment: foam balls, cones

Set-up: Split the class into even relay teams. Give each team a home marker and foam balls (scoops) plus cones (the waffle cones). Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Relay A — Scoop stack: Runner 1 carries one foam ball to the far cone, balances it as a scoop, and sprints back to tag the next player. First team to stack the set number of scoops without a collapse wins.
  2. Relay B — Build the cone: Cones start nested at home. Runners take turns carrying one cone out and stacking a taller ice-cream cone at the far end. Rebuild if it falls.
  3. Relay C — Take the order: Teacher calls an order (“3 scoops”). The runner must carry that many balls in one trip, place them on the cone, and return.
  4. Change locomotor skills between relays (run, skip, gallop). Use as a field-day rotation or a single lesson.
  5. High-five the next runner at home. First team sitting quietly after two rotations earns a cheer, then play a quality round.
  6. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Ice Cream Cone Relays.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: December, February — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Ice Cream Cone Relays for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Repeats the named locomotor or send with control to the line and back.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the tag, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. The group finishes the task together.
  • Character Development. Restarts a drop without blaming a classmate.
  • Safety. Runs in a lane. No diving at the line. Equipment carried, not thrown at people.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses names and kind words; waits a turn without grabbing.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Skip one way, jog back — or weaker foot/hand home. A student starter on the whistle.

Safety. Pass on the right. No diving at a hoop, pin, or beanbag. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
