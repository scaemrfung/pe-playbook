155. Back to Base

Locomotor & listen

When: June · Slot: 0–5 closer · Whole class · 2 min as a closer.

Play any travel game, and on the whistle everyone returns to their home base. Use it to practise listening and a quick response to a signal.

Equipment: Home hoops or spots.

Set-up: Assign bases. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Play any travel game.
  2. Whistle: everyone back to base.
  3. Last group leads a stretch, then plays again.
  4. If you miss a signal, skip the next one and rejoin — no extra laps as punishment.
  5. Play Back to Base in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Whistle means home.
  • Last group leads a stretch — they are not out.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Move the way I named (walk, skip, gallop) — not a race.
  • Stop in a balanced shape: two feet, knees soft.
  • Give neighbours a hoop of space.

Variations

  • Must travel a named locomotor home.
  • Colour bases.
  • Whisper round, then a music round. Same skills, different energy.
  • Let a student call two commands (teacher stays on freeze and safety).
  • Add a shape at the freeze: tuck, star, or stork.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Back to Base.
  • Watch for crashing on a freeze. Practise a walk-freeze before any sprint command.
  • Year plan: June — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Each student or pair has a home hoop.
  • On the whistle, everyone returns. Use after circus or station work.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Back to Base for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Performs the named locomotor, freeze, or shape on the signal.
  • Skill focus. Finds an open pathway on the go signal; does not cut a neighbour in Back to Base.
  • Skill focus. Last group leads a stretch — not out.
  • Character Development. Freezes honestly on the signal even if it is fun to keep running.
  • Safety. Returns to the home hoop on the whistle.
  • Healthy Relationships. Moves around people, not through them; helps a classmate who missed the cue.
Grades 1–2. Walk first. Two commands only (go and freeze). Teacher models every shape.
Grades 3–4. Add skip, gallop, and a shape at the freeze. Students may suggest one command.
Grades 5–6. A student DJ or caller for two minutes. Teacher keeps freeze and safety.

Safety. Give a hoop of space. Freeze means still feet — not a last-second shove. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
