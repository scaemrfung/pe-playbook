93. Low-ball

Target & send

When: January, March · Slot: 16–25 · 3–5 even teams or pairs · 8–12 minutes · fetchers stay to the side of the throwing line.

Students send dodgeballs at feet or shins only, and a hit player does a 10-second stretch, then returns. Use it to practise a controlled, low throw.

Equipment: 4–6 dodgeballs, centre line.

Set-up: Two halves. Balls start on the line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. On the whistle, send balls at opponents’ feet or shins only.
  2. A catch is a save. A below-waist hit: 10-second stretch on your sideline, then back in.
  3. Balls that go high are dead. Pick them up and start again.
  4. Balls come back along the side. Restart on a teacher call, not a scramble through the middle.
  5. Play Low-ball in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Feet and shins.
  • Stretch, then back in.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.

Variations

  • Roll only for 1–2.
  • Step back one giant step after each make (grades 3–6).
  • Opposite hand or weaker foot for the last round.
  • Team score is ‘makes in two minutes’ — not a ranking of throwers.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Low-ball.
  • Watch for fetchers walking through the throwing line. Make them a job to the side.
  • Year plan: January, March — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • A high ball is dead. A hit is 10 seconds, not an out.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Low-ball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Sends below the waist.
  • Skill focus. Chooses a target and a force — close and soft, or far and firm — not a wild throw.
  • Skill focus. Waits a turn; collects on the call so the next player can send.
  • Character Development. Records or resets honestly; a miss is another try.
  • Safety. Re-enters after the stretch.
  • Healthy Relationships. Cheers a classmate’s send; does not block someone from a turn.
Grades 1–2. Short throwing line. Underhand or roll is allowed. Teacher fetches with the first group.
Grades 3–4. Standard distance. Opposite-foot step. Fetchers stay to the side.
Grades 5–6. Step back after a make. Weaker hand or weaker foot on the last round. A student official on the line.

Safety. Throwing line is closed until fetchers are to the side. One object in the air per lane. Foam dodgeballs. Hits below the waist. A high ball is dead. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
