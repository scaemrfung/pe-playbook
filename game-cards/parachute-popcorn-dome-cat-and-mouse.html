202. Parachute popcorn / dome / cat-and-mouse

Cooperative & circus

When: October, December · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class on the chute · 8–10 min.

Run the core parachute activities: waves, the dome and a chase under the parachute. Use it to practise working together on a count.

Equipment: Parachute or king-size sheet, beanbags.

Set-up: Everyone has a colour handle. Practise a count of three to lift. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Ripples, then big waves.
  2. Dome/mushroom: lift together, step in, sit on the edge.
  3. Popcorn: beanbags on the chute; keep them bouncing.
  4. Cat-and-mouse: one student on top on hands and knees, one under; class makes small waves. Switch often.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  6. A drop or a tangle is a restart from the last good shape, not a loss.

Cues

  • Hold the handle, not the cloth.
  • Bend knees for a dome.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Use names. Ask before you grab a hoop, rope, or balloon.
  • If it drops, restart together — no blame.
  • Quiet voices so the group can hear the next job.

Variations

  • Number games under the chute.
  • Mushroom freeze.
  • Smaller groups of 6 if the whole-class version tangles.
  • Silent round: only gestures, then talk again to plan.
  • Add one extra object (second balloon, extra hoop) when the first task is easy.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Parachute popcorn / dome / cat-and-mouse.
  • Watch for one student doing all the work. Give every group a ‘must-touch’ rule.
  • Year plan: October, December — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Ripples: small waves. Popcorn: light balls or beanbags on the chute — keep them on. Dome: step in, lift, sit on the edge if you taught it.
  • Cat and mouse: one student on top (walking), one underneath — only if the dome is well-held and you have taught it. Skip for Grade 1 if the chute is chaotic.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Parachute popcorn / dome / cat-and-mouse for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Matches force with the group.
  • Skill focus. Plans a simple order or role with teammates before rushing.
  • Skill focus. The chute stays round; nobody lets go to chase.
  • Character Development. Restarts a drop without blaming a classmate.
  • Safety. Moves at a speed the group can control. Consent for partner shapes — a student may say no.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses names and kind words; waits a turn without pulling equipment away.
Grades 1–2. Small ripples first. Walk under.
Grades 3–4. Whole-class or groups of 8. Plan for 20 seconds, then try.
Grades 5–6. Students count the lift. Pop to a target hoop.

Safety. Shoulders down. No wrapping anyone. One cat, one mouse. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
