177. Partner Orienteering

Locomotor & listen

When: May, June · Slot: 16-25 · Daly Exercise · slot 16-25.

Map-to-ground. The pair stays together. Both hold the map. Daly Exercise game. Nobody sits the period.

Also called: Daly Exercise: Partner Orienteering

Equipment: Simple maps; controls; record cards

Set-up: Map matches the real space. Show how to turn the map.

How we play

  1. Orient the map, walk to the first control.
  2. Record the answer. Swap who leads.
  3. Pairs stay together the whole time.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset kit, then the next round.

If this happens

  • Kit knocks a person: pause, widen space, restart.
  • Argument over a point: teacher call, play on.
  • Someone sits out after a miss: they rejoin on the next turn with a short task instead.

How a round ends. Play 3–6 minutes, or first team/player to the posted target, then rotate jobs.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • One job at a time. Extra players fetch or count.
  • A miss is a short task, then back in.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Colour-coded first loop.
  • Full simple course.
  • More controls. No splitting up.

Teaching tips

  • Source: Daly Exercise. Same house rules as the rest of the playbook.
  • Watch for crowding at one piece of kit. Add a second station before you add speed.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Partner Orienteering for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the taught action in Partner Orienteering with control, not a rush that knocks kit or people.
  • Skill focus. Chooses space, timing, or a partner job in Partner Orienteering instead of crowding.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares kit in Partner Orienteering.
  • Character Development. Honest about a miss in Partner Orienteering.
  • Safety. Freeze on the whistle.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a classmate try again after a miss.
