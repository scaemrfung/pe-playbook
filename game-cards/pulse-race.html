204. Pulse Race

Cooperative & circus

When: December, October · Slot: 16–25 · Two or more lines of 6–10 · coin or colour card · one grab-object per line

A legal pulse beats a guessed grab. Honesty is the skill.

Also called: Pulse · Daly Pulse

Equipment: Coin or colour card · one grab-object per line

Set-up: Sit or kneel in files, hands touching the next person. Object beyond the last player. First players face you.

How we play

  1. Show heads/tails or a colour only the front players can see.
  2. Tails / wrong colour: nobody moves. A squeeze here is a false start.
  3. Heads / right colour: front player squeezes. The pulse travels. Last player grabs.
  4. Fastest legal grab wins the round. Reset hands and the object.

If this happens

  • Squeeze on tails: that line cannot score the round.
  • Pulse dies mid-line: no grab, replay.
  • Last player peeks and grabs early: same as a false start.

How a round ends. First line to 5 legal grabs, or play 4–6 rounds.

Cues

  • Gentle squeeze.
  • Last player takes the ball.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Use names. Ask before you grab a hoop, rope, or balloon.
  • If it drops, restart together — no blame.
  • Quiet voices so the group can hear the next job.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Classroom sit-down.
  • Standing gym lines.
  • Hockey day: sticks parked, pulse with hands only.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Pulse; Daly Pulse.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Pulse Race for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the needed skill (pass, step-through, carry, balance) so the group can finish.
  • Skill focus. Plans a simple order or role with teammates before rushing.
  • Skill focus. Waits for the squeeze.
  • Character Development. Resets after a false start.
  • Safety. Moves at a speed the group can control. Consent for partner shapes — a student may say no.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses names and kind words; waits a turn without pulling equipment away.
Grades 1–2. Teacher stands where the line can see a thumbs-up.
Grades 3–4. Whole-class or groups of 8. Plan for 20 seconds, then try.
Grades 5–6. Groups design one extra rule that still includes everyone, then teach it to another group.

Safety. Ask before you pull a hoop, rope, or teammate. Stop if hair, glasses, or a wrist is caught. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in.
