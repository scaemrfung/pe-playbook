47. End Zone Ball

Invasion & team

When: September, October · Slot: 16–25 · Same family as End Ball · 2 × 4 min.

Players roll, not throw, the ball to teammates in the end zone while the other team tries to intercept. Use it to practise accurate sending and intercepting.

Equipment: 4–8 footballs or soccer balls, pinnies, optional mats as obstacles.

Set-up: Two halves. Each team names 2–3 end-zone catchers who stay in their end. Pinnies on one team. Show the two attacking directions before GO. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Players in their half roll balls to their own catchers.
  2. The other team tries to intercept rolls.
  3. A clean catch in the end zone scores.
  4. Play 4–6 minute periods. Extra players are bumpers, walls, or the next wave — they stay in a job.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Move after you pass.
  • Eyes up before you throw.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Head up. Pass or move — do not crowd the ball.
  • Find a passing lane, not a pile-up.
  • Soft send; the ball stays below the chest unless the sport needs a high set.

Variations

  • Two balls for a big class.
  • Score only in a hoop inside the end zone.
  • Must complete one pass before a shot or a score.
  • Add a second foam ball if more than 24 students so more players get a touch.
  • Extra players are walking walls — they may deflect a ball with feet but may not score.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in End Zone Ball.
  • Watch for one student holding the ball. Cue a pass or a 3-second pivot.
  • Year plan: September, October — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Flag-football shape with a sport ball or beanbag.
  • 3-second or 5-second hold. No running with the ball.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in End Zone Ball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Carrier pivots — no running with the ball.
  • Skill focus. Looks for a teammate before a score.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role (attacker, defender, wall, sideline) and rotates without fuss.
  • Character Development. Honest score; tries again after a miss.
  • Safety. Heads up. No slide tackles or body checks. Equipment stays below the agreed height.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls a teammate’s name; includes a player who has not touched the ball.
Grades 1–2. Small space, big targets, walking or jogging. Teacher may be a walking bumper. Hands may help a trap if the ball is soccer.
Grades 3–4. Full game as written, foam or real sport ball as the unit says. One pass before a score if the class crowds.
Grades 5–6. Add a 3-second hold and a must-pass rule.

Safety. No slide tackles, no body checks. The ball is not thrown at a face. Walk on and off mats. No diving onto a house mat. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
