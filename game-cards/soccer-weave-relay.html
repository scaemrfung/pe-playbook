147. Soccer weave relay

Relays & stations

When: September · Slot: 16–25 · Even teams of 4–6 · 8–12 m lanes · two full rotations then a quality round (not a ranking).

Students weave a soccer ball out through cones with small touches, then dribble straight home. Use it to practise close ball control.

Equipment: 6 cones, one soccer ball per team.

Set-up: Zig-zag cones. Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. One runner at a time. Next player starts behind the home cone and goes on a high-five — not a shout from halfway.
  2. Weave out. Straight dribble home. Foot stays near the ball.
  3. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  4. Play Soccer weave relay in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Small touches.
  • Head up.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Weaker foot home in 5–6.
  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Soccer weave relay.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Weave out. Straight home.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Soccer weave relay for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Keeps the ball in the lane.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the high-five, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Waits behind the line; high-fives the next runner kindly.
  • Character Development. No extra steps past the line; a drop is picked up and the leg continues.
  • Safety. No slide tackles.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a drop.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Skip one way, jog back — or weaker foot/hand home. A student starter on the whistle.

Safety. Balls stay on the floor. No slide tackles. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
