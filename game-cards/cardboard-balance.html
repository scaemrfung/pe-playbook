190. Cardboard Balance

Cooperative & circus

When: December, February, October · Slot: 8-15 · Daly Exercise · slot 8-15.

Tilt together so balls fall through the hole, not off the edge. Daly Exercise game. Nobody sits the period.

Also called: Daly Exercise: Cardboard Balance

Equipment: Cardboard tray with four corner strings, 2-3 balls, box

Set-up: Hole in the centre. Pair opposite, two strings each.

How we play

  1. Tilt together. Talk: left, right, hold.
  2. Ball through the hole stays in the box.
  3. Ball off an edge: stop, put it back.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset kit, then the next round.

If this happens

  • Kit knocks a person: pause, widen space, restart.
  • Argument over a point: teacher call, play on.
  • Someone sits out after a miss: they rejoin on the next turn with a short task instead.

How a round ends. Play 3–6 minutes, or first team/player to the posted target, then rotate jobs.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • One job at a time. Extra players fetch or count.
  • A miss is a short task, then back in.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • One ball. Larger hole.
  • Two or three balls.
  • Three holders on a bigger tray.

Teaching tips

  • Source: Daly Exercise. Same house rules as the rest of the playbook.
  • Watch for crowding at one piece of kit. Add a second station before you add speed.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Cardboard Balance for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the taught action in Cardboard Balance with control, not a rush that knocks kit or people.
  • Skill focus. Chooses space, timing, or a partner job in Cardboard Balance instead of crowding.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares kit in Cardboard Balance.
  • Character Development. Honest about a miss in Cardboard Balance.
  • Safety. Freeze on the whistle.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a classmate try again after a miss.
