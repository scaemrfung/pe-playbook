134. Fetch Relay

Relays & stations

When: December, February · Slot: 0–5 or 16–25 · File of runners · item pile · home hoop or sack

One item per trip. Tag. Next person.

Also called: Attention Relay · Bucket Carry · Around the Bases Relay · Cone Flipper · Three Course Meal · Cone Collector · Post the Parcel

Equipment: Item pile · home hoop or sack

Set-up: Pile at the far end. Home hoop at the start. Show what ‘one item’ looks like.

How we play

  1. On go (and on a colour if you are using colours), first runner fetches one legal item, places it home, tags.
  2. Wrong item goes back.
  3. Continue until the pile is gone or time ends.

If this happens

  • Two items: extra goes back.
  • Sack or bucket left behind: return for it.

How a round ends. Most correct items, or first team finished.

Cues

  • Wait for the call.
  • Start on your number or colour.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Teacher calls the colour after go.
  • Bib-sack must travel.
  • Roll the ball around a slalom cone (Snowball).
  • Flip three cones, three tries each (Cone Flipper).
  • Move one ‘course’ one hoop forward (Three Course Meal).
  • Cone balanced on one foot home (Cone Collector).
  • Around the Bases: one item is a touch of each base.
  • Bucket Carry: the bucket must travel with the item.
  • Post the Parcel: hide five beanbags under a cone 5–10 m away; each runner carries a tennis ball out, leaves it under the cone, brings a beanbag back and tags the next runner; done when every beanbag is swapped.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Attention Relay; Bucket Carry; Around the Bases Relay; Cone Flipper; Three Course Meal; Cone Collector.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Attention Relay for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Waits behind the line for the tag.
  • Skill focus. Starts on the tag, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Hands off — does not throw at a teammate.
  • Character Development. No extra steps past the line; a drop is picked up and the leg continues.
  • Safety. Runs in a lane. No diving at the line. Equipment carried, not thrown at people.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the next runner; does not shame a drop.
Grades 1–2. Walk or skip. Shorter lane. Redo a trip if the skill falls apart — no extra penalty laps.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Add a skill on the loop (3 jumps, a skip).

Safety. Pass on the right. No diving at a hoop, pin, or beanbag. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
