139. Hungry Snake

Relays & stations

When: December, February · Slot: 16–25 · One snake of 4–6 · 5 min.

Each team stays linked in a line while the head collects objects and passes them back to the tail. Use it to practise teamwork and moving together.

Equipment: 20–40 beanbags or yarn balls, 2 bags or bins.

Set-up: Two teams in two lines, each student holding the shirt or shoulders of the person in front. Objects scattered. Even teams of 4–6. Lanes about 8–12 m, home cone behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. On go, the snake slithers. Only the head may pick up an object.
  2. Pass it back person to person into the tail bag.
  3. If the snake breaks, freeze, reconnect, then go.
  4. High-five the next runner at home. First team sitting quietly after two rotations earns a cheer, then play a quality round.
  5. Play Hungry Snake in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Hold waists or a hoop train — no yanking.
  • The head tags.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Wait behind the cone. Go on the high-five.
  • Stay in your lane. Pass on the right if you meet someone.
  • Quality over sprint — reset if the skill falls apart.

Variations

  • Must stay on lines.
  • Two snakes from the start.
  • Change the locomotor: walk, skip, gallop, side-slide. Running is not the only option.
  • Quality round: the team must redo a trip if the ball, bag, or pin is dropped.
  • Weaker hand or weaker foot home on the last rotation (grades 5–6).

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Hungry Snake.
  • Watch for early starts. Next runner stays behind the cone until the high-five.
  • Year plan: December, February — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • A small chain is the snake. The head tags runners. A tagged runner joins the tail.
  • Split at 6 into two snakes.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Hungry Snake for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. The chain splits before it gets unsafe.
  • Skill focus. Starts on go, not early; chooses a path that does not cut off another team.
  • Skill focus. Holds on without yanking.
  • Character Development. Only the head picks up a drop; the snake reconnects and continues.
  • Safety. Equipment carried, not thrown at people.
  • Healthy Relationships. Encourages the snake; does not shame a break or a drop.
Grades 1–2. Slow waves. Short snakes of 4.
Grades 3–4. Jog. Full lane. High-five hand-off. One quality redo if a pin or ball is dropped.
Grades 5–6. Travel the whole gym. No talking round.

Safety. No running while linked in a long chain. Break at 6 if it whips. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
