2. Angry Neighbour

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · 1-5 · 5–8 min rounds.

Neighbours with pool noodles chase players off the yard, and tagged players go to a house mat, then re-enter. Use it to practise changing speed and safe noodle tagging.

Also called: On/Off

Equipment: pool noodles, exercise mats

Set-up: Lay exercise mats as houses along one side. The open floor is the yard. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Give 3–6 “neighbours” a pool noodle (the broom).
  2. Players start in the yard. Neighbours chase and tag with the noodle (legs/hips).
  3. Tagged players go to a house mat. Re-enter on a teacher call (“new yard!”) or a teammate high-five — pick one rule.
  4. Rotate neighbours every 60–90 seconds.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.
  • Noodle is a tap on a hip or shoe — never a swing at a head.

Variations

  • Walk-tag first, then jog. Add a second tagger only when space is clean.
  • Safe islands: three hoops that freeze a tagger for three seconds if a runner reaches one.
  • Tagged players become helpers (peelers, medics, or extra taggers) instead of sitting.
  • Teacher holds the only extra noodle in grades 1–2.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Angry Neighbour.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Noodles: demo a hip tap on a volunteer, then a wrong swing, then the hip tap again.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Angry Neighbour for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Changes speed and pathway to avoid a tag in Angry Neighbour.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Angry Neighbour.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Angry Neighbour; does not crowd a hospital or jail.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Angry Neighbour.
  • Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk-tag. Teacher plus one student tagger. 30–45 second bursts. Re-enter with a high-five or the game’s hospital rule. Teacher may hold the only extra noodle.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Full speed with two-finger tags. Add one tactic (fake, safe-zone worth one use, or blob split). Students can referee a boundary.

Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tags on the back or shoulder — never the head. Noodles tap hips or shoes; they are not swords. Walk on and off mats. No diving onto a house mat. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
