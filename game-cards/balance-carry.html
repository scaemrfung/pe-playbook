210. Balance Carry

Fitness & landings

When: February, April · Slot: 16–25 · One object per person or pair · a finish or a court

The object stays on. If it falls, reset and continue.

Also called: Racquet waiter · Beanbag head walk · Body-part beanbags · Protect the Ice Cream · Cone Stack · Core Beanbag Rescue · Pancake Relay

Equipment: One object per person or pair (ball on cone, bag on racquet, bag on head)

Set-up: Show the carry (ball on cone, bag on racquet, bag on head). No body checks.

How we play

  1. On go, travel.
  2. If it falls, stop, replace, continue from there.
  3. Optional: knock other ice creams off for a point.

If this happens

  • Thumb pinching the ball on the whole time: remind them it must sit.
  • Shoulder charge: no point, both reset.

How a round ends. First across with it still on, or fewest drops.

Cues

  • Palm up.
  • Soft toss.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Quiet landings. Bend ankles, knees, and hips.
  • Quality beats speed. Stop if a wrist, knee, or back complains.
  • Breathe out on the work; do not hold a breath to rush.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Cone on one foot, hop home (Cone Collector).
  • Bib in the air, place a cone in a hoop, catch the bib (Cone Stack).
  • Feet stay in hoop 1, rescue bags from hoop 2 (Core Beanbag Rescue).
  • Racquet waiter: bag on a racquet.
  • Beanbag head walk.
  • Body-part beanbags: named body part holds the bag.
  • Pancake Relay: small relay teams; balance a beanbag “pancake” on a tennis racket around the team cone and back, replace it if it drops, then hand racket and pancake to the next runner.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Racquet waiter; Beanbag head walk; Body-part beanbags; Protect the Ice Cream; Cone Stack; Core Beanbag Rescue.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Racquet waiter for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Keeps the bag on the racquet to the cone.
  • Skill focus. Paces the set so they can finish, not sprint and collapse.
  • Skill focus. Works beside a partner or in a hoop without bumping others out.
  • Character Development. Records a private number honestly, or skips the number and names effort.
  • Safety. Tosses at the hoop, not a person.
  • Healthy Relationships. Lets a classmate have a hoop or spot; no blocking a landing.
Grades 1–2. Half the distance or fewer reps. Knees-down options. Stop if wrists or backs complain.
Grades 3–4. Full lane or full set. Quiet landings. Partner counts.
Grades 5–6. Walk around a cone before the toss.

Safety. Quiet landings. Stop a station if a wrist, knee, or back complains — switch to a walk option. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
