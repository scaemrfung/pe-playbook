209. Wall Rally

Cooperative & circus

When: December, October · Slot: 16–25 · Wall, rope, or a square of floor markers · 1 soft ball · pairs or 2v2

Keep a legal rally going. One bounce. Point on a miss.

Also called: Cooperative Wall Ball · Hand Tennis · Floorball · Wall-ball rally · Gaga

Equipment: 1 soft ball · wall, rope, or a square of floor markers

Set-up: Small court. Underhand serve. Soft hands or a low racket.

How we play

  1. Serve so it lands on the other side or on the wall legally.
  2. Return after one bounce.
  3. More than one bounce, out, or a high racket = point. Winner serves.

If this happens

  • Fist smash: redo as an open-hand hit.
  • Ball lifted in floorball: dead ball, restart.

How a round ends. First to 7, or highest score at time.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Use names. Ask before you grab a hoop, rope, or balloon.
  • If it drops, restart together — no blame.
  • Quiet voices so the group can hear the next job.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Bench as a net.
  • Mini goals (Floorball).
  • Cone-ring gaga pit, hits below the waist.
  • Cooperative wall ball: count consecutive hits, drop restarts the count.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Cooperative Wall Ball; Hand Tennis; Floorball; Wall-ball rally; Gaga.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

More details: Cooperative Wall Ball · Grades 1-8

Equipment: ball, beach ball

A teamwork game with 3 levels of difficulty. Start by asking players what co-operation means; they will find that working together gets the task done faster.

  1. Ask the class what cooperation looks like and sounds like.
  2. Level 1: Keep a playground ball going against a wall for a target number of hits. Drop = restart the count together.
  3. Level 2: Same task, but no player may touch the ball twice in a row.
  4. Level 3: Switch to a beach ball that the group must control off the wall together.
  5. Time attempts. Celebrate the group streak, not star players. Split a huge class into wall groups.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Cooperative Wall Ball for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the needed skill (pass, step-through, carry, balance) so the group can finish.
  • Skill focus. Plans a simple order or role with teammates before rushing.
  • Skill focus. The group finishes the task together.
  • Character Development. Restarts a drop without blaming a classmate.
  • Safety. Moves at a speed the group can control. Consent for partner shapes — a student may say no.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses names and kind words; waits a turn without grabbing.
Grades 1–2. Small groups of 4–6. Teacher in the group if the task needs an extra pair of hands.
Grades 3–4. Whole-class or groups of 8. Plan for 20 seconds, then try.
Grades 5–6. Groups design one extra rule that still includes everyone, then teach it to another group.

Safety. Ask before you pull a hoop, rope, or teammate. Stop if hair, glasses, or a wrist is caught. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
