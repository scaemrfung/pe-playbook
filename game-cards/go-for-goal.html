50. Go for Goal

Invasion & team

When: October, January · Slot: 16–25 · Stations or 1v1 · 8 min.

Small teams of 2–3 pass once before shooting at cone goals, and waiting players act as walls or the next wave. Use it to practise passing and shooting.

Equipment: Soccer balls, cone goals, pinnies.

Set-up: 2–3 a side. Extra players are waiting walls or next wave. Pinnies on one team. Show the two attacking directions before GO. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Name the two jobs (attack / defend or the two end-lines) and which ball we are using.
  2. Pass once before a shot.
  3. Rotate every 2 minutes.
  4. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.
  5. Play Go for Goal in short rounds so more students get the key job, then freeze and reset.

Cues

  • Head up.
  • Shoot from a marked arc.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Head up. Pass or move — do not crowd the ball.
  • Find a passing lane, not a pile-up.
  • Soft send; the ball stays below the chest unless the sport needs a high set.

Variations

  • Must shoot with the weak foot or off-hand.
  • One-touch finish.
  • Must complete one pass before a shot or a score.
  • Add a second foam ball if more than 24 students so more players get a touch.
  • Extra players are walking walls — they may deflect a ball with feet but may not score.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Go for Goal.
  • Watch for one student holding the ball. Cue a pass or a 3-second pivot.
  • Year plan: October, January — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Small cone goals. 1v1 or 2v2. Rotate after a goal or after 45 seconds.
  • Use hockey sticks only if you already taught a safe grip and the ball is a hockey ball or a puck.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Go for Goal for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Ball stays close; trap or dribble under control.
  • Skill focus. One ball at a time; looks before sending.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role (attacker, defender, wall, sideline) and rotates without fuss.
  • Character Development. Honest score; tries again after a miss.
  • Safety. Heads up. No slide tackles or body checks. Equipment stays below the agreed height.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls a teammate’s name; includes a player who has not touched the ball.
Grades 1–2. Small space, big targets, walking or jogging. Teacher may be a walking bumper. Hands may help a trap if the ball is soccer.
Grades 3–4. Full game as written, foam or real sport ball as the unit says. One pass before a score if the class crowds.
Grades 5–6. Student captains or officials. Offside-lite or a must-pass rule. Keep score without ranking classmates.

Safety. No slide tackles, no body checks. The ball is not thrown at a face. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
