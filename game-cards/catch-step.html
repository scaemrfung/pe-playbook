115. Catch & Step

Strike & field

When: June · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · 1-8 · 5–8 min rounds.

Partners step back after each catch and step forward after a drop. Use it to practise throwing and catching as the challenge grows.

Equipment: Type of ball, or water balloons

Set-up: Partners start close and play catch with a ball (or, outdoors in warm weather, a water balloon). Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. After every successful catch, both players take one step backward.
  2. On a drop, they take a step forward (or, if you are using beanbags, that pair is wet and rejoins with a ball).
  3. See which pair ends farthest apart after a time cap.
  4. Balls come back along the side. Restart on a teacher call, not a scramble through the middle.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Ready position in the field — feet still, hands out.
  • Hit, then run the path. Do not watch your own hit.
  • Fielders send to the base or hoop, not at a runner.
  • Open-hand tap. Do not catch or punch the balloon.

Variations

  • Tee or a bounced teacher-toss for grades 1–2.
  • Three-pitch maximum, then a run-out anyway so the line keeps moving.
  • Fielders rotate one spot after each batter.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Catch & Step.
  • Watch for a long batting line. Use a continuous format or a 3-pitch max.
  • Year plan: June — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

A throwing and catching game that gets harder as players succeed. Partners face each other about 30 cm (1 foot) apart with a ball, or a water balloon outdoors. They toss back and forth. After a catch, the catcher steps back; after a miss, the player who missed steps forward. See how far apart pairs can get. Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Catch & Step for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Sends a catchable throw; ready hands on a catch.
  • Skill focus. Knows when to step back and when to step forward.
  • Skill focus. Takes turns throwing and catching with a partner.
  • Character Development. Honest about a catch or a drop; no arguing a step.
  • Safety. Throws stay away from faces; balloons stay in the pair’s square.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls ‘mine’ before the catch; encourages a partner after a drop.
Grades 1–2. Tee or a bounced toss. Run to one base or hoop and stay. Teacher fields the first few.
Grades 3–4. Live send (kick, strike, or throw). Three chances, then a run so the line moves.
Grades 5–6. Fielders call ‘mine.’ Rotate the strike job every three batters. Add a force-out at one base.

Safety. The ball is not thrown at a face. Balloons stay in the pair’s square. Tie extra balloons so they are not mouthed. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
