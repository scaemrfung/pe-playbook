33. Wolf’s Den

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · 2 wolves · 3 crossings, then reset.

Runners cross a middle zone past the wolves, and tagged players help tag for one crossing, then rejoin. Use it to practise changing speed with no one out for the period.

Equipment: Two end lines and a 3-m middle strip.

Set-up: Two wolves in the den. Everyone else on one end. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. On “Cross!” runners try to reach the far line without a tag.
  2. Tagged: 5 jumping jacks at the side, then help tag for one crossing only.
  3. Reset wolves every three crossings so the den does not fill forever.
  4. Rotate taggers every 45–90 seconds so the same three students are not chasing all period.
  5. A tagged player takes the next role or uses the game’s re-enter rule right away — nobody sits out.
  6. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Tag only in the den.
  • Jacks, then one turn as a helper.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.

Variations

  • Walk for 1–2.
  • Large-group setup from PHYSEDGAMES: This is the standard (width-of-the-gym) version for about 15–30 players. For 40–60 use Large Group Wolf’s Den instead.
  • Large-group (PHYSEDGAMES): run the den the LENGTH of the gym so 40–60 can play. More wolves. Rabbits run end to end. Pinnies on new wolves.
  • Walk-tag first, then jog. Add a second tagger only when space is clean.
  • Safe islands: three hoops that freeze a tagger for three seconds if a runner reaches one.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Wolf’s Den.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Cross on the call. Tagged help for one crossing. Reset wolves.
  • PHYSEDGAMES large-group setup lives on this card — no separate game.

More details: Large Group Wolf’s Den · Grades 1-6

Equipment: scarves (or pinnies)

A large-group version of Wolf’s Den. Depending on your space, it can work with 40 to 60 players, so it suits two or more classes together. It can also run outdoors, with cones marking the den. For a regular group of 15–30 players, the den usually runs across the width of the gym; for a large group, run it along the length.

  1. Mark a long, narrow den down the LENGTH of the gym (the large-group setup). Wolves must stay inside that strip.
  2. Pinny several wolves (more wolves for 40–60 players).
  3. Rabbits start on one end line. On GO they run through the den to the far end without being tagged.
  4. Two-finger tags. Tagged rabbits become wolves (put on a scarf) or visit a crate/jail until the next wave.
  5. After each wave, rabbits turn and run back through. Add an optional crate in the den for dropped scarves.
  6. Play 3–5 minutes, then choose new starting wolves. Outdoors: cone the den.

More details · Grades K-6

Equipment: None

The wolf moves around the den and tries to tag the rabbits as they run back and forth through it. Use lines or cones to mark the den in the middle, between two safe areas. The wolf must stay in the den. Rabbits run through the den from side to side without being tagged. A tagged rabbit becomes a wolf; keep pinnies handy so new wolves can put one on quickly. Play until all of the rabbits are caught. Works in the gym, outside or at camp.

  1. This is the standard (width-of-the-gym) version for about 15–30 players. For 40–60 use Large Group Wolf’s Den instead.
  2. Mark a den strip across the WIDTH of the gym. 2–4 wolves stay in the den.
  3. Rabbits start on one end, run through the den to the other end, then back on the next signal.
  4. Tagged rabbits become wolves or sit a turn. Wolves may not leave the den.
  5. Play several waves, then new wolves.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Wolf’s Den for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Crosses on the call.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Wolf’s Den.
  • Skill focus. Re-enters after the stretch.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Wolf’s Den.
  • Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head. Freeze on the whistle.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch, hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk-tag. Teacher plus one student tagger. 30–45 second bursts. Re-enter with a high-five or the game’s hospital rule.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Wolves may only tag in the den.

Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tags on the back or shoulder — never the head. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
