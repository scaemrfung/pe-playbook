9. Everybody’s It

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · Whole class · optional one soft ball or spot markers

Anyone may tag. A tag has a small cost. Then you keep playing.

Also called: Clothespin Tag · Hot Potato · Pizza Tag

Equipment: Optional one soft ball or spot markers · optional clothespins

Set-up: Court marked. Soft two-finger tags. Show the cost.

How we play

  1. On go, tag anyone.
  2. If two people tag at once, RPS. The slower pose does 3 squats. Both stay in.
  3. Keep playing until the whistle.

If this happens

  • Hard tag: warning, then sit 10 seconds.
  • Ball version thrown at a head: switch to hold-and-touch only.

How a round ends. Timed rounds. No winner required.

Cues

  • Everyone tags.
  • RPS, then both stay in.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Hot Potato: only the person holding the ball may tag, by touch, no throw.
  • Pizza: spot marker on one hand; knock other pizzas off.
  • Clothespin: steal one pin from a back, clip it on yourself.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Clothespin Tag; Hot Potato; Pizza Tag.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

More details · Grades 1-8

Equipment: None

A classic tag game. Everyone is it, and everyone can be tagged. A tagged player sits where they were tagged and stays down until the player who tagged them is tagged; then they get back up. Expect lots of running. If players sit too long, call “free pass” so they can get back up. If two players tag each other at the same time, they play Rock, Paper, Scissors to see who sits.

  1. Everybody is it AND everybody can be tagged. No equipment.
  2. If you are tagged, sit where you were tagged. You stay down until the person who tagged you gets tagged — then you stand up.
  3. Lots of running. If people sit too long, the teacher calls “free pass” and everyone stands.
  4. If two players tag each other at the same time, they play Rock-Paper-Scissors to see who sits.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Everybody’s It for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Moves and tags at the same time.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Everybody’s It.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Everybody’s It; does not crowd a hospital or jail.
  • Character Development. Does the squats and comes back.
  • Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head. Freeze on the whistle.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch, hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk-tag. Teacher plus one student tagger. 30–45 second bursts. Re-enter with a high-five or the game’s hospital rule.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Full speed with two-finger tags. Add one tactic (fake, safe-zone worth one use, or blob split). Students can referee a boundary.

Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tags on the back or shoulder — never the head. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher).
