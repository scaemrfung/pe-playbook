17. Mystery Number Tag

Tag & chase

When: December, September · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · 2-6 · 5–8 min rounds.

Hand out folded notes so a few students secretly become taggers when you say GO, then reshuffle the notes every 1–2 minutes. Use it to practise changing speed and safe tagging (idea from Chantal Dubois).

Equipment: sticky notes, or pieces of paper

Set-up: Prepare sticky notes. A few say IT (or a special number); the rest are blank/free. Use the basketball lines as the boundary; cones mark any safe zone. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Every player gets a folded note. They look only when you say GO.
  2. ITs tag. Runners flee. Tagged runners freeze until a free player high-fives them, or they become IT (pick one).
  3. After 1–2 minutes, reshuffle notes so new mystery taggers appear.
  4. Rotate taggers every 45–90 seconds so the same three students are not chasing all period.
  5. Prepare sticky notes.
  6. A few say IT (or a special number); the rest are blank/free.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Change pathway. Do not run someone over to make a tag.
  • If you are tagged, show it honestly and take the next job.
  • Blade or stick stays below the knee. No slapshots.

Variations

  • Walk-tag first, then jog. Add a second tagger only when space is clean.
  • Safe islands: three hoops that freeze a tagger for three seconds if a runner reaches one.
  • Tagged players become helpers (peelers, medics, or extra taggers) instead of sitting.
  • Feet-only hockey-ball version if sticks are new or the class is tired.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Mystery Number Tag.
  • Watch for head-down sprints and pile-ups at a safe zone. Pause, spread out, restart.
  • Sticks: teach grip and ‘blade down’ in the hallway line before the first dribble.
  • Year plan: December, September — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Mystery Number Tag for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Changes speed and pathway to avoid a tag in Mystery Number Tag.
  • Skill focus. Uses space, a fake, or a safe zone instead of a pile-up in Mystery Number Tag.
  • Skill focus. Takes a role and shares space in Mystery Number Tag; does not crowd a hospital or jail.
  • Character Development. Honest about a tag, a catch, or a line — no argument that stalls Mystery Number Tag.
  • Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tag on the back or shoulder — never the head.
  • Healthy Relationships. Helps a tagged classmate re-enter (high-five, stretch hospital, or role swap).
Grades 1–2. Walk-tag. Teacher plus one student tagger. 30–45 second bursts. Re-enter with a high-five or the game’s hospital rule. Feet-only if sticks are new.
Grades 3–4. Jog-tag. 3–5 taggers. 60–90 second rounds. Soft two-finger or noodle tags. Rotate every round.
Grades 5–6. Full speed with two-finger tags. Add one tactic (fake, safe-zone worth one use, or blob split). Students can referee a boundary.

Safety. Soft two-finger or noodle tags on the back or shoulder — never the head. Sticks stay below the knee. No slapshots. Blade on the floor when you stop. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
