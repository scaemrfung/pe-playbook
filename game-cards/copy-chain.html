193. Copy Chain

Cooperative & circus

When: December, October · Slot: 16–25 · Lines or circles of 6–10 · optional music

Copy what you saw, then add one move. A mix-up restarts the phrase.

Also called: Follow the Leader · Dance Whispers · Dance Chain · Dance Tag · Disco Stu’s Dance Tag

Equipment: Optional music

Set-up: Agree: mix-up restarts, nobody sits the period. Keep phrases to 4–8 counts.

How we play

  1. Person 1 shows one move.
  2. Person 2 copies it and adds one. Continue.
  3. If the order is lost, restart from that person. Celebrate mutations.

If this happens

  • Phrase becomes a 30-second routine: cut it down.
  • Someone shouts the moves ahead: start again silently.

How a round ends. Longest clean chain, or likeness score out of 5 if you passed it as a whisper.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Use names. Ask before you grab a hoop, rope, or balloon.
  • If it drops, restart together — no blame.
  • Quiet voices so the group can hear the next job.
  • One extra person in a hoop during a swap — no shoving out.

Skins — same game, different wrapper

  • Whisper down a line, last person performs.
  • Mirror Mirror: freeze in a pose, partner matches.
  • Dance Tag: start a clear dance before the tag to be safe.
  • Follow the Leader: one leader, class copies, swap often.

Teaching tips

  • Also called: Pass It On; Mirror Mirror; Follow the Leader; Dance Whispers; Dance Chain; Dance Tag; Disco Stu’s Dance Tag.
  • House rules: Freeze on the whistle. Tags are two-finger or noodle on the back or shoulder. A miss is a short task and you stay in. Extra players get a job (bumper, next wave, fetcher). No elimination.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Pass It On for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Uses the needed skill (pass, step-through, carry, balance) so the group can finish.
  • Skill focus. Plans a simple order or role with teammates before rushing.
  • Skill focus. The group finishes the task together.
  • Character Development. Restarts after a mistake without blaming a classmate.
  • Safety. Moves at a speed the group can control. Consent for partner shapes — a student may say no.
  • Healthy Relationships. Uses names and kind words; waits a turn without grabbing.
Grades 1–2. Small groups of 4–6. Teacher in the group if the task needs an extra pair of hands.
Grades 3–4. Whole-class or groups of 8. Plan for 20 seconds, then try.
Grades 5–6. Groups design one extra rule that still includes everyone, then teach it to another group.

Safety. Ask before you pull a hoop, rope, or teammate. Stop if hair, glasses, or a wrist is caught. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in.
