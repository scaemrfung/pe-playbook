53. Home Alone

Invasion & team

When: October, January · Slot: 16–25 · Large group · K-4 · 5–8 min rounds.

Each homeowner guards a cone key in a hoop while thieves try to steal it without being tagged. Use it to practise awareness, attacking and defence.

Equipment: Hoops, cones

Set-up: Lay out about 8 hoops. One player stands in each hoop with a cone — that cone is the KEY to the house. They must protect it. Pinnies on one team. Show the two attacking directions before GO. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Everyone else is a thief and tries to steal a key without being tagged by the player in that hoop.
  2. If tagged, try a different house. If the steal succeeds, thief and homeowner swap roles.
  3. House players may tag only while keeping one foot in (or next to) their hoop.
  4. Play short rounds so many students get to be homeowners.
  5. Play 4–6 minute periods. Extra players are bumpers, walls, or the next wave — they stay in a job.
  6. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Head up. Pass or move — do not crowd the ball.
  • Find a passing lane, not a pile-up.
  • Soft send; the ball stays below the chest unless the sport needs a high set.

Variations

  • Must complete one pass before a shot or a score.
  • Add a second foam ball if more than 24 students so more players get a touch.
  • Extra players are walking walls — they may deflect a ball with feet but may not score.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Home Alone.
  • Watch for one student holding the ball. Cue a pass or a 3-second pivot.
  • Year plan: October, January — match the month’s sport ball when you can.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Home Alone for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Sends, receives, or travels with control using the taught skill (foot, hand, or implement).
  • Skill focus. Uses space: spread out, fake, or change speed to get past a defender.
  • Skill focus. Honest tags; no puppy-guarding a key or hoop.
  • Character Development. Honest score and honest tags; tries again after a miss.
  • Safety. Two-finger or noodle tag.
  • Healthy Relationships. Calls a teammate’s name; includes a player who has not had a turn as homeowner.
Grades 1–2. Small space, big targets, walking or jogging. Teacher may be a walking bumper. Hands may help a trap if the ball is soccer.
Grades 3–4. Full game as written, foam or real sport ball as the unit says. One pass before a score if the class crowds.
Grades 5–6. Student captains or officials. Offside-lite or a must-pass rule. Keep score without ranking classmates.

Safety. No body checks. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
