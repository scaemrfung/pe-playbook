104. Slide Sling Save

Target & send

When: January, March · Slot: 16–25 · Two teams · 6 min · beanbags.

Students slide beanbags at the other team’s feet, and a hit player returns by catching a ball thrown by a teammate. Use it to practise sliding, throwing and catching.

Equipment: Beanbags, dodgeballs, two jail hoops.

Set-up: Two halves. Slide from behind a line. Agree the freeze signal before the first round.

How we play

  1. Slide beanbags at the other team’s feet.
  2. A hit player walks to the far jail hoop.
  3. A teammate throws a dodgeball for them to catch — a catch brings them back.
  4. Balls come back along the side. Restart on a teacher call, not a scramble through the middle.
  5. Freeze on the whistle. Reset spots, then the next round.

Cues

  • Slide at feet.
  • A hit walks to jail; a teammate catch frees you.
  • Freeze on the whistle — then eyes on the teacher.
  • Opposite-foot step. Eyes on the target, not the teacher.
  • Wait for the fetch before the next throw.
  • One object in the air at a time per pair or lane.

Variations

  • Must bounce once.
  • Step back one giant step after each make (grades 3–6).
  • Opposite hand or weaker foot for the last round.
  • Team score is ‘makes in two minutes’ — not a ranking of throwers.

Teaching tips

  • Reset: freeze, walk back to start spots, change the key role, then GO. Two or three short rounds beat one long messy one in Slide Sling Save.
  • Watch for fetchers walking through the throwing line. Make them a job to the side.
  • Year plan: January, March — match the month’s sport ball when you can.
  • Slide beanbags along the floor at feet. A hit player walks to a jail hoop.
  • A teammate may catch a bag to free one jailed player.

Alberta PEW outcomes

Pick one or two look-fors per class.

  • Active Living. Stays in Slide Sling Save for the set time; can name breath or enjoyment after — not a ranking.
  • Movement Skill Development. Dodgeballs stay below the waist; a catch is a save.
  • Skill focus. Chooses a target and a force — close and soft, or far and firm — not a wild throw.
  • Skill focus. Waits a turn; collects on the call so the next player can send.
  • Character Development. Records or resets honestly; a miss is another try.
  • Safety. Hits stay low; a hit is a stretch, then back in.
  • Healthy Relationships. Cheers a classmate’s send; does not block someone from a turn.
Grades 1–2. Short throwing line. Underhand or roll is allowed. Teacher fetches with the first group.
Grades 3–4. Standard distance. Opposite-foot step. Fetchers stay to the side.
Grades 5–6. Step back after a make. Weaker hand or weaker foot on the last round. A student official on the line.

Safety. Slides on the floor only. Hits at feet, not shins with force. No elimination: a sit is a stretch, a jail, a hospital, or a role swap, then back in. Freeze on the whistle. Watch a demo or clip before the first play if you have one.
