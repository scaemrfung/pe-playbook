How to use this handbook

  • Every game includes grade band, equipment, a short overview from the original page, numbered how-to steps, the YouTube explanation, and the source URL.
  • Safety defaults for all throwing/kicking games: foam or soft balls, tags below the shoulders, no head shots, two-finger or noodle tags (no grabbing).
  • Dodgeball is optional programming — several pages on the source site note it is not a fit for every group. Prefer Detective, Net, Bench, or Prison variations when you want more students moving.
  • Large-group in this catalog often means doubled classes (about 40–60) if you have the space. Shrink boundaries and tagger counts for a single class.
  • Games and videos are created by PHYSEDGAMES and contributing teachers. This handbook is an organized classroom reference with links back to those originals.

Tag & Chasing

Game 01

Backyard Dog Tag

Grades: K-4  ·  Equipment: Cones, 4 foam balls

Some students are dogs (they can act like a dog if they want). The dogs are trying to chase people away from their yard. Another fun tag game idea to try in your PE class (thanks to Deric Hafer).

How to play

  1. Cone off several small “yards.” Place a foam ball (bone/toy) in each yard if you want a steal object.
  2. Assign one dog per yard. Dogs may act like dogs but stay in or right beside their own yard.
  3. The rest of the class are visitors trying to sneak into a yard (and optionally steal the bone) without being tagged.
  4. A tagged visitor leaves that yard. Optional swap: the tagged player becomes the new dog.
  5. Play 45–90 second rounds and rotate dogs often.

Game 02

Massive 10 Second Tag

Grades: 1-6  ·  Equipment: none

Tried a quick modification of Joel Sweetland’s ’10-second tag’ for a large group that I had. Wasn’t sure if it’d work but it went really good! Note: the cycles don’t have to be exactly 10 seconds (to keep the flow of the game going, you might add or subtract a few seconds here or there).

How to play

  1. Everyone spreads out. On GO, EVERYONE is it for about 10 seconds.
  2. Gentle two-finger shoulder tags only. You can tag others even if you get tagged during the burst.
  3. Whistle ends the burst. Anyone tagged in that burst sits or takes a knee.
  4. Start the next burst with whoever is still standing. Continue until a few remain, then reset sitters back in.
  5. Bursts can be 7–12 seconds — keep the flow, not the stopwatch. Split the gym if numbers are huge.

Game 03

Angry Neighbour

Grades: 1-5  ·  Equipment: pool noodles, exercise mats

This game is quick and easy and angry. For some reason, the neighbours (taggers) are not happy with the players on their yard, so they chase them around with a broomstick. At least that’s how the story goes. A good game for some laughs and quality exercise. Thanks James Barton for another game idea!

How to play

  1. Lay exercise mats as houses along one side. The open floor is the yard.
  2. Give 3–6 “neighbours” a pool noodle (the broom).
  3. Players start in the yard. Neighbours chase and tag with the noodle (legs/hips).
  4. Tagged players go to a house mat. Re-enter on a teacher call (“new yard!”) or a teammate high-five — pick one rule.
  5. Rotate neighbours every 60–90 seconds.

Game 04

Large Group Wolf’s Den

Grades: 1-6  ·  Equipment: scarves (or pinnies)

This is now my new favourite LARGE GROUP game. Depending on your space, it could work with 40, 50, maybe even 60 players. Not sure why I’ve never done it this way after all these years, but I will definitely more often use this when I have 2 or more classes doubled up. It could even be done outdoors in a large area and throw done cones to form a wolf’s den. I can personally vouch that I’ve had an awesome time with this game recently with different age groups doubled up. Hope you enjoy a ‘new’ version of an old favourite. And if it’s a completely new game for you, it’s normally played with the den along the width of the gym, instead of along the length (the set-up for a regular group of 15-30 players).

How to play

  1. Mark a long, narrow den down the LENGTH of the gym (the large-group setup). Wolves must stay inside that strip.
  2. Pinny several wolves (more wolves for 40–60 players).
  3. Rabbits start on one end line. On GO they run through the den to the far end without being tagged.
  4. Two-finger tags. Tagged rabbits become wolves (put on a scarf) or visit a crate/jail until the next wave.
  5. After each wave, rabbits turn and run back through. Add an optional crate in the den for dropped scarves.
  6. Play 3–5 minutes, then choose new starting wolves. Outdoors: cone the den.

Game 05

Wolf’s Den

Grades: K-6  ·  Equipment: None

Watch out for the wolves! In this game, the wolf roams around his den trying to catch the rabbits as they run back-and-forth through the wolf’s territory. Action-packed and always great for a laugh in PE class, outside, or at camp. Using lines or cones as markers, create a wolf’s den area right in the middle of two safe areas. The wolf must stay in his den. The rabbits attempt to run through the den from side to side without getting tagged by the wolf. If a rabbit gets tagged, he becomes a wolf. A good idea is to have a stash of pinnies so that when a rabbit is tagged, he first quickly puts on a pinnie so you can distinguish who is a wolf and who is a rabbit. Play until all of the rabbits have been caught!

How to play

  1. This is the standard (width-of-the-gym) version for about 15–30 players. For 40–60 use Large Group Wolf’s Den instead.
  2. Mark a den strip across the WIDTH of the gym. 2–4 wolves stay in the den.
  3. Rabbits start on one end, run through the den to the other end, then back on the next signal.
  4. Tagged rabbits become wolves or sit a turn. Wolves may not leave the den.
  5. Play several waves, then new wolves.

Game 06

Mystery Number Tag

Grades: 2-6  ·  Equipment: sticky notes, or pieces of paper

This game is a mystery! Players don’t know who the catchers are. But they’ll soon find out. Try this game out for lots of laughs and lots of running! It’s another game that will not disappoint! (Thanks Chantal Dubois)

How to play

  1. Prepare sticky notes. A few say IT (or a special number); the rest are blank/free.
  2. Every player gets a folded note. They look only when you say GO.
  3. ITs tag. Runners flee. Tagged runners freeze until a free player high-fives them, or they become IT (pick one).
  4. After 1–2 minutes, reshuffle notes so new mystery taggers appear.

Game 07

Home Free Bad Guy Blob Tag

Grades: 1-6  ·  Equipment: Hula hoops, pool noodles

What a great game, seriously one of the best tag games out there. This is a must play. Lots of Dynamics, lots going on in this unique idea thanks to Dan Penna for another hit.

How to play

  1. Scatter hoops as short-stay “home free” bases (about 3 seconds max — no camping).
  2. 2–4 bad guys with pool noodles tag runners.
  3. A tagged runner joins the blob by holding hands/wrists with a bad guy. Only noodle ends tag; the chain may not break.
  4. Optional rescue: a free runner at a hoop calls HOME FREE and releases one blob player.
  5. Reset when the blob is huge or after 2–3 minutes.

Game 08

Freedom Catch

Grades: 1-6  ·  Equipment: Cones, foam balls

New favourite activity! Tagging, throwing, catching, strategy all jam-packed into one awesome game from Jennifer Holub. Really fun, a must try, especially if you’re looking for a game for large groups (50 players +).

How to play

  1. Large coned field. Several taggers; free players have foam balls.
  2. Taggers freeze runners in place with hands ready to catch.
  3. A teammate frees a frozen player by completing a catch to them.
  4. A drop leaves the player frozen (or freezes the thrower — pick one and teach it).
  5. Timed rounds; rotate taggers. Built for 50+ if space is big.

Game 09

Everybody’s It

Grades: 1-8  ·  Equipment: None

‘Everybody’s It’ is a classic all-time favorite tag game with non-stop action! Everybody is it, and everybody can also get tagged. If a player gets tagged, he must sit where he was tagged.  He is stuck down until the player who tagged him gets tagged, and then he’s back up.  It’s that simple!  This is a very active game; lots of running. If players are sitting too long then the teacher can yell out, “free pass” and then they can get back up. If players tag eachother at the same time then they play a round of ‘Rock, Paper, Scissors’ to see who sits. Players spread out around the gym. Everyone is ‘It’ but everyone can also get tagged. Players sit if tagged. Players get back up when player who tagged them gets tagged. If a player is sitting too long, give a ‘free pass’ so he can get back up. If two players tag each other at the same time, they play ‘Rock, Paper, Scissors’ to see who sits.

How to play

  1. Everybody is it AND everybody can be tagged. No equipment.
  2. If you are tagged, sit where you were tagged. You stay down until the person who tagged you gets tagged — then you stand up.
  3. Lots of running. If people sit too long, the teacher calls “free pass” and everyone stands.
  4. If two players tag each other at the same time, they play Rock-Paper-Scissors to see who sits.

Game 10

Dead Ant Tag

Grades: K-5  ·  Equipment: None

Hilarious idea for a simple tag game: if a player gets tagged, he must turn over on his back and put arms and legs up in the air like a dead ant on its back. To get back up? 4 players who aren’t tagged must attach themselves to an arm or leg and then that player is free. Tagger can’t guard or tag those who are helping. This is a great game to use with large groups of students. Give it a go!  Everyone spreads out in playing area. Choose a couple of taggers. When tagged, player turns into a dead ant. 4 other players go and save that ant by attaching to the limbs.

How to play

  1. Choose 3–6 taggers. Everyone else runs.
  2. A tagged player lies on their back with arms and legs in the air like a dead ant.
  3. To free them, FOUR untagged players must each hold one arm or one leg. Then the ant stands and all five are free.
  4. If a rescuer gets tagged on the way, they become a dead ant too.
  5. Rotate taggers often.

Game 11

Banana Tag

Grades: K-5  ·  Equipment: None

Such a simple tag game; kids go bananas, literally! As players get tagged, they must curve their bodies into the shape of a banana. Someone else will come along and ‘peel’ them so that they are free.

How to play

  1. Choose taggers. Everyone else is a runner.
  2. A tagged player must curve their body into a banana shape and hold it.
  3. A free teammate “peels” them (a two-hand sweep down the sides, agreed and appropriate) to free them.
  4. Rotate taggers. Keep tags gentle.

Game 12

Fruit Salad

Grades: K-5  ·  Equipment: Pinnies (different colors)

Fruit salad is a tag game for kindergarten to grade 5. Give out different color pinnies to the taggers and ask the students to name fruits that are the same colors of the pinnies. If students get tagged by a certain color then they freeze into the shape of the particular fruit that matches the color. As a class you can decide upon different ideas of how to freeze into a particular fruit. The class also determines how to unfreeze the different pieces of fruit so there are lots of different kinds of actions and they can make up a new version almost every time. Examples: Apple – Cut, Banana – Peel, Blueberry – Wash, Watermelon – Roll, etc. Try it out with the VEGETABLE theme as well! Move and learn at the same time with the Fruit Salad physical education game. (Thanks to Curtis Glasgow for this game idea)

How to play

  1. Give taggers different-coloured pinnies. As a class, name a fruit for each colour and a freeze shape for that fruit (apple = round; banana = curve).
  2. Also agree how each fruit gets unfrozen (apple is “cut,” banana is “peeled,” blueberry is “washed,” watermelon is “rolled,” etc.).
  3. Taggers tag. A tagged player freezes in that fruit’s shape until the matching unfreeze action is done by a free teammate.
  4. Play short rounds and let the class invent a vegetable version next time.

Game 13

Reindeer Roundup

Grades: K-6  ·  Equipment: Hula hoops, pool noodles

A Christmas game for the holidays (thanks to Angela Crepeele).

How to play

  1. Scatter hula hoops as stables. Give 3–6 elves / herders pool noodles.
  2. Everyone else is a reindeer and gallops around the gym.
  3. Elves tag reindeer with a noodle touch on the hip or shoulder (not the head).
  4. A tagged reindeer must stand inside the nearest hoop.
  5. Free reindeer may rescue by joining hands and galloping a captured reindeer out of the hoop without being tagged.
  6. Play 1–2 minute rounds and switch elves. Holiday music optional.

Game 14

Bench Dodgeball

Grades: 4-8  ·  Equipment: benches, dodgeballs

It’s the classic game of bench dodgeball, at least I think it’s a classic. It might just be classic for me? Note: this game is different from BENCHBALL (where you try to get all players onto the bench – that’s not a dodgeball game, though it’s also a really good game). In this game, you try to help your players get OFF the bench. Mrs. Gracie at my school really likes to play this game when I’m away. The kids really like it and of course, it works on lots of skills!

How to play

  1. Place a bench on each end line. Two teams, one half each. Foam dodgeballs only.
  2. This is NOT Bench Ball. Here you are trying to get your own players OFF the bench.
  3. When you legally hit an opponent (below the shoulders), one teammate on your bench may re-enter.
  4. A player who is hit goes to their own bench and waits to be freed.
  5. Play timed rounds. Winner: more live players at the whistle, or first team to empty its bench.

Game 15

Backboard Dodgeball

Grades: 4-8  ·  Equipment: basketball backboards, dodgeballs

Highly requested game of dodgeball. Lots of fun, and lots of skills as usual! Two teams faceoff in this fun version that uses the basketball court (full court or half court), thanks to Michelle Weaver!

How to play

  1. Two teams on a basketball court (full or half). Foam dodgeballs.
  2. Play regular throwing/dodging on the floor. Hits below the shoulders.
  3. Bonus: a throw that hits the opponent’s backboard (or goes in the hoop — choose) returns all of your out players or scores a point.
  4. Hit players wait on the sideline until a bonus or a catch brings them back.
  5. Timed games keep sitting-out time short.

Game 16

Prison Ball

Grades: 4-8  ·  Equipment: Foam Balls

Prison ball is a dodgeball-type game where 2 teams face-off against each other. Along the ends of the gym, behind each team is a prison area. Whenever a player gets hit, he or she must head to prison behind the opposite team on that end. They are stuck there until they can throw a ball and successfully hit someone while they’re in prison — and then they can return home to their team, while the player they hit must then go to prison! Very interesting type of dodgeball game where players must be aware of their complete surroundings, front, back, sides. Balls flying from all over the place makes it challenging and lots of fun!

How to play

  1. Two teams, each half. Behind each end is a PRISON zone.
  2. If you are hit with a foam ball (below shoulders), go to the prison BEHIND the other team.
  3. From prison you may throw at the team in front of you. If you hit someone, you return home and they go to prison.
  4. Balls fly from front and behind, so players must check all directions.
  5. Play timed games. Foam balls only.

Game 17

Battleball

Grades: 1-8  ·  Equipment: 4 foam balls

This is a special type of dodgeball game with official rules, refs, and the whole show! With sportsmanship being the number 1 rule, teams will compete in an epic battle to be the last ones standing. Great game incorporating lots of skills. Popular in Australia, kids love this one and get nice and sweaty. There’s talk of some major tournament play and school-wide fun. Try it out! Also see the official rules manual (WORD DOCUMENT DOWNLOAD) posted below: BATTLEBALL RULES 2014

How to play

  1. Two teams, 4 foam balls, official-style dodgeball with sportsmanship as rule #1.
  2. Agree on refs (teachers or trained students) and the school’s hit / catch / boundary rules before the first throw.
  3. Play to last players standing or a time cap with “all out players return” resets so nobody sits long.
  4. Download the posted rules sheet from the game page if you want the full tournament version: https://physedgames.com/i/BATTLEBALL-RULES-2014.doc
  5. Keep it recreational: foam balls, no head shots, no targeting from close range.

Game 18

Net Dodgeball

Grades: 3-8  ·  Equipment: Dodgeballs, nets/goals

Net dodgeball is basically a standard game of dodgeball, except that each team also has a net and a goalie. Any time a team scores a goal by throwing a dodgeball past the opposing goalie, all of the players who were out get to enter back into the game. Also use detective dodgeball rules to ensure players are getting maximum participation and movement time…if you’re unsure what detective dodgeball rules are, find the rules to that game on this site!

How to play

  1. Standard two-team foam dodgeball, PLUS a net and a goalie on each end.
  2. If a team throws a ball past the other goalie into the net, ALL of that team’s out players come back in.
  3. Use Detective Dodgeball re-entry if you also want hit-players tracking who hit them.
  4. Goalies may use hands; field players follow your regular dodgeball rules.

Game 19

Warzone Dodgeball

Grades: 4-8  ·  Equipment: Dodgeballs, ‘walls’ (mats, benches)

Warzone Dodgeball is definitely what the name says – a dodgeball warzone. Also known as ‘Paintball Dodgeball’, to set up for this game, place some obstacles and barriers for players to hide behind. Things like mats and tubes that represent walls and trenches. Then let the teams go at it. Add in a Capture the Flag element to further increase the intensity. At physedgames, we realize that dodgeball games may not be an acceptable game for all groups.

How to play

  1. Build cover with mats, benches, and safe “walls” / trenches. Two teams. Also called Paintball Dodgeball.
  2. Play foam dodgeball using the barriers as hiding spots. No moving the walls into people.
  3. Optional: add a Capture-the-Flag object in each back zone to raise the objective beyond just getting people out.
  4. Not every school community wants dodgeball — choose this only if it fits your program. Foam balls, no head shots.

Game 20

Standard Dodgeball

Grades: 2-8  ·  Equipment: Dodgeballs

Standard dodgeball is the classic version of the game. In our opinion, many of the variations of this game are huge improvements and should be played in place of the standard version, however, here it is for you to take a look at.

How to play

  1. Two teams, one half each. Foam dodgeballs in the middle. On the whistle, rush the balls.
  2. Hit an opponent below the shoulders to send them out. A caught ball typically sends the thrower out and can return a teammate — confirm your house rules.
  3. No crossing the mid-line. Play until one team is eliminated, then reset.
  4. PHYSEDGAMES notes that many variations keep more kids moving and are often a better daily choice.

Game 21

Detective Dodgeball

Grades: 4-8  ·  Equipment: Dodgeballs

This is a dodgeball game where if a player is hit, he must remember who hit him, because he’s out until the player who hit him gets hit. That’s the whole detective part. A great advancement to regular dodgeball to keep things flowing better. Create 2 teams, each on one half of the gym. Add in the dodgeballs. When a player is hit, he must go off to the side. He must remember who got him out, because when that person gets hit, he is able to return to play. If players are sitting out for too long, give everyone a ‘free pass’ back in the game. When one team has no players left, start a new round! This has been the favorite dodgeball game of choice for many grade 8 classes.

How to play

  1. Two teams, each on one half. Foam dodgeballs in play.
  2. If you are hit, go to the side and REMEMBER who hit you. You are out only until THAT person is hit, then you come back in.
  3. That “detective” memory rule keeps the game flowing and cuts long sit-outs.
  4. If anyone sits too long, call a free pass and everyone returns.
  5. When one team has nobody left on the floor, start a new round. A favourite for older grades.

Game 22

Pin Knockover

Grades: 2-8  ·  Equipment: Plastic bowling pins, dodgeballs

Pin Knockover is a classic target throwing or rolling game. Two teams match-up on each side of the gym and be the first team to knock over all of the opposing teams pins. Other skills involved are blocking, goaltending, running, underhand, overhand, ducking, etc. Great team game for development and enjoyment. Create 2 teams, each on one half of the gym. Set-up an equal amount of pins on both sides. Those are the targets. Use a line in front of the pins where students can’t ‘puppy guard’ behind. Add in the dodgeballs. First team to knock over all the other teams pins wins. If a player accidentally knocks over his own pin, too bad. Play over and over again!

How to play

  1. Two teams, each with a row of plastic pins on its end. Foam dodgeballs.
  2. Throw or roll to knock down the other team’s pins. First team to knock all opponent pins wins, or most pins down at time.
  3. Players may block and “goal tend” in front of their own pins. No crossing mid-line if you want a throwing-only game.
  4. Reset pins and play again. Skills: underhand, overhand, blocking, ducking.

Game 23

Powerball

Grades: 4-8  ·  Equipment: 3 exercise balls, dodgeballs

Powerball is intense. It’s a target throwing game to get the heart-rate and excitement up! Teams throw dodgeballs at large exercise balls in order to push them passed the opposing teams goal line. Points are scored everytime that happens. Try it out sometime! Create 2 teams on the sides of the gym at the lines. Place exercise balls or other in the middle as targets. Give players dodgeballs. Players throw dodgeballs at the exercise balls in order to push them across the opposite line to score points. Play for determined amount of time or score.

How to play

  1. Place 3 large exercise balls on a centre strip. Two teams on opposite halves with foam dodgeballs.
  2. Throw dodgeballs at the exercise balls to push them over the other team’s goal line.
  3. Each exercise ball that fully crosses the opponent goal line scores a point. Roll it back to centre and continue.
  4. No kicking the exercise balls unless you add that as a house rule. Watch toes and pile-ups.

Game 24

End Wall Soccer

Grades: 3-8  ·  Equipment: foam soccer ball, pinnies

A fun type of soccer game, can be especially good with large groups (thanks CIRA Ontario for this idea: https://www.ciraontario.com/)!

How to play

  1. Make two teams (pinnies on one side). Play the full gym or a large field.
  2. Each team’s GOAL is the entire end wall / end line — no small net required. That is what makes this work with a crowd.
  3. Play soccer rules with a foam soccer ball: feet only. Decide in advance whether a goalie is allowed to use hands.
  4. A goal counts when the ball hits the opponent’s end wall or fully crosses the end line (choose one and stick to it).
  5. Restart at centre after each goal. Add a second foam ball if numbers are very high so more players get touches.
  6. Play 4–6 minute periods and rotate roles.

Game 25

Sideline Soccer

Grades: 2-8  ·  Equipment: Foam Balls

In sideline soccer, teams become goalies along their respective sidelines, using their best goaltending skills. Imagine the set-up as big lines of goalies stretching across the entire length of the sidelines – for each team. Meanwhile the first 5 players from each team enter the playing area from the front of their line to play some soccer (choose 2-3 balls to use all at the same time). Whenever a goal is scored on a team, just put the ball back into play and keep going – no need to stop the play for anything! After 2 minutes blow the whistle and the 5 players from each team that were on the playing area will go to the back of their teams line of goalies and 5 new players who were at the front of the line enter in as players. Great game to play to work on all the soccer (football) skills. Also a great game for large groups.

How to play

  1. Each team lines its entire sideline as a wall of goalies.
  2. The first 5 players from each line step onto the floor and play soccer with 2–3 foam balls at once.
  3. If a ball is kicked through a sideline, that is a save / miss — put it straight back in. Do not stop play for celebrations.
  4. Every 2 minutes, the 5 floor players go to the BACK of their goalie line and the next 5 step on.
  5. Works well for large groups because everyone is either playing or goalkeeping.

Game 38

Strikeball is a great little game to work on aspects of goaltending, fielding, striking, and reaction time. Game 49

Fitness Musical Hoops

Grades: 2-8  ·  Equipment: Hula hoops, music

In this fitness or warm-up game, hula hoops are placed around the basketball court perimeter. Choose a direction for players to travel: clockwise or counter clockwise staying on the outside of the basketball court line. Players cannot cross through center. Once the music starts they perform the cardio exercise of the teacher’s choice: jogging, running, skipping, cross-overs, etc. When the music stops they must get to a hoop within a few seconds. How to play

  3. Music off: everyone must stand inside a hoop (all feet in) within a few seconds. Hoops may be shared.
  4. If anyone is not in a hoop, the WHOLE class does a muscular exercise (e.g. push-ups).
  5. When music restarts, remove one hoop. Repeat until one hoop remains. Then use a lighter consequence (e.g. 2 jumping jacks per person left out).

Etc, etc, keep taking hoops away and eventually students start crowding eachothers personal space. Great for laughs and getting sweaty!

How to play

  1. Start with many hoops on the floor and music playing. Students travel among the hoops.
  2. When music stops, every student must jump into a hoop (sharing allowed).
  3. Remove hoops each round. Space gets tighter — talk about personal space and consent before bodies get close.
  4. When few hoops remain, end the game rather than forcing an unsafe squash. Use a fitness challenge instead of elimination.

Game 55

The Video Camera Game

Grades: K-5  ·  Equipment: None

Very simple warm-up game with a simple idea: the teacher calls out familiar actions that you would find on a video camera – play, stop, rewind, fast forward, etc. For each word that’s called out, the students will have to perform the related action within the playing area. Call random orders and mix it up! Switch leaders after awhile too to create their own patterns or sequences. Here’s what we have to get you started: Play – walk around Rewind – travel backwards Pause – jump! Fast forward – run Stop – stop movement Slow motion – slow movement Delete – face down lying flat on the floor Can you think of some other actions?

How to play

  1. Teach the camera words and actions, for example: PLAY = jog forward; STOP = freeze; REWIND = run backward; FAST FORWARD = sprint; PAUSE = freeze in a pose; SLOW MOTION = exaggerated slow move; RECORD = wave an imaginary camera.
  2. Caller mixes the words. Students perform the matching action in the playing area.
  3. After a minute, switch callers so students invent sequences.
  4. Use as a K–5 warm-up.

Game 56

Animals

Grades: K-2  ·  Equipment: None

Such a simple warm-up game! Choose a theme – for example, the jungle. Then students can choose a jungle animal that they will pretend to be, perhaps a monkey or parrot, and spend a minute moving around like that animal, making the animal noises. After a minute, change the theme. For example, the arctic. Students will then choose an arctic animal like a penguin or seal and do the same thing.

How to play

  1. Call a habitat theme (jungle, arctic, farm, ocean).
  2. Each student picks an animal from that habitat and moves + makes the sound for about one minute.
  3. Change the theme and repeat. Encourage different levels (high bird, low snake) and speeds.
  4. K–2 warm-up; no equipment.

Game 57

Red Line

Grades: K-6  ·  Equipment: Cones, balls

Here’s a unique commands and directions game, with a ‘race’ and competition element, can be used pretty much through all the grade levels (thanks James Barton)!

How to play

  1. Use court lines or cone several parallel lines. Students start on a baseline.
  2. Teach a small set of commands (example: “red line,” “back,” “spin,” “ball”).
  3. Call commands in mixed order. Students race to the correct line or perform the action.
  4. Add a team race: first team to finish a sequence correctly scores.
  5. For older students add two-part commands or a fitness hold on arrival.

Game 58

Oyster Shell

Grades: 2-8  ·  Equipment: 1 beanbag, floor lines (or cones)

Here’s a fun little reacting and running competition that can basically be used for all ages. You don’t need much for equipment either. Split the group into 2 teams, they don’t even have to be even numbers. If you’ve got cones, you could try it outside too!

How to play

  1. Two teams on opposite sidelines facing a centre line. Teams do not need even numbers.
  2. Place one beanbag on the centre line (the oyster).
  3. Number the players on each side. Call a number; those two sprint to the beanbag.
  4. Whoever grabs it tries to run it home across their sideline. The other player tries to tag them.
  5. 1 point for a successful return OR 1 point for a tag. Reset and call a new number. Call two numbers for 2-v-2.

Team-building & Cooperative

Game 59

Ice Cream Cone Relays

Grades: K-4  ·  Equipment: foam balls, cones

Here are 3 relay ideas with an ice cream theme to use as a team-building, stand-alone, or field day event!

How to play

  1. Split the class into even relay teams. Give each team a home marker and foam balls (scoops) plus cones (the waffle cones).
  2. Relay A — Scoop stack: Runner 1 carries one foam ball to the far cone, balances it as a scoop, and sprints back to tag the next player. First team to stack the set number of scoops without a collapse wins.
  3. Relay B — Build the cone: Cones start nested at home. Runners take turns carrying one cone out and stacking a taller ice-cream cone at the far end. Rebuild if it falls.
  4. Relay C — Take the order: Teacher calls an order (“3 scoops”). The runner must carry that many balls in one trip, place them on the cone, and return.
  5. Change locomotor skills between relays (run, skip, gallop). Use as a field-day rotation or a single lesson.

Game 60

Cooperative Wall Ball

Grades: 1-8  ·  Equipment: ball, exercise ball

Here’s a fun teamwork game. It’s very easy to play, and it’s great to use to for a fun cooperative experience. There are actually 3 ways to play, each with a varying level of difficulty. Start by asking your players to explain what cooperation means. And they will soon realize that if they work together, they will accomplish the task quickly and effectively!

How to play

  1. Ask the class what cooperation looks like and sounds like.
  2. Level 1: Keep a playground ball going against a wall for a target number of hits. Drop = restart the count together.
  3. Level 2: Same task, but no player may touch the ball twice in a row.
  4. Level 3: Switch to a large exercise ball that the group must control off the wall together.
  5. Time attempts. Celebrate the group streak, not star players. Split a huge class into wall groups.

Game 61

Pass It On

Grades: 2-8  ·  Equipment: Hula hoops, various items

This team-building game is great for any physical education class. Players will work together to pass objects to each other to be the first team to successfully move all objects from one end to the other. There’s a catch though…players are must all lay down on their backs and pass only with their feet. Create teams of even numbers. In their teams, players lay down on their backs and form a line, head to toe. One hula hoop is placed at the foot of the first person in line. This hula hoop contains random objects to be moved. One hula hoop is placed at the head of the last person in line. This hula hoop is the collection bin. On the signal, players work together to be the first team to successfully pass all of their objects from one hoop to the other by only using their feet. Play as many times as desired.

How to play

  1. Teams lie on their backs in a line from one end of the gym toward the other.
  2. Objects start at one end. Players may pass ONLY with their feet — no hands.
  3. Move every object down the line into a hoop or pile at the far end.
  4. First team to move all objects wins. If an item touches a hand or the floor off-plan, restart that item.
  5. Great team-builder; mix item sizes to change difficulty.

Game 62

Hoop Pass

Grades: K-8  ·  Equipment: Hula Hoop

Team-building game. A group holds hands in a circle or line and passes a hula hoop from player to player without breaking the chain-link. Not as easy as it sounds! Time trials, team vs. team, or large group are all fun ways to play this game.

How to play

  1. A group holds hands in a circle or line. A hula hoop starts on one person’s arm.
  2. Pass the hoop around the group WITHOUT letting go of hands — step through, wriggle it over bodies.
  3. Time trials, team vs team, or one giant class circle all work.
  4. If the chain breaks, restart the hoop at the break. Talk about communication.

Game 63

The Great Outdoor Treasure Hunt

Grades: K-8  ·  Equipment: Nature

What better way to use outdoor space than a scavenger hunt!? This hunt is a bit different from your typical hunt, because instead of a list that players need to just check off when they see the items, they must BRING THE ITEMS HOME to their collection area. You can and should create a secret treasure item that you’ve hidden somewhere outside beforehand (for example hide a Kleenex box somewhere good and players need to also retrieve a tissue from the box). Simply start by creating a home area where individuals or partners (depending how you want to do it) put their hula hoop on the ground, which is their collection area to bring the items into. Next, go over the rules with all students, and give them a list of the items (or leave a master poster at the home area so they have to use their memory). Note that they can only bring 1 item back at a time! Obviously choose items that students can find or access in your area/community. Stress the importance of respecting property and not taking things that they shouldn’t. At the end, players will return all the items that need to be returned!

How to play

  1. Each student or pair places a hoop as HOME / collection zone.
  2. Give a list of natural or outdoor items they may collect (or post one master list at home base). Hide one secret “treasure” (example: a tissue from a hidden box).
  3. Players may carry only ONE item home at a time.
  4. Respect property: take only what you listed; no harming plants or taking things that are not allowed.
  5. At the end, return every item that should be returned. Count collections and who found the secret treasure.

Invasion, Capture & Role Games

Game 64

Capture The Flag Outdoors

Grades: 3-8  ·  Equipment: 2 Flags, large playing area

This has been the GAME OF THE YEAR at our school. LOTS of Fun, many skills and laughs have been had. Competition at its finest, and what a workout. It’s the standard version of Capture The Flag brought to physical education class! Find a large area (field, community center, playground, bushes, forest), make 2 teams, and get started. Teams spend a minute hiding their flag on their half of the area (no peeking). Once hidden, GO! The goal of the game is to capture the other teams flag before they capture yours and bring it across the middle line. Careful on the opponents side, because if you get tagged, you go to the jail for 2 mins. The tagger needs to tap the jail (a bench or something) and then they can resume play. No puppy-guarding the flag. Add in extra rules as you see fit, please watch the video for more specifics, I don’t have the time or the will to type everything out… just want to say thanks to Paul Grosskopf, a friend and colleague for setting all these things up for us this year (rules, mods, locations, flags, etc).

How to play

  1. Find a large outdoor area and split it in half. Two teams, one flag each.
  2. Give teams about a minute to hide their flag on their own half (no peeking). No “puppy-guarding” the flag.
  3. On GO, teams try to steal the other flag and carry it back across the middle line.
  4. If you are tagged on the opponent’s half, go to jail for 2 minutes. A teammate can free you by tagging the jail object after a tagger taps it — follow the video for the exact jail sequence used at their school.
  5. Add house rules as needed (boundary, flag-hide limits). Watch the official video before the first play.

Game 65

Ultimate Warriors

Grades: K-8  ·  Equipment: Foam balls, cones

Ultimate Warriors is another awesome game that gets students moving, having fun, and working on a variety of skills! Split the gym into 3 sections with cones and lines. Tell the players that there will be 3 games of dodgeball going on at the same time (each 3rd correlates with a group: farmers -} knights -} warriors). If a player is hit with a ball, the player moves down and the thrower moves up. The goal is to get to the top league (the warriors). If you’re in the top league (the warriors) and you hit someone you don’t move up and if you’re in the bottom league (the farmers) you don’t move down. Set a time limit to the game. The winners are the players who finish in the top league at the end of the round. Use your own dodgeball rules and as always HAVE FUN!!! (Thanks to Joe Defreitas)

How to play

  1. Cone the gym into 3 zones: Farmers (bottom), Knights (middle), Warriors (top).
  2. Three mini-dodgeball games run at once, one in each zone, with foam balls.
  3. If you HIT someone, you move UP a league. If you GET hit, you move DOWN a league.
  4. Farmers cannot go lower. Warriors cannot go higher. Use your regular PE dodgeball hit rules (below shoulders, foam only).
  5. Play a time cap. Winners are whoever finishes the round as Warriors.

Game 66

Home Alone

Grades: K-4  ·  Equipment: Hula hoops, cones

A great game to help with visual awareness, strategic play, attacking, and defense. Try out HOME ALONE. Basically goes like this: lay out 8 hoops in a playing area and choose 1 player to stand in each hoop. Give the players in the hoop a cone (or item of your choice) – the cone/item represents the key to their house. THEY MUST PROTECT THE KEY!! Everyone else who doesn’t have a key is a theif and they will try to take the key without getting tagged by the player in the hoop. If tagged then they try stealing from someone else, however, if successful, then they swap with the player in the hoop. Give it a go!!! (Thanks to Joe Defreitas)

How to play

  1. Lay out about 8 hoops. One player stands in each hoop with a cone — that cone is the KEY to the house. They must protect it.
  2. Everyone else is a thief and tries to steal a key without being tagged by the player in that hoop.
  3. If tagged, try a different house. If the steal succeeds, thief and homeowner swap roles.
  4. House players may tag only while keeping one foot in (or next to) their hoop.
  5. Play short rounds so many students get to be homeowners.

Game 67

Bench Ball

Grades: 3-8  ·  Equipment: 2 benches, dodgeballs

Bench ball is arguably one of the best low organized throwing and catching games. The goal is to be the first team to get your players onto the bench. But how do you get on the bench? Your ball must be caught by your teammate(s) who are already on the bench. Every catch adds another player to the bench. Teams can guard and block as well to increase the challenge. Lots of fun to be had by all, and lots of skill work and development as well. Set-up the benches in the gym – 1 on each side. Create 2 teams. 1 player from each team starts on the opposite bench. Add in the dodgeballs. Teams try to be the first team to get their players on the bench (for example first to have 6 on the bench). Players get to go to the bench when their ball is caught by their teammate on the bench. Players can block to make it harder for the catchers. Play many, many rounds and get lots of throwing and catching reps. As well as HAVING FUN!

How to play

  1. Two teams. A bench at each far end. Start with 1–2 teammates already on your bench.
  2. Stay on your half. Throw a foam ball so a teammate ON THE BENCH catches it.
  3. Every legal catch lets one new teammate climb onto the bench.
  4. Defense may intercept or block throws. First team to get all (or a target number) of its players on the bench wins.
  5. This is the catch-onto-the-bench game — not Bench Dodgeball.
